## Decision register and source baseline

### Decisions

Each **Decided** choice gives the date it was taken; choices without a date were taken on 15 October 2026. **Provisional** values are in use but not yet confirmed, and **Open** items still block an interoperable release.

D1 **Asset identifier and state shape.** Decided: each Original is a `did:og`, `did:og:<SCID>`, whose log is its asset log, a VH-Log specialisation (amended 21 November 2026, D10; earlier drafts used `did:vh:<SCID>` under a proposed did:vh extension); there is no separate DID log and no `did:cel`. Each entry’s state is the full asset state, and the DID document is derived, with a `#files` service built from the resource descriptor. The Original gains a did:webvh alias, its own small log bound in both directions, when published on the web, and a did:btco alias when anchored (section 3, P3.6–P3.9). No Originals were issued under earlier formats except the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, written under the previous inscription encoding; it is not converted and no longer verifies (P14 intro). Provisional (21 October 2026, recording the SDK’s choices): the method value, `og:1.0-draft.1` (P14.1), the asset state schema and the derived DID document (section 14). Open: confirming them, exact-checkpoint resolution and deactivation history.

D2 **Author key binding and key policy.** The baseline is one author per Original, for its whole life, whose identity-bound key signs each author entry directly, with no dedicated per-Original keypair; editors sign editor entries with their own keys. Pre-rotation is on the DID side, not the asset log. The author DID is the asset log’s controller [S25]: author entries are signed by any assertionMethod key of the author DID that is not an updateKey of its own log, and rotation happens only in the author DID (section 5, P5.3). Decided (19 October 2026): there is no separate Originals declaration in the author DID; the author’s key SHOULD be used only for Originals entries, which verifiers cannot check (P5.11). Decided (21 October 2026): the author grants editors, by DID, permission to change the name, the metadata or the resource; editors sign their own entries without the author’s countersignature; only author entries change grants, aliases, lifecycle or parameters, anchor or deactivate; grants and revocations take effect from the next entry (P5.15–P5.18). Editors rely on the editors from a controller of VH-Log pull request 11 [S25]; pull request 6 [S28] draws the same boundary for logs with updateKeys. Decided (21 October 2026): key custody is out of scope for this profile; applications choose how author and editor keys are stored, and P5.11’s signer rules apply whatever the custody. Decided (1 November 2026): with one resource per Original, a grant allows or refuses new versions of it (P5.15). Amended (19 November 2026; D8), then restored (21 November 2026; D13): signer sets, parent Originals and scoped grants are experimental additions, so `did:og` 1.0 keeps one author per Original.

D3 **Historical identity and ordering.** Decided (19 October 2026): asset logs do not use VH witnesses, hosted or not (P9.8, P9.9). Off Bitcoin, an Original has claimed ordering only (P7.6); Bitcoin orders everything from the anchoring entry on (P9.10, P9.13), and the first valid anchor wins (P9.16). Witnesses were dropped because, once activated, they could not be removed and every later entry needed their threshold, so losing a threshold would freeze every Original using them, and Originals witnesses would be stateful services; anchoring already orders entries on the anchoring sat. It does not establish uniqueness: a competing anchor on another sat can exist undiscovered, so uniqueness is reported as not established and ownership as provisional (P9.16). Author DIDs keep whatever their own method offers, including did:webvh witnesses. Also decided: author DIDs may use any method with verifiable version history that the profile lists, and the baseline lists did:vh, did:webvh and did:btco, whose versions are inscriptions ordered by chain order, and an inscribed entry may name only a did:btco version that precedes its inscription (P5.8, P5.13); an inscribed entry is checked against the author DID as it stood at its inscription time, so later rotation or author DID deactivation does not make an anchored Original non-current (P9.15). Superseded: witness selection, independence, the witness declaration and the 60-second witness freshness bound; the Originals declaration was dropped too (D2). Decided (21 October 2026): a did:webvh signer that moves keeps its identity only through moves its own verified log records, never through `alsoKnownAs` or redirects (P5.14). Decided (21 October 2026): off Bitcoin there is no freshness policy that makes an Original current; an unanchored Original is reported only as valid as of its verified checkpoint, and only an anchored Original can be current (P7.10). Decided (21 October 2026): for an anchored Original, a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s DID is as fresh as the verifier knows about. It uses the newest version it has obtained or retained, states it, and need not prove that no newer one exists (P7.8, P9.15); the cost is that a host withholding a key removal can get later entries accepted by readers who do not know of it. Decided (21 October 2026): anchoring does not require did:btco DIDs for the author or editors; it stays a SHOULD for authors who want Bitcoin to be all a reader needs (P9.18). Decided (23 October 2026): a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s freshness is established only by that DID’s own witnesses or watchers; a log merely obtained from a location is reported with where and when it was obtained, and readers are told plainly that a newer version may exist (P7.8). A moved identity’s log is first sought where the latest verified entry, or failing that the identity’s earliest entry, points, and an unverified entry can direct a request but never decides what is accepted (P5.14). Open: how identity versions are packaged.

D4 **Entry state and package.** Decided: resource digests are SHA-256 multihashes, the only algorithm in the baseline set (P4.2); canonicalization is whatever the pinned VH Log version uses, and proofs use `eddsa-jcs-2022` or `bip340-jcs-2025`, permitted under VH-Log’s proposed rule for additional cryptosuites [S21] (P5.12). Decided (21 October 2026): the limits in P14.13; there is no resource count limit, since an Original has one resource (1 November 2026). Decided (23 October 2026): a reader rejects a carriage return, non-compact JSON or a missing final line feed (P14.2); the 2<sup>53</sup> − 1 magnitude bound applies to every number however it is written (P14.3); `name` is measured in UTF-16 code units (P14.5); a proof has exactly the five members P14.8 lists; and writers bind ZIP order, date, flags and mode (P14.10). Provisional: entry state fields, file names, digest encoding, and the evidence package format and its completeness declaration (section 14). Proposed (9 November 2026): carried logs, a file that keeps its media type and carries the asset log; their carriers, media types and JSON member name are provisional (P7.11–P7.16, P14.14–P14.17). Decided after an experimental SDK build (11–12 November 2026): the 65,536-byte entry limit is measured over the entry’s JCS form in UTF-8 in every format (P14.13); descriptor media types carry no parameters, and one with a parameter is rejected (P14.6); a carried file whose remaining bytes do not match fails overall, with an explicit reason (P7.12); importing a carried file is not an opt-in to resolve (P7.13); a checkpoint from before the anchoring entry may be carried as a historical checkpoint (P7.14, P7.15); a reader detects the carrier, treating a file extension as a hint only (P14.14); a JSON member is written last and found anywhere in the root object, duplicate root names are rejected, and the root is nesting level 1 (P14.17); a malformed marker structure is no carrier, and the writer’s refusal is a parse test (P14.16). Decided on the questions that build raised (13 November 2026): an opt-in to resolve a carried log’s signer DIDs applies to a single read, there is no standing opt-in, and importing never counts as one (P7.13); a writer places a text block only where the host format’s parser reads it as a comment, one generic rule, with per-type refusals given only as non-normative examples (P14.16). Open: confirming those provisional values, the removal of a JSON member that is not last, a text block’s rules for mixed line ends and trailing whitespace, and test vectors. Publish real test vectors only after those choices.

D5 **Operational edge cases.** Decided: an update that leaves a digest unchanged does not advance that resource’s version (P4.4); an explicit null removes metadata, and the name cannot be null (P4.6); only Bitcoin settles a fork: until an Original is anchored, divergent valid branches are an unresolved conflict and none is current, whatever the timestamps (19 October 2026; P6.6, P9.16). Decided (21 October 2026): a shared host accepts one append at a time by comparing the submitted parent with its head atomically, refuses others with a stale-parent conflict, and never changes a signed submission; the refused change is kept and reconciled into a new signed candidate (P6.7, P6.8); off-chain forks stay unresolved, and only an author’s anchor settles one (P6.6). Provisional: millisecond versionTime precision, which P14.2 uses under [S22]. Open: atomic local commit details, and confirming that precision.

D6 **Transport profiles.** Decided for the Bitcoin representation (20 October 2026; P9.5, P9.13, P9.17): inscriptions on the anchoring sat carry complete entries, with no compact delta; the anchoring inscription carries the whole log from genesis through the anchoring entry, and later inscriptions one entry each, so Bitcoin alone verifies the log (P9.17); resource bytes are not needed for the log to verify, and only did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories still come from elsewhere (P9.18); an inscription counts at one confirmation, every report states its depth, and a reorganization that removes an accepted inscription is reported; the Bitcoin `src` kind is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias; did:btco author DIDs follow the did:btco method v0.2.0 [S26]. A verifier compares the sat number and network of a did:btco DID with where its index reports each inscription, and does not accept placement that two indexes it uses disagree on (P9.10). Decided (1 November 2026): an inscription’s metadata carries its entries, as a JSON array in CBOR, and its body is the exact bytes of the resource version its last entry names, under that version’s media type, so the sat shows the Original’s current version; the 350,000-byte limit covers metadata and body together; the JSON-to-CBOR mapping, ord’s for `--json-metadata`, is provisional (P9.17, P14.12). Decided (18 November 2026): by default an inscription’s body is a preview of the resource version its last entry names, at most 8,192 bytes, which that entry signs by naming its media type, size and digest beside the version; for an image version the writer generates it as WebP, for any other media type the author may supply one, and with none the inscription has no body; a writer may instead inscribe the full version within the 350,000-byte limit; previews are named only from the anchoring entry on, an entry adding a version names a new preview or none, and any other entry keeps the last or names none; a body that is not the named preview, or, with none named, not the version and not absent, makes the inscription invalid; and the SDK tells the author, before inscribing, whether the preview or the full file is inscribed and what it costs (P9.17, P9.18, P9.30, P14.5, P14.12, P14.13). Creators pay for anchoring, and only Bitcoin is permanent: at 1 sat/vB an inscription with a preview of about 8 kB costs about 2,500 sats in fees, about US$1.50 in November 2026, whatever the size of the resource. The `preview` member’s name and shape are provisional with the other entry state fields (D4). Decided (21 October 2026): one confirmation on every network, the 350,000-byte inscription limit, the did:btco network prefixes `sig:`, `test:` and `reg:`, which this profile uses as the SDK does. Decided (23 October 2026): an inscription after the anchor whose signer version cannot be verified is reported as unverifiable and does not stop the accepted state being current, and one naming a version the verifier holds under another versionId is rejected (P9.15); a later publication from the same head waits while an earlier inscription extending it is unverifiable, so chain order decides, accepting that such an entry can leave the Original waiting permanently unless the named signer updates its DID through the version number it names, and rejecting an entry naming a version above the P14.13 limit or after a deactivated version (P9.15); a submitted anchoring inscription the network is not observed to hold is labelled submitted, not observed, never pending (P9.18); whether a did:btco inscription is a version depends only on its own metadata (P5.13). Upstream ask: the did:btco method defines no network prefixes, proposed in [S30]. Decided (13 November 2026): a writer checks its committed, unpublished log as a local copy, verified whole, reported unverified on Bitcoin and never current, and valid only for its own check before inscribing, never in a report given to anyone else (P9.18). Withdrawn (18 November 2026): anchoring on an EVM chain. Under it, an Original whose genesis entry named a listed EVM chain, by the end Base alone, was to be anchored on one token in a contract with no administrator, with a did:evm alias, a trust class for each chain, finality and provider rules, and move entries up through the anchor layers to Bitcoin, recorded in an `anchor` member of the asset state (P3.9, P7.14–P7.16, P9.19–P9.29, P14.5, P14.18–P14.22). Its only purpose was a lower anchoring cost, which previews in Bitcoin inscriptions provide with far less protocol complexity, and only Bitcoin is permanent. Bitcoin is the only anchor. Rule numbers P9.19–P9.29 and P14.18–P14.22 are not used, and are not reused; what stays from that work is general: a resolvable DID at every layer (P3.9), carried logs without their EVM readings (P7.11–P7.16, P14.14–P14.17), the entry limit measured over the JCS form in every format (P14.13) and the writer’s local-copy check (P9.18). An archive of the SDK build of EVM anchoring is kept at the tag `archive/evm-anchoring-experimental` of onionoriginals/sdk. Open: hosted discovery and relocation, and any compact encoding (P9.14).

D7 **Later capabilities.** Decided: authorship never transfers. An Original anchors once, terminally, to one sat, from the local or the web layer, by an update entry that adds the sat’s did:btco alias (P9.10), and after anchoring no entry can add or replace a did:webvh alias (20 October 2026; P9.10). Layers move one way: local to web, and either to Bitcoin (P9.10); an anchored Original needs no web alias for discovery or verification, because its did:btco alias names a sat that carries its complete log (P9.17). The alias it had at anchoring, if any, may stay; the sat’s holder is the provisional owner (P9.16), and transferring an Original means moving the sat, with the evidence that stays off Bitcoin handed over separately (P9.18). On Bitcoin a change needs an authorized signature, by the author or a granted editor, and an inscription on the sat, so a sale freezes the Original unless a signer and the owner cooperate (P9.10–P9.13). The first valid anchor in chain order wins, and a verifier reports its uniqueness as not established and ownership as provisional (P9.16). Decided (1 November 2026): an Original is exactly one resource, which can have many versions (P4.1). Open: whether to add encryption or recovery. The anchoring sat carries the complete log and the preview, or the full bytes, of each version inscribed on it; versions not inscribed in full and did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories are carried off Bitcoin, in evidence packages and on hosts (P9.17, P9.18). Apart from the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, no Originals were issued under earlier formats, and no migration or legacy verification is provided for it.

D8 **Signer sets, parent authority and scoped grants.** Moved (21 November 2026; D13) to the experimental additions document, with its rules (P5.19–P5.30, P14.23) and its members in section 14. Decided on 19 November 2026 as an additive change to `originals:1.0`; it is not part of `did:og` 1.0.

D9 **Read authorization.** Moved (21 November 2026; D13) to the experimental additions document, with its rules (P8.6–P8.14, P14.24) and the `readers` member. Decided on 19 November 2026 as part of this specification; it is not part of `did:og` 1.0. In the core, any host may serve any Original it holds, and privacy means keeping evidence off public hosts (section 8).
