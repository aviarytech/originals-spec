## Decision register and source baseline

### Decisions

Each **Decided** choice gives the date it was taken; choices without a date were taken on 15 October 2026. **Provisional** values are in use but not yet confirmed, and **Open** items still block an interoperable release.

D1 **Asset identifier and state shape.** Decided: each Original is a `did:og`, `did:og:<SCID>`, whose log is its asset log, in a log format `did:og` defines itself (amended 21 November 2026, D10; earlier drafts used `did:vh:<SCID>` under a proposed did:vh extension); there is no separate DID log and no `did:cel`. Each entry’s state is the full asset state, and the DID document is derived, with a `#files` service built from the resource descriptor. The Original gains a did:webvh alias, its own small log bound in both directions, when published on the web, and a did:btco alias when anchored (section 3, P3.6–P3.9). No Originals were issued under earlier formats except the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, written under the previous inscription encoding; it is not converted, and a verifier of this draft reports it `unsupported` (P14.1, T14-085); its inscriptions are kept as a prior-format fixture under `vectors/prior-format/`. Provisional (21 October 2026, recording the SDK’s choices): the method value, `og:1.0-draft.1` (P14.1), the asset state schema and the derived DID document (section 14). Open: confirming them, exact-checkpoint resolution and deactivation history.

D2 **Author key binding and key policy.** The baseline is one author per Original, for its whole life, whose identity-bound key signs each author entry directly, with no dedicated per-Original keypair; editors sign editor entries with their own keys. Pre-rotation is on the DID side, not the asset log. The author DID is the asset log’s controller (P14.1): author entries are signed by any assertionMethod key of the author DID that is not an updateKey of its own log, and rotation happens only in the author DID (section 5, P5.3). Decided (19 October 2026): there is no separate Originals declaration in the author DID; the author’s key SHOULD be used only for Originals entries, which verifiers cannot check (P5.11). Decided (21 October 2026): the author grants editors, by DID, permission to change the name, the metadata or the resource; editors sign their own entries without the author’s countersignature; only author entries change grants, aliases, lifecycle or parameters, anchor or deactivate; grants and revocations take effect from the next entry (P5.15–P5.18). `did:og` defines editor entries itself (P5.15–P5.18); VH-Log pull requests 11 [S25] and 6 [S28] proposed the same boundary upstream, and are background only (D10). Decided (21 October 2026): key custody is out of scope for this profile; applications choose how author and editor keys are stored, and P5.11’s signer rules apply whatever the custody. Decided (1 November 2026): with one resource per Original, a grant allows or refuses new versions of it (P5.15). Amended (19 November 2026; D8), then restored (21 November 2026; D13): signer sets, parent Originals and scoped grants are experimental additions, so `did:og` 1.0 keeps one author per Original.

D3 **Historical identity and ordering.** Decided (19 October 2026): asset logs do not use VH witnesses, hosted or not (P9.8, P9.9). Off Bitcoin, an Original has claimed ordering only (P7.6); Bitcoin orders everything from the anchoring entry on (P9.10, P9.13), and the first valid anchor wins (P9.16). Witnesses were dropped because, once activated, they could not be removed and every later entry needed their threshold, so losing a threshold would freeze every Original using them, and Originals witnesses would be stateful services; anchoring already orders entries on the anchoring sat. It does not establish uniqueness: a competing anchor on another sat can exist undiscovered, so uniqueness is reported as not established and the holder as provisional (P9.16). Author DIDs keep whatever their own method offers, including did:webvh witnesses. Also decided: author DIDs may use any method with verifiable version history that the profile lists, and the baseline lists did:vh, did:webvh and did:btco, whose versions are inscriptions ordered by chain order, and an inscribed entry may name only a did:btco version that precedes its inscription (P5.8, P5.13); an inscribed entry is checked against the author DID as it stood at its inscription time, so later rotation or author DID deactivation does not make an anchored Original non-current (P9.15). Superseded: witness selection, independence, the witness declaration and the 60-second witness freshness bound; the Originals declaration was dropped too (D2). Decided (21 October 2026): a did:webvh signer that moves keeps its identity only through moves its own verified log records, never through `alsoKnownAs` or redirects (P5.14). Decided (21 October 2026): off Bitcoin there is no freshness policy that makes an Original current; an unanchored Original is reported only as valid as of its verified checkpoint, and only an anchored Original can be current (P7.10). Decided (21 October 2026): for an anchored Original, a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s DID is as fresh as the verifier knows about. It uses the newest version it has obtained or retained, states it, and need not prove that no newer one exists (P7.8, P9.15); the cost is that a host withholding a key removal can get later entries accepted by readers who do not know of it. Decided (21 October 2026): anchoring does not require did:btco DIDs for the author or editors; it stays a SHOULD for authors who want Bitcoin to be all a reader needs (P9.18). Decided (23 October 2026): a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s freshness is established only by that DID’s own witnesses or watchers; a log merely obtained from a location is reported with where and when it was obtained, and readers are told plainly that a newer version may exist (P7.8). A moved identity’s log is first sought where the latest verified entry, or failing that the identity’s earliest entry, points, and an unverified entry can direct a request but never decides what is accepted (P5.14). Superseded for inscribed entries (21 November 2026, D12): every entry inscribed on an anchoring sat is checked only against signer versions that Bitcoin evidences before its inscription, from a did:btco signer’s own sat or from a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s history inscribed on the anchoring sat, never by versionTime or against versions known only from elsewhere (P9.15, P9.33). This replaces, for those entries, the inscription-time check, the freshness-as-known rule of 21 October 2026 and the retroactive revocation conflict, and anchoring now requires that evidence where it only recommended did:btco signers (P9.18). The freshness rules above still apply to entries that are not inscribed (P7.8). Amended (22 November 2026, D22): signer histories are no longer inscribed; a did:vh or did:webvh signer of an inscribed entry is obtained by resolving its DID, and the entry is checked at the version it names only, so the Bitcoin-evidenced check applies to did:btco signers alone (P9.15, P9.33). An author or editor DID that is a did:vh follows the standalone did:vh method [S3] (D10). Open: how identity versions are bundled.

D4 **Entry state and bundle.** Decided: resource digests are SHA-256 multihashes, the only algorithm in the baseline set (P4.2); canonicalization is JCS (P14.3), the log format is the one `did:og` defines, byte-compatible with VH Log at [S2] and did:vh at [S3] (P14.27–P14.29; amended 21 November 2026, D10), and proofs use `eddsa-jcs-2022` with Ed25519 keys only (decided 21 November 2026, replacing `eddsa-jcs-2022` or `bip340-jcs-2025`; P5.12, P14.8). Decided (21 October 2026): the limits in P14.13; there is no resource count limit, since an Original has one resource (1 November 2026). Decided (23 October 2026): a reader rejects a carriage return, non-compact JSON or a missing final line feed (P14.2); the 2<sup>53</sup> − 1 magnitude bound applies to every number however it is written (P14.3); `name` is measured in UTF-16 code units (P14.5); a proof has exactly the five members P14.8 lists; and writers bind ZIP order, date, flags and mode (P14.10). Provisional: entry state fields, file names, digest encoding, and the bundle format and its completeness declaration (section 14). Proposed (9 November 2026): carried logs, a file that keeps its media type and carries the asset log; their carriers, media types and JSON member name are provisional (P7.11–P7.16, P14.14–P14.17). Decided after an experimental SDK build (11–12 November 2026): the 65,536-byte entry limit is measured over the entry’s JCS form in UTF-8 in every format (P14.13); descriptor media types carry no parameters, and one with a parameter is rejected (P14.6); a carried file whose remaining bytes do not match fails overall, with an explicit reason (P7.12); importing a carried file is not an opt-in to resolve (P7.13); a checkpoint from before the anchoring entry may be carried as a historical checkpoint (P7.14, P7.15); a reader detects the carrier, treating a file extension as a hint only (P14.14); a JSON member is written last and found anywhere in the root object, duplicate root names are rejected, and the root is nesting level 1 (P14.17); a malformed marker structure is no carrier, and the writer’s refusal is a parse test (P14.16). Decided on the questions that build raised (13 November 2026): an opt-in to resolve a carried log’s signer DIDs applies to a single read, there is no standing opt-in, and importing never counts as one (P7.13); a writer places a text block only where the host format’s parser reads it as a comment, one generic rule, with per-type refusals given only as non-normative examples (P14.16). Open: confirming those provisional values, the removal of a JSON member that is not last, a text block’s rules for mixed line ends and trailing whitespace, and the test vectors. Provisional test vectors for `og:1.0-draft.1` are published under `vectors/` (21 November 2026; P14.26); they are regenerated, with a new draft number, whenever a provisional choice they depend on changes.

D5 **Operational edge cases.** Decided: an update that leaves a digest unchanged does not advance that resource’s version (P4.4), except that a change of mediaType alone is a new version (21 November 2026; P4.4, P14.6); an explicit null removes metadata, and the name cannot be null (P4.6); only Bitcoin settles a fork: until an Original is anchored, divergent valid branches are an unresolved conflict and none is current, whatever the timestamps (19 October 2026; P6.6, P9.16). Amended (21 November 2026; D17): an author entry that supersedes the other branches settles off-chain a fork whose other branches hold only editor entries (P6.9); a fork with an author entry on more than one branch still needs Bitcoin. Decided (21 October 2026): a shared host accepts one append at a time by comparing the submitted parent with its head atomically, refuses others with a stale-parent conflict, and never changes a signed submission; the refused change is kept and reconciled into a new signed candidate (P6.7, P6.8); off-chain forks stay unresolved, and only an author’s anchor settles one (P6.6), except an editor-only fork that an author entry supersedes (P6.9; D17). Provisional: millisecond versionTime precision, which P14.2 defines. Open: atomic local commit details, and confirming that precision.

D6 **Transport profiles.** Decided for the Bitcoin representation (20 October 2026; P9.5, P9.13, P9.17): inscriptions on the anchoring sat carry complete entries, with no compact delta; the anchoring inscription carries the whole log from genesis through the anchoring entry, and later inscriptions one entry each, so Bitcoin alone verifies the log (P9.17); resource bytes are not needed for the log to verify, and only did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories still come from elsewhere (P9.18); an inscription counts at one confirmation, every report states its depth, and a reorganization that removes an accepted inscription is reported; the Bitcoin `src` kind is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias; did:btco author DIDs follow the did:btco method v0.2.0 [S26]. A verifier compares the sat number and network of a did:btco DID with where its index reports each inscription, and does not accept placement that two indexes it uses disagree on (P9.10). Decided (1 November 2026): an inscription’s metadata carries its entries, as a JSON array in CBOR, and its body is the exact bytes of the resource version its last entry names, under that version’s media type, so the sat shows the Original’s current version (amended 18 November 2026 to a preview by default, below, and 21 November 2026 to a signed body mode, D19); the 350,000-byte limit covers metadata and body together; the JSON-to-CBOR mapping, ord’s for `--json-metadata`, is provisional (P9.17, P14.12). Decided (18 November 2026; amended 21 November 2026 by D19, so that the entry’s signed `body` member, not the writer, chooses between the preview, the full version and no body): by default an inscription’s body is a preview of the resource version its last entry names, at most 8,192 bytes, which that entry signs by naming its media type, size and digest beside the version; for an image version the writer generates it as WebP, for any other media type the author may supply one, and with none the inscription has no body; a writer may instead inscribe the full version within the 350,000-byte limit (superseded by D19: only if the entry signs `body` as `full`); previews are named only from the anchoring entry on, an entry adding a version names a new preview or none, and any other entry keeps the last or names none; a body that is not the named preview, or, with none named, not the version and not absent, makes the inscription invalid; and the SDK tells the author, before inscribing, whether the preview or the full file is inscribed and what it costs (P9.17, P9.18, P9.30, P14.5, P14.12, P14.13). Creators pay for anchoring, and only Bitcoin is permanent: at 1 sat/vB an inscription with a preview of about 8 kB costs about 2,500 sats in fees, about US$1.50 in November 2026, whatever the size of the resource. The `preview` member’s name and shape are provisional with the other entry state fields (D4). Decided (21 October 2026): one confirmation on every network, the 350,000-byte inscription limit, the did:btco network prefixes `sig:`, `test:` and `reg:`, which this profile uses as the SDK does. Decided (23 October 2026): an inscription after the anchor whose signer version cannot be verified is reported as unverifiable and does not stop the accepted state being current, and one naming a version the verifier holds under another versionId is rejected (P9.15); a later publication from the same head waits while an earlier inscription extending it is unverifiable, so chain order decides, accepting that such an entry can leave the Original waiting permanently unless the named signer updates its DID through the version number it names, and rejecting an entry naming a version above the P14.13 limit or after a deactivated version (P9.15); a submitted anchoring inscription the network is not observed to hold is labelled submitted, not observed, never pending (P9.18); whether a did:btco inscription is a version depends only on its own metadata (P5.13). Upstream ask: the did:btco method defines no network prefixes, proposed in [S30]. Decided (13 November 2026): a writer checks its committed, unpublished log as a local copy, verified whole, reported unverified on Bitcoin and never current, and valid only for its own check before inscribing, never in a report given to anyone else (P9.18). Withdrawn (18 November 2026): anchoring on an EVM chain. Under it, an Original whose genesis entry named a listed EVM chain, by the end Base alone, was to be anchored on one token in a contract with no administrator, with a did:evm alias, a trust class for each chain, finality and provider rules, and move entries up through the anchor layers to Bitcoin, recorded in an `anchor` member of the asset state (P3.9, P7.14–P7.16, P9.19–P9.29, P14.5, P14.18–P14.22). Its only purpose was a lower anchoring cost, which previews in Bitcoin inscriptions provide with far less protocol complexity, and only Bitcoin is permanent. Bitcoin is the only anchor. Rule numbers P9.19–P9.29 and P14.18–P14.22 are not used, and are not reused; what stays from that work is general: a resolvable DID at every layer (P3.9), carried logs without their EVM readings (P7.11–P7.16, P14.14–P14.17), the entry limit measured over the JCS form in every format (P14.13) and the writer’s local-copy check (P9.18). An archive of the SDK build of EVM anchoring is kept at the tag `archive/evm-anchoring-experimental` of onionoriginals/sdk. Superseded (21 November 2026): signer DID histories no longer come from elsewhere for an anchored Original, and an inscription naming a did:vh or did:webvh version not inscribed on the sat is rejected, not left waiting (D12; P9.15, P9.33), itself amended (22 November 2026, D22): did:vh and did:webvh signer histories again come from resolving the DID, a missing log or version leaves an inscription unverifiable, and one held under another versionId is rejected (P9.15, P9.33), itself amended the same day (D22): a versionId that no verifying log contains is missing, never rejected; a publication may span up to 16 consecutive inscriptions that take effect together (D16; P9.32); the genesis entry names the network and a sat holds at most one Original (D15; P9.34); every Originals inscription carries the metaprotocol `og` and Bitcoin is read as ord 0.29.x reads it, at a stated tip (D18; P9.31); each entry signs its body mode, so the inscriber no longer chooses between the full version and no body (D19; P9.17); and chain order within a block orders publications, replacing the strictly greater block height (P9.13). Open: hosted discovery and relocation, and any compact encoding (P9.14).

D7 **Later capabilities.** Decided: authorship never transfers. An Original anchors once, terminally, to one sat, from the local or the web layer, by an update entry that adds the sat’s did:btco alias (P9.10), and after anchoring no entry can add or replace a did:webvh alias (20 October 2026; P9.10). Layers move one way: local to web, and either to Bitcoin (P9.10); an anchored Original needs no web alias for discovery or verification, because its did:btco alias names a sat that carries its complete log (P9.17). The alias it had at anchoring, if any, may stay; the sat’s holder is the Original’s provisional holder (P9.16), and transferring an Original means moving the sat, with the evidence that stays off Bitcoin handed over separately (P9.18). On Bitcoin a change needs an authorized signature, by the author or a granted editor, and an inscription on the sat, so a sale freezes the Original unless a signer and the holder cooperate (P9.10–P9.13). The first valid anchor in chain order wins, and a verifier reports its uniqueness as not established and the holder as provisional, unless an index of Originals inscriptions it trusts shows no competing anchor through a given block (P9.16; D18). Decided (1 November 2026): an Original is exactly one resource, which can have many versions (P4.1). Open: whether to add encryption or recovery. Later addition (21 November 2026; D19): a hash-only Bitcoin timestamp, proving that a log existed by a given block without anchoring or disclosing it. Later addition (21 November 2026): signing with other cryptosuites, such as BIP-340 Schnorr signatures over secp256k1 keys, which many did:btco and Bitcoin-wallet keys use (P5.12). The anchoring sat carries the complete log and the preview, or the full bytes, of each version inscribed on it; versions not inscribed in full are carried off Bitcoin, in bundles and on hosts, and signer DID histories are not inscribed: did:btco signers' are on their own sats, and did:vh or did:webvh signers' logs are resolved from their DIDs (P9.17, P9.18, P9.33; D22). Apart from the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, no Originals were issued under earlier formats, and no migration or legacy verification is provided for it.

D8 **Signer sets, parent authority and scoped grants.** Moved (21 November 2026; D13) to the experimental additions document, with its rules (P5.19–P5.30, P14.23) and its members in section 14. Decided on 19 November 2026 as an additive change to `originals:1.0`; it is not part of `did:og` 1.0.

D9 **Read authorization.** Moved (21 November 2026; D13) to the experimental additions document, with its rules (P8.6–P8.14, P14.24) and the `readers` member. Decided on 19 November 2026 as part of this specification; it is not part of `did:og` 1.0. In the core, any host may serve any Original it holds, and privacy means keeping evidence off public hosts (section 8).

D10 **Identifier basis.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): an Original is a `did:og`, `did:og:<SCID>`, its own DID method (P3.1, P14.1). Originals no longer uses the did:vh name, because the did:vh published by the Decentralized Identity Foundation is a different method that claims the same identifier form and rejects an Originals log. The did:vh extension mechanism of the earlier pre-draft [S19] is withdrawn as a dependency. An author or editor DID MAY still be a did:vh, and is then verified under that standalone method [S3] (P5.8, P5.14). Amended (21 November 2026, on review of the design-review pull request): VH Log is not being standardized at present, so `did:og` defines its whole log format itself, as did:vh does: the entry members, versionId, versionTime, parameters, SCID derivation, entry hashes and proofs (P14.1–P14.3, P14.8, P14.27–P14.29). It keeps the entry shape and the SCID and entry-hash algorithms of VH Log at [S2] and did:vh at [S3], byte for byte, so that existing libraries parse its logs and the vectors stay valid, but it depends on neither: they are lineage, and the open VH-Log pull requests are background, listed under “Upstream background”. The genesis entry carries `updateKeys` `[]`, the common entry shape’s parameter, which authorizes nothing in `did:og`: authority comes from the author DID and editor grants alone (P5.3, P5.16, P14.1). An author entry may carry `updateKeys`, an editor entry may not, and in this version only `[]` is valid; a non-empty value is reserved for a later method version, and restricting which of the author DID’s keys may sign is a later capability (D7).

D11 **What authorship means.** Deferred (21 November 2026) with signer sets to the experimental additions document (D13). In the core, an Original has one author for its whole life and authorship never transfers (P5.8, D7). Whether a single-author Original may become a set, and whether the genesis author can be removed from one, is open there.

D12 **Signer identity on Bitcoin.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): every entry inscribed on the anchoring sat is judged only against signer DID versions that Bitcoin evidences before its inscription, from a did:btco signer’s own sat (P5.13) or from a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s DID log entries inscribed on the anchoring sat (P9.33). An entry naming a version that is not so evidenced is rejected (P9.15, P9.35). Anchoring therefore requires that evidence, where it previously only recommended did:btco signers (P9.8, P9.18). This makes a backdated key removal, or a signer version published after a sale, unable to change the anchored state, at the cost that, for a did:vh or did:webvh signer, a key removal binds only once it is inscribed (section 11). Amended (22 November 2026; D22): signer histories are no longer inscribed; a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s log is resolved, and each inscribed entry it signed is checked at the version it names only, so its key removals never bind inscribed entries; the judgement against Bitcoin-evidenced state now applies to did:btco signers only (P9.15, P9.33, P9.35).

D13 **What goes in `did:og` 1.0.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): `did:og` 1.0 is a self-contained core of one author, editors, the local, hosted and Bitcoin layers, and evidence. Signer sets, parent Originals, scoped grants, objects named from a resource and read authorization (D8, D9; P5.19–P5.30, P8.6–P8.14, P14.23, P14.24) move to a separately versioned experimental additions document, with their conformance rows and the core text they had amended. Their rule numbers are not reused in the core. The review findings against them are tracked as issues on that document.

D14 **Cross-Original disclosure.** Deferred (21 November 2026) with read authorization and parent Originals to the experimental additions document (D13). Whether a child Original, or another Original naming the same digest, may ever cause a host to disclose a private Original’s log or bytes without a decision on that Original’s side is open there. In the core, every Original a host holds is public (section 8).

D15 **Binding the sat, the SCID and the network.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): the genesis entry names the Bitcoin network in the asset state’s `network` member, fixed for life, and every chain-order comparison is within that network (P9.34, P14.5). A sat carries at most one Original: the first publication on it that is valid as an anchoring publication takes it, and later Originals inscriptions on it for another `did:og` are rejected, so the bare did:btco alias resolves to one Original (P9.34, P3.9).

D16 **Anchoring a long history.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): a publication MAY span up to 16 consecutive Originals inscriptions on the anchoring sat, which take effect together when the final one confirms (P9.32, P14.12, P14.13). Entries stay complete, with no delta encoding (D6). Inscribed signer histories counted toward the same publication until D22 removed them (22 November 2026; P9.33).

D17 **Settling forks off-chain.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): an author entry that names, in its `supersedes` member, the entries it supersedes settles off-chain a fork whose other branches hold only editor entries after the common predecessor (P6.9, P14.4). A fork in which another branch holds an author entry still needs Bitcoin to settle it (D5). Whether editors may advance a child Original’s pin of its parent is deferred with parent Originals (D13).

D18 **What the sat’s holder is.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): the specification calls whoever holds the anchoring sat its *holder*, not its owner, since holding confers no authority (P9.11, P9.12). Every Originals inscription carries the ord metaprotocol tag (tag 7) with the provisional value `og` (P9.31), so that Originals inscriptions can be enumerated. A verifier MAY report that no competing anchor for the same `did:og` exists through a given block, from an index of Originals inscriptions it trusts; otherwise the holder is reported as provisional (P9.16).

D19 **Inscription body and private timestamps.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): each entry signs its inscription body mode, `preview`, `full` or `none`, in the asset state’s `body` member, and an inscription whose body does not match is invalid, so whoever inscribes no longer decides whether a version’s bytes become public (P9.17, P9.30, P14.5). This replaces D6’s choice between the full version and no body. A hash-only Bitcoin timestamp, with no anchor and no disclosure, is a later addition (D7).

D20 **Read authorization on master.** Recorded (21 November 2026): read authorization (D9) was re-landed on master after the design review’s snapshot, and was then moved, with the other 19 November additions, to the experimental additions document by D13.

D21 **First interoperable release.** Decided (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review): the first interoperable release of `did:og` covers all layers at once, with no separate conformance profiles. Two independent implementations must reproduce the published test vectors, byte for byte and report for report (P14.26), and pass the whole matrix in section 12, as verifiers, writers and hosts. Section 10 is a non-normative build order, not a conformance target.

D22 **No signer histories on Bitcoin.** Decided (22 November 2026, owner decision), amending D12 and D16: Originals inscriptions no longer carry signer DID histories. Metadata is `{entries, part?}`; a writer MUST NOT write `signers`, and a reader ignores a `signers` member, as inscriptions written before this decision carry, such as the mainnet Original on sat 1927917275413739, and never uses it as evidence (P14.12; bounded by amendment D below). For every entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, each did:vh or did:webvh signer’s log comes from the evidence set, as for entries that are not inscribed: from a bundle, a retained copy, or resolving the DID, a did:webvh from its locations with no redirects (P5.14, P8.5), and is an evidence-set input with its source and the time obtained (P7.17), as for entries that are not inscribed; did:btco signers are still read from their own sat (P5.13). Every entry by a did:vh or did:webvh signer, before or after the anchoring entry, is checked at the version it names only (P5.3, P9.33): a removal or deactivation in its log never invalidates an inscribed entry, and a verifier may warn of it beside the result (P7.8). The state of a signer DID at an inscription, and the removal and deactivation checks of P9.15 row 8, including an editor entry’s dependency on the author DID’s deactivation (P5.18), now apply only to did:btco DIDs (P9.35). A missing resolved log is `signer-history-missing` and a log short of the named version `signer-version-missing`, handled as an unread did:btco sat: incomplete identity evidence in the anchoring publication, `unverifiable` after it, never accepting (P9.15 row 4, P9.36). Bitcoin alone therefore verifies the log, its publication and anchoring, and did:btco signers; a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s host can withhold its log or serve a truncated one, which leaves evidence incomplete, but cannot forge one (P9.18, section 11). The cost is that a key removal never binds inscribed entries for a did:vh or did:webvh signer, so an author or editor who needs one to bind uses a did:btco DID (P9.33). Inscriptions get smaller and no longer repeat history that the signer’s DID already publishes. The method value stays `og:1.0-draft.1`: the owner chose not to bump the draft number for this change, an exception to the ruling that the number increases with every change that alters validity (P14.1), and the vectors were regenerated under the same number (P14.26). Amended (22 November 2026, owner decisions on review of the D22 pull request): (A) Other sats never block. An anchoring publication of the same `did:og` on a sat other than the anchoring sat under consideration affects the anchor only if it verifies, and is then the anchor if earlier in chain order; one that cannot be decided does not stop a later valid anchoring publication on another sat from being the anchor, is ignored, with no listing or diagnostic and no effect on the default or transfer check (owner ruling, 23 November 2026), and only stops the anchor’s uniqueness from being reported as established through its block. On the same sat an earlier undecidable anchoring publication still leaves `bitcoin` `undetermined`, and an anchoring publication of another `did:og` matters only there. The Original names its own sat, and publications on other sats are on sats someone else may hold; the residual risk is in section 11 (P9.16, P7.19, P7.22). (B) Forked signer logs: the evidence set may hold several logs for one DID; each that verifies from the DID’s genesis is evidence, and one that fails anywhere is not evidence at all, with no verified prefix used. An entry naming versionId V is checked at V if any verifying log contains V, and is rejected if its proof fails there; if none does, whether the logs stop short or hold another versionId at that number, the version is missing (`signer-version-missing`), never a rejection. The result never depends on input order, and the report’s latest versionId for the DID is the highest-numbered version among the verifying logs, ties broken by code-point order of the versionId, with the source, time obtained and freshness of a log that holds it (P9.15 rows 3 and 4, P9.33, P7.8, P7.17, P14.25). This replaces the rule that a versionId held under another one is rejected. (C) A holder who inscribes an entry naming a version that never exists freezes the Original on its sat for ever; only the holder can inscribe, so this harms only the holder (section 11). (D) A reader ignores a `signers` member only in an inscription on mainnet in a block at height 977,150 or lower; the only such Originals inscription is on sat 1927917275413739. Anywhere else a `signers` member makes the metadata invalid like any other unknown member (P14.12; T14-103, T14-106). (E) Resolving a signer DID of an anchored Original needs the caller’s opt-in, as P7.13 requires for a carried log, because the author picks the host and learns who resolves; without it the evidence set holds no resolved log, and the result is `signer-history-missing` with identity incomplete (P9.33, P7.13; section 11). This replaces the rule that resolving these DIDs is part of verification and not an opt-in. (F) Decided 24 November 2026, owner: an author who withholds the log versions an earlier anchoring publication on another sat names can re-anchor the same Original for readers with no copy of them (section 11). Two mitigations, neither changing a result: a verifier SHOULD warn beside the report that the earlier claim could not be checked (P9.16; T9-153), and SHOULD retain the signer logs it verified and supply them in later verifications (P7.8; T7-079), as the SDK does by default.

D23 **Bundles.** Decided (26 November 2026, owner decision): the evidence package is renamed the *bundle*, on the wire as well as in prose. The manifest is `originals-bundle.json` and its `format` is `originals-bundle/0.1`; an evidence-set input holding one has kind `bundle`, and a signer DID log inside one has source `bundle` (P14.10, P14.25). The archive layout, the exact ZIP bytes and the manifest's other members are unchanged. There is no transition: a ZIP whose manifest is `originals-evidence.json` with `format` `originals-evidence/0.1` is not a bundle and a reader MUST reject it (P14.10; T14-108); the Original it carries is unaffected and can be bundled again from its log. The SDK's evidence-package API is renamed to match, without deprecated aliases. The method value stays `og:1.0-draft.1`: it versions the asset log, which this change leaves alone, and a bump would make every existing log `unsupported` (P14.1, P7.18) for a change to a container. The bundle has its own version, the manifest's `format`, and the new name is that change. A bundle is a plain ZIP: its file name ends in `.zip` and its media type is `application/zip`, so that ordinary tools open it and checksums of its members can be taken after unpacking; this specification defines no extension or media type of its own, and a reader recognises a bundle by its manifest, never by its name. The vectors were regenerated (P14.26). *Evidence set* (P7.17) keeps its name: a bundle is one of its inputs.

Rulings on minor findings (21 November 2026, owner decisions from the design review):

- A change of a resource’s mediaType alone, on the same digest, is valid and is a new version (P4.4, P14.6; D5).
- A holder can inscribe any signed but uninscribed entry at any time; signed entries do not expire, and this is documented, not prevented (section 11).
- Deactivation is terminal on its branch and permanent only once inscribed; before then, a sibling branch is an author-versus-author fork that only Bitcoin settles (P6.5, D5).
- The method value is `og:1.0-draft.N`, where N increases with every change that alters validity or what a verifier reports, except a change to the bundle format alone, which changes the manifest’s `format` value instead (D23), and freezes as `og:1.0` at release (P14.1; D1).
- Chain order is block height, then transaction index within the block, then inscription index within the transaction (P9.13).
- Entries are signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022` over Ed25519 keys only; BIP-340 and secp256k1 are a later addition (P5.12, P14.8; D4, D7).
- Bitcoin is read as the ord 0.29.x release line reads it, with 0.29.1 as the reference [S31] (P9.31).
- An earlier anchoring candidate that does not verify from Bitcoin evidence is not an anchor, and the first later valid one wins (P9.16).

Rulings on review of the design-review pull request (21 November 2026, owner decisions):

- A preview made from a new version’s bytes MAY be byte-identical to its parent’s; the entry names it and the inscription shows the same bytes again. That the writer made it from the new version binds writers only, and a verifier does not reject an identical preview (P9.30).
- The genesis entry’s parameters are exactly `method`, `scid` and `updateKeys` `[]`; an author entry may carry `updateKeys` `[]`, an editor entry may not, a non-empty value is rejected and reserved, and `updateKeys` authorizes nothing (P14.1; D10).
- `did:og` defines its whole log format itself, byte-compatible with VH Log at [S2] and did:vh at [S3], which are lineage, not dependencies (P14.27–P14.29; D10).
- The mainnet gate is a scripted regtest run with bitcoind and ord 0.29.x, then a fresh mainnet inscription by the owner; the acceptance Original of 1 November 2026 is a prior-format fixture, reported `unsupported` (P14.1; D1).
- The two implementations that must agree on the genesis vector are the Originals SDK and the vectors checker (P14.26; D21).

Rulings on the follow-up review of the design-review pull request (22 November 2026, owner decisions):

- A still-active author MAY endorse an entry by an editor whose DID has since been deactivated: such an editor entry, labelled `did-deactivated-since`, passes the default acceptance check once a later `current-key` author entry on the same branch endorses it. When the deactivated DID is the author DID, no entry it affects ever passes, since no later author entry can then be `current-key` (P7.20; T7-065, T7-066).
- The rules of this specification, not VH Log’s or any DID method’s, govern the asset log; upstream methods apply only to the signer and alias logs that use them (P5.5, P5.7, P8.5).
- Missing evidence for the author DID’s state is never accepting: a post-anchor editor entry whose did:btco author’s sat is not read in full is `unverifiable` (P9.15 row 6; T9-130–T9-133).
- A preview follows the same new-version predicate as P4.4: a digest change, or a change of `mediaType` alone (P9.30; T9-134).
- The checker derives each vector’s whole report from its evidence set, and writers are compared by canonical entry bytes, JCS plus a line feed (P14.25, P14.26).
- A report gives each missing or failed item as a typed diagnostic from a closed list of codes, replacing the manifest-style `missing` and the `dependencies` list (P14.25; T14-094–T14-096). Amended the same day (below): the change records against a prior result that this ruling also introduced are withdrawn.
- `offline` is derived from the evidence set: a complete bundle or complete loose inputs, including a sat read in full where the report depends on one, is `self-contained`, and anything missing is a diagnostic (P7.21; T7-067–T7-070).
- Comparison against a prior result is removed, as over-engineered for how rarely it matters: an evidence set has no prior result, a report describes only the evidence at its tip, and a caller that wants to know what changed compares two reports. The `changes` member and its causes are gone (P7.17, P9.5, P9.16, P9.36, P14.25). Reorganizations are met by depth instead: the transfer check requires the anchoring publication, and every publication the reported head depends on, to have 6 confirmations at the tip, and a shallower one is an `insufficient-depth` diagnostic (P7.22, P14.13). Diagnostics are kept. The `reorg/` vector becomes `anchor-absent/` (P14.26; T9-135, T7-071–T7-073).
- Writers MAY emit the members of the inscription metadata’s CBOR maps in any order; JCS stays the form that the hashes and proofs of `did:og` entries are computed over, never the CBOR bytes. The vector’s envelopes remain a fixed read fixture, and writer interoperability is tested by decoding equivalent maps in different orders (P14.12, P14.26; T14-078, T14-097, T14-098).

Rulings on the SDK build's findings (22 November 2026). Each adopts the choice the SDK build (onionoriginals/sdk#1079–#1084) had already made, so the two agree; all are provisional:

- A deactivation's `operation` is `deactivate`, and `deactivate` appears in no other entry (P6.5, P14.4; T6-027, T6-028; SDK finding F3).
- The DIF did:vh and the VH-Log-based pre-draft both write `did:vh:1.0`, so a did:vh signer's log is judged by [S3]'s rules alone: one that verifies under them is evidence, whichever draft wrote it, and any other is not (P5.8; T5-030, T5-061, T5-062; F5).
- Under `conflict`, the reported checkpoint is the fork point, the last entry common to every branch (P7.18, P14.25; T7-075; F6).
- An evidence set that lacks the target checkpoint, or holds no entry, gives `state` `none`, `outcome` `incomplete` and a `target-missing` diagnostic (P3.2, P7.18, P14.25; T3-013, T7-076; F7).

- Each diagnostic code carries one fixed rule (P14.25; T14-101; F14).
- With no log entry in the evidence set, `binding` is `bound`, since nothing contradicts it, and the `target-missing` diagnostic carries the gap, so such a report's `outcome` is `incomplete`, not `invalid` (P7.18; T7-076; F16).

### Upstream background

Until 21 November 2026 `did:og` was a VH-Log specialisation pinned to VH Log at [S2], revision 9bffcc18 of 6 November 2026, and relied on VH-Log hooks: merged ones for an authoritative source [S20], additional cryptosuites [S21], versionTime precision [S22] and locations not reached over HTTPS [S23], and three still open as pull requests, listed below. VH Log is not being standardized at present, so `did:og` now defines its log format itself (D10): each of those hooks is a rule of this specification (P5.3, P5.9, P9.13, P9.14, P14.1, P14.2, P14.27–P14.29), and none of the pull requests is a dependency. They are recorded at the head revisions checked on 21 November 2026, as background for anyone comparing the two.

| Pull request | Head revision | What it proposed | Where `did:og` now defines it |
|---|---|---|---|
| VH-Log 11 [S25], authorized keys and editors from a controller | 2fe380dbf44bc4068dbeb737929aaf81f9580c60 | A controller DID as the source of a log’s authorized keys, and editors | P5.3, P5.9, P5.15–P5.18, P14.1 (`updateKeys` `[]`, authorizing nothing) |
| VH-Log 10 [S24], another encoding for log entries | 1867a2b1880d75507bc6e454556263ad443490f0 | Alternative encodings that decode to full entries | P9.14; this version defines no compact encoding |
| VH-Log 6 [S28], editor keys for state-only updates | e7710306ec13d01f1aed68783006f7718597da2d | The same editor boundary for logs with updateKeys | P5.16 |

The did:vh extension mechanism [S19], at head revision b3e7a56d9e3658f60411ba031d145cefc2127384, is withdrawn as a dependency (21 November 2026): `did:og` does not use the did:vh name, and an author or editor DID that is a did:vh follows the standalone did:vh method [S3], which needs no extension.
