## Decision register and source baseline

### Decisions

Choices marked **Decided** were taken in review on 15 October 2026 unless a later date is given; **Open** items still block an interoperable release.

D1 **Asset identifier and state shape.** Decided: each Original is a did:vh, `did:vh:<SCID>`, whose log is its asset log under the Originals did:vh extension [S19]; there is no separate DID log and no `did:cel`. Each entry’s state is the full asset state, and the DID document is derived, with a `#files` service built from the resource descriptor. The Original gains a did:webvh alias, its own small log bound in both directions, when published on the web, and a did:btco alias when anchored, or, as proposed, a did:evm alias when anchored on an EVM chain (section 3, P3.6–P3.9). No Originals were issued under earlier formats except the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, written under the previous inscription encoding; it is not converted and no longer verifies (P14 intro). Provisional (owner decision, 21 October 2026, to record the SDK’s choices for review): the `originals:1.0` method value, the asset state schema and the derived DID document (section 14). Open: confirming them, exact-checkpoint resolution and deactivation history.

D2 **Author key binding and key policy.** The baseline is one author per Original, for its whole life, whose identity-bound key signs each author entry directly, with no dedicated per-Original keypair; editors added by owner decision (21 October 2026) sign editor entries signed by editors’ own keys. Pre-rotation is on the DID side, not the asset log. The author DID is the asset log’s controller [S25]: author entries are signed by any assertionMethod key of the author DID that is not an updateKey of its own log, and rotation happens only in the author DID (section 5, P5.3). Decided (owner decision Q6, 19 October 2026): there is no separate Originals declaration in the author DID; the author’s key SHOULD be used only for Originals entries, which verifiers cannot check (P5.11). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): the author grants editors, by DID, permission to change the name, the metadata or the resource; editors sign their own entries without the author’s countersignature; only author entries change grants, aliases, lifecycle or parameters, anchor or deactivate; grants and revocations take effect from the next entry (P5.15–P5.18). Editors rely on VH-Log PR 11’s editors from a controller [S25] (owner decision, 21 October 2026); PR 6 [S28] draws the same boundary for logs with updateKeys. Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): key custody is out of scope for this profile; applications choose how author and editor keys are stored, and P5.11’s signer rules apply whatever the custody. Decided (owner decision, 1 November 2026): with one resource per Original, a grant allows or refuses new versions of it (P5.15).

D3 **Historical identity and ordering.** Decided (owner decision Q14, 19 October 2026): asset logs do not use VH witnesses, hosted or not (P9.8, P9.9). Off Bitcoin, an Original has claimed ordering only (P7.6); Bitcoin orders everything from the anchoring entry on (P9.10, P9.13), and the first valid anchor wins (P9.16). Witnesses were dropped because, once activated, they could not be removed and every later entry needed their threshold, so losing a threshold would freeze every Original using them, and Originals witnesses would be stateful services; anchoring already orders entries on the anchoring sat. It does not establish uniqueness: a competing anchor on another sat can exist undiscovered, so uniqueness is reported as not established and ownership as provisional (P9.16). Author DIDs keep whatever their own method offers, including did:webvh witnesses. Also decided: author DIDs may use any method with verifiable version history that the profile lists, and the baseline lists did:vh, did:webvh and did:btco, whose versions are inscriptions ordered by chain order, and an inscribed entry may name only a did:btco version that precedes its inscription (P5.8, P5.13); an inscribed entry is checked against the author DID as it stood at its inscription time, so later rotation or author DID deactivation does not make an anchored Original non-current (P9.15). Superseded: witness selection, independence, the witness declaration and the 60-second witness freshness bound; the Originals declaration was later dropped too (D2). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): a did:webvh signer that moves keeps its identity only through moves its own verified log records, never through `alsoKnownAs` or redirects (P5.14). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): off Bitcoin there is no freshness policy that makes an Original current; an unanchored Original is reported only as valid as of its verified checkpoint, and only an anchored Original can be current (P7.10). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): for an anchored Original, a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s DID is as fresh as the verifier knows about. It uses the newest version it has obtained or retained, states it, and need not prove that no newer one exists (P7.8, P9.15); the cost is that a host withholding a key removal can get later entries accepted by readers who do not know of it. Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): anchoring does not require did:btco DIDs for the author or editors; it stays a SHOULD for authors who want Bitcoin to be all a reader needs (P9.18). Decided (owner decision, 23 October 2026): a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s freshness is established only by that DID’s own witnesses or watchers; a log merely obtained from a location is reported with where and when it was obtained, and readers are told plainly that a newer version may exist (P7.8). A moved identity’s log is first sought where the latest verified entry, or failing that the identity’s earliest entry, points, and an unverified entry can direct a request but never decides what is accepted (P5.14). Open: how identity versions are packaged.

D4 **Entry state and package.** Decided: resource digests are SHA-256 multihashes, the only algorithm in the baseline set (P4.2); canonicalization is whatever the pinned VH Log version uses, and proofs use `eddsa-jcs-2022` or `bip340-jcs-2025`, permitted under VH-Log’s proposed rule for additional cryptosuites [S21] (P5.12). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): the limits in P14.13; the resource limit was dropped when an Original became one resource (owner decision, 1 November 2026). Decided (owner decision, 23 October 2026): a reader rejects a carriage return, non-compact JSON or a missing final line feed (P14.2); the 2<sup>53</sup> − 1 magnitude bound applies to every number however it is written (P14.3); `name` is measured in UTF-16 code units (P14.5); a proof has exactly the five members P14.8 lists; and writers bind ZIP order, date, flags and mode (P14.10). Provisional: entry state fields, file names, digest encoding, and the evidence package format and its completeness declaration (section 14). Proposed (owner request, 9 November 2026): carried logs, a file that keeps its media type and carries the asset log; their carriers, media types and JSON member name are provisional (P7.11–P7.16, P14.14–P14.17). After an experimental SDK build (owner decisions, 11–12 November 2026, in review of pull request 59): the 65,536-byte entry limit is measured over the entry’s JCS form in UTF-8 in every format (P14.13); descriptor media types carry no parameters, and one with a parameter is rejected (P14.6); a carried file whose remaining bytes do not match fails overall, with an explicit reason (P7.12); importing a carried file is not an opt-in to resolve (P7.13); a checkpoint from before the anchoring entry may be carried as a historical checkpoint (P7.14, P7.15); a reader detects the carrier, treating a file extension as a hint only (P14.14); a JSON member is written last and found anywhere in the root object, duplicate root names are rejected, and the root is nesting level 1 (P14.17); a malformed marker structure is no carrier, and the writer’s refusal is a parse test, with an unclosed `pre` no longer a reason to refuse (P14.16). On the open questions from that build (owner decisions, 13 November 2026): while P14.21 pins no contract, an Original whose genesis entry has an `anchor` member is unsupported, even from a carried log with no anchoring entry, and section 14 takes precedence over P7.14’s reading of a historical checkpoint (P7.14). Open: confirming those provisional values, the per-type embedding-safety checks, the removal of a JSON member that is not last, a text block’s rules for mixed line ends and trailing whitespace, and test vectors. Publish real test vectors only after those choices.

D5 **Operational edge cases.** Decided: an update that leaves a digest unchanged does not advance that resource’s version (P4.4); an explicit null removes metadata, and the name cannot be null (P4.6); only Bitcoin settles a fork: until an Original is anchored, divergent valid branches are an unresolved conflict and none is current, whatever the timestamps (owner decision Q5, 19 October 2026; P6.6, P9.16); proposed: for an Original whose genesis entry names an EVM anchor chain, the anchor on its token settles a fork, under that chain’s finality and trust class, and on an attested chain the settlement is never reported as proven (P9.20, P9.24, P9.27). Decided (owner decision, 21 October 2026): a shared host accepts one append at a time by comparing the submitted parent with its head atomically, refuses others with a stale-parent conflict, and never changes a signed submission; the refused change is kept and reconciled into a new signed candidate (P6.7, P6.8); off-chain forks stay unresolved, and only an author’s anchor settles one (P6.6). Provisional: millisecond versionTime precision, which P14.2 uses under [S22]. Open: atomic local commit details, and confirming that precision.

D6 **Transport profiles.** Decided for the Bitcoin representation (owner decision Q15, 20 October 2026; P9.5, P9.13, P9.17): inscriptions on the anchoring sat carry complete entries, with no compact delta yet; the anchoring inscription carries the whole log from genesis through the anchoring entry, and later inscriptions one entry each, so Bitcoin alone verifies the log (owner decision, 20 October 2026, replacing Q15’s anchoring-entry-only inscription; P9.17); resource bytes are not needed for the log to verify, and only did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories still come from elsewhere (P9.18); an inscription counts at one confirmation, every report states its depth, and a reorganization that removes an accepted inscription is reported; the Bitcoin `src` kind is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias; did:btco author DIDs follow the did:btco method v0.2.0 [S26]. A verifier compares the sat number and network of a did:btco DID with where its index reports each inscription, and does not accept placement that two indexes it uses disagree on (P9.10). Decided (owner decision, 1 November 2026, replacing the JSON Lines body and media type `application/vnd.originals.vh+jsonl` of 21 October 2026): an inscription’s metadata carries its entries, as a JSON array in CBOR, and its body is the exact bytes of the resource version its last entry names, under that version’s media type, so the sat shows the Original’s current version; the 350,000-byte limit covers metadata and body together; the JSON-to-CBOR mapping, ord’s for `--json-metadata`, is provisional (P9.17, P14.12). Decided (owner decisions, 21 October 2026): one confirmation on every network, the 350,000-byte inscription limit, the did:btco network prefixes `sig:`, `test:` and `reg:`, which this profile uses as the SDK does. Decided (owner decision, 23 October 2026): an inscription after the anchor whose signer version cannot be verified is reported as unverifiable and does not stop the accepted state being current, and one naming a version the verifier holds under another versionId is rejected (P9.15); a later publication from the same head waits while an earlier inscription extending it is unverifiable, so chain order decides, accepting that such an entry can leave the Original waiting permanently unless the named signer updates its DID through the version number it names, and rejecting an entry naming a version above the P14.13 limit or after a deactivated version (P9.15); a submitted anchoring inscription the network is not observed to hold is labelled submitted, not observed, never pending (P9.18); whether a did:btco inscription is a version depends only on its own metadata (P5.13). Upstream ask: the did:btco method defines no network prefixes, proposed in [S30]. Proposed (owner request, 9 November 2026): an Original’s genesis entry may fix an EVM chain as its anchor chain instead of Bitcoin; it is anchored on the one token derived from it and its minter, in a single contract with no administrator deployed at the same address on every supported chain; ordering, ownership and the ability to publish then rest on that chain; did:btco signers are excluded (P9.19–P9.29, P14.18–P14.22). Within that proposal (owner decisions, 10 November 2026): the supported chains are a named list, each identified by the hash of its first block and not by its chain ID alone, replacing support for any chain that runs the contract; each listed chain has a trust class, `consensus`, `exit-protected`, `consortium` or `operator`, which every report states, and ordering on a `consortium` or `operator` chain is never reported as proven (P9.21, P9.27, P14.22); a publication’s entries and resource version are held in the contract’s state, not in event logs, so they last as long as the chain and the contract can read them, and a publication that does not change the resource refers to the bytes already stored (P9.25, P14.20); the first list is Ethereum, Monad, Base, Arbitrum One, Arc, Tempo, Robinhood Chain and Elysium (P14.22). After review (11 November 2026): the contract enforces the 350,000-byte limit and serves bytes in ranges, so no record is too large to read or to reject and a holder cannot stop earlier history from resolving (P9.25, P14.20); two providers are compared at the lower of their finalized blocks, and one being behind is not a disagreement (P9.22); a record holds no block number, because a contract on an Arbitrum chain sees its parent chain’s, so publications are ordered by record index alone and more than one can be accepted from one block (P9.20, P14.20). Also (owner decision, 11 November 2026): an Original anchored on an EVM chain can move up, to a chain in a higher layer or to Bitcoin, and never sideways, down or back; the anchor layers are an attested chain, of class `operator` or `consortium`, then a proven chain, of class `exit-protected` or `consensus`, then Bitcoin; a move needs the author’s signature and a departure published by the owner, carries the whole log to the destination, raises nothing that is said of the earlier stretch, and leaves the Original dependent on the chains it left (P9.28, P9.29); the move entry’s signer is checked at the departure only, so a later key rotation or DID deactivation does not stop the arrival (owner decision, 11 November 2026; P9.29). Also (owner direction, 11 November 2026): an Original has a resolvable DID at every layer, so the alias of an Original anchored on an EVM chain is a DID of the did:evm method, which this profile defines and which names its token, replacing the first draft’s CAIP-19 alias; the CAIP-19 asset ID is derived from it, and a DID left behind by a move resolves to a report of the move (P3.9, P14.18). After an experimental SDK build (owner decisions, 11–12 November 2026, in review of pull request 59): a token has no record cap and readers no budget, a verifier resumes from a retained checkpoint, and a first verification can be costly (P9.25); finality is assumed in normal operation, and evidence of a finality failure is a warning that invalidates retained results (P9.22); two providers are compared only at the lower finalized block, and providers must serve state at it however far it trails the head (P9.22); uniqueness on an attested chain is described as attested by the operator or by the validators (P9.24); the holder, the address approved for the token and an operator approved for all may publish (P9.23); a referring record reports the reused body’s length, and reused bytes do not count again toward the limit (P14.20); `begin` emits an event carrying the upload ID, as a discovery aid only (P14.20); minting stays open, and a token’s existence or metadata never shows a valid Original (P9.23); the contract may have a per-chain address where the common one is not available, with its code still checked (P14.21); the SDK discloses staged transactions and the cost of large metadata (P9.27); a move is judged by current classes until its departure record is read (P9.28); a final departure with an unreadable destination is reported as such, with no arrival claimed, a verified move is not a change of anchor, and entries from before the first anchoring entry are described as carried, with claimed ordering (P9.29); with a chain the Original left unreadable, proofs and hash links are still checked and that stretch’s ordering is claimed only (P7.14, P9.29); a did:evm resolver takes a candidate from an anchoring or arrival publication and verifies it (P3.9); the anchoring entry is the signed entry that first adds the derived token’s alias, and an arrival is distinct from the first anchor (section 2, P9.19). On the open questions from that build (owner decisions, 13 November 2026): one usable provider is enough, even when it is the only one configured; a provider that fails the chain ID or block 0 check, or cannot serve state at the finalized block, is dropped and the remaining providers’ reads stand, replacing the reading that its failure makes evidence missing; two usable providers that answer differently are missing evidence; and every report names the providers its read came from and marks a read that one provider answered as uncorroborated (P9.22, P9.27); a final departure with an unreadable destination leaves at most the entries before the first anchoring entry reported, as P7.14 does for an unreadable current chain, with no arrival claimed (P9.29); a checkpoint is the head entry’s versionId, which pins every earlier entry, with the chain, token, accepting record index, finalized block number and hash, and trust class of the read that verified it; it holds no log, the log supplied to resume must reach exactly that versionId, and none is made while a publication waits on an unverifiable entry (P9.25); a writer checks its committed, unpublished log as a local copy, verified whole, reported unverified on the chain and never current, and valid only for its own check before publishing, never in a report given to anyone else (P9.18). Carried logs and EVM anchoring are read together: a carried log never stands in for an anchor on any chain, and a carrier is never part of a publication’s body (P7.14, P7.16). Open: hosted discovery and relocation, Boop adapter changes, and any compact encoding (P9.14); the name of the did:evm method and its registration (P14.18); the EVM anchor contract and the form of its `tokenURI` (P14.20, P14.21), the parts of the class assessments that the chains’ own documents do not settle (P14.22), and whether to keep Bitcoin’s one accepted publication per block on EVM chains, which would need each chain’s own block number in the record (P9.20), how the history of chain classes is kept and resolved (P9.28), and how an evidence package could carry proof of a departure, so that a moved Original need not depend on the chain it left staying readable (P9.29).

D7 **Later capabilities.** Decided: authorship never transfers. An Original anchors once, terminally, to one sat, from the local or the web layer, by an update entry that adds the sat’s did:btco alias (P9.10), and after anchoring no entry can add or replace a did:webvh alias (owner decision on #41, 20 October 2026; P9.10). Layers move one way: local to web, and either to Bitcoin (P9.10); proposed: an Original first anchored on an EVM chain is the exception to anchoring once, and can move up through the anchor layers to Bitcoin, where its anchor is terminal (P9.28, P9.29); an anchored Original needs no web alias for discovery or verification, because its did:btco alias names a sat that carries its complete log (P9.17). The alias it had at anchoring, if any, may stay; the sat’s holder is the provisional owner (P9.16), and transferring an Original means moving the sat, with the evidence that stays off Bitcoin handed over separately (P9.18). On Bitcoin a change needs an authorized signature, by the author or a granted editor, and an inscription on the sat, so a sale freezes the Original unless a signer and the owner cooperate (P9.10–P9.13). The first valid anchor in chain order wins, and a verifier reports its uniqueness as not established and ownership as provisional (P9.16). Decided (owner decision, 1 November 2026, replacing that of 21 October 2026 that let author entries add and remove resources): an Original is exactly one resource, which can have many versions (P4.1). Open: whether to add encryption or recovery. The anchoring sat carries the complete log and every resource version inscribed on it; earlier versions and did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories are carried off Bitcoin, in evidence packages and on hosts (P9.17, P9.18). Apart from the mainnet acceptance Original of 1 November 2026, no Originals were issued under earlier formats, and no migration or legacy verification is provided for it.

### Release blockers

Every open or provisional choice, the rules it affects and what it blocks. An **interoperable release** means two independent implementations reproduce the same results; a **mainnet release** means anchoring real Originals on Bitcoin. None of these permits skipping a check: until a choice is made, implementations apply the rules as written and label provisional values as such.
