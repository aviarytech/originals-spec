## Hosted publication and Bitcoin boundaries

### Hosted publication

The SDK hosted path publishes two logs: a did:webvh method log (`did.jsonl`) signed separately with Ed25519, and the asset’s own log (`cel.json`). This draft keeps two logs with different roles: the Original’s history is only in its did:vh asset log, and the did:webvh log is a small web alias that points to the did:vh and locates its files through `#files` (section 3). A host serves the selected VH profile and exact resource evidence; the public reader verifies the same checkpoint semantics as the local reader.

The Boop adapter needs its actual publishing path replaced: ordinary publication uses local per-asset Ed25519 signing rather than SDK publishToWeb. Replacing only publishToWeb leaves that signing path in place. The exact-snapshot public-reader requirement from Boop issue 232 [S10] remains a regression requirement.

### Proposed hosted rules

P9.1 A published snapshot MUST bind its asset state, history checkpoint and resource descriptors. The public reader MUST verify that exact snapshot and MUST NOT combine history from one checkpoint with metadata or bytes presented as another.

P9.2 A publish retry MUST resume or repeat the same intended snapshot. Uploading bytes, publishing a locator or changing an index MUST NOT create a new asset transition unless an explicit authorized transition is part of the operation.

P9.3 Host relocation and locator updates MUST preserve authenticated continuity under the chosen profile. Public delivery MUST use the new profile discriminator and MUST NOT relabel evidence in another format as VH.

### Bitcoin adapter

The current inscription encodings are replaced, not retained. P9.17 defines the representation: the anchoring inscription carries the whole log through the anchoring entry, each later inscription one entry, no metadata, resources not inscribed, under a provisional media type. The confirmation policy is provisional (P9.5), and size limits and any compact encoding (P9.14) are open (D6).

P9.4 A Bitcoin publication MUST identify the exact accepted checkpoint and content commitment it represents. Only a verified change beyond the prior durable published boundary may form the next delta. A pending or failed transaction MUST NOT advance that durable boundary.

P9.5 Forks, reorganizations and competing publications MUST be exposed explicitly. Confirmation policy and reorganization handling MUST be defined by the adapter; transaction observation alone MUST NOT be described as permanent finality. In the baseline profile an inscription counts once it has one confirmation (provisional, D6); an unconfirmed inscription is reported as observed, never as part of the accepted log, and every report MUST state the confirmation depth of what it relied on. A reorganization that removes an accepted inscription MUST be reported with VH-Log’s `authoritative-source-changed` warning [S20], not silently.

P9.6 Transaction retries MUST distinguish rebroadcasting the same intended transaction from preparing a replacement. Asset edits MUST NOT be silently re-signed or rebased because a transaction is retried. A wallet or the holder of an anchoring sat MUST NOT acquire author or VH update authority by possession alone; for a did:btco author DID, possession of the DID’s sat is control of the author DID (P5.13).

P9.7 A host MUST serve resource bytes with their signed mediaType and MUST NOT let a valid signature override content-safety policy. Active content such as HTML or SVG MUST be served from an origin without user credentials, with content-type sniffing disabled and a restrictive content security policy, or rendered only in a sandbox. Any SDK consumer that renders resources or asset state strings, including desktop and mobile clients, MUST apply the same isolation and MUST escape names and metadata.

P9.8 An Original’s asset log MUST NOT use VH witnesses, whether or not it is hosted, and hosted publication requires none. Off Bitcoin an Original is author-signed with claimed ordering only (P7.6); ordering on the anchoring sat and a provisional owner come from anchoring (P9.10–P9.16); anchoring does not establish that no other sat carries a competing anchor (P9.16). An author DID MAY use its own DID method’s witnesses or watchers, and this profile does not change them; the author DID of an Original SHOULD use them where its method has them, because P7.8 and P9.15 depend on its history.

P9.9 An asset log entry that sets the VH witness parameter to anything other than an empty object MUST be rejected (P9.8).

### Anchoring and ownership

An Original moves up through layers one way only: from local to web, and from either to Bitcoin. Bitcoin is the last layer. The SDK requires web publication before anchoring; this draft does not (P9.10). Once an Original is anchored to a sat, its did:btco alias names that sat, and every later change is a reinscription on it.

An Original has one author for its whole life. Only the author anchors it, and after anchoring the author or an editor the author has granted signs each change (P5.16). Ownership is separate: the owner is whoever holds the anchoring sat. On Bitcoin a change needs both: an authorized signature, and an inscription on the sat, which only its holder can make. After a sale the Original is frozen unless an authorized signer and the new owner cooperate. Selling or giving an Original means moving the sat, and needs nothing from the author.

P9.10 An Original MAY be anchored from the local layer or from the web layer. The author anchors it with an ordinary update entry (section 6) whose asset state adds the did:btco alias of the anchoring sat (section 3), inscribed on that sat. The first entry whose asset state lists a did:btco alias is the anchoring entry, and an entry inscribed on a sat whose alias its state does not list is not one. P9.16 decides which anchoring entry is the anchor. A did:btco DID in this profile has the provisional form `did:btco:` followed by an optional network prefix, `sig:` for signet, `test:` for testnet or `reg:` for regtest, with no prefix meaning mainnet, and then the sat number in decimal with no sign and no leading zeros, below the total supply of sats (D6). A verifier MUST reject any other form. It MUST treat an inscription as on the sat a did:btco DID names only if the Bitcoin index it uses, on that network, reports the inscription on the sat with that number; the label under which inscriptions were supplied to it does not count. An inscription the index places on another sat, or a read from a different network, is not on that sat. Placement rests on the index: a verifier SHOULD use an index it runs itself or two independent indexes, MUST report which it used (P9.11), and MUST NOT accept an anchoring entry, a later inscription or a did:btco version whose placement two indexes it uses disagree on; it reports missing evidence until they agree. Anchoring MUST happen at most once and is terminal: every later entry MUST keep that did:btco alias, and a later entry that removes or changes it, or adds another did:btco alias, MUST be rejected. After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT add a did:webvh alias that the anchoring entry’s state does not list, including one that replaces an existing alias, and a verifier MUST reject it: the web is a layer before Bitcoin, and an Original does not move back to an earlier layer (D7). A did:webvh alias the anchoring entry’s state lists MAY stay or be removed, but once removed it MUST NOT be added again. An anchored Original is found through its did:btco alias, whose sat carries its complete log (P9.17).

P9.11 The owner of an anchored Original is the current holder of its anchoring sat, as determined by the Bitcoin adapter under its confirmation policy (P9.5). An Original that is not anchored has no owner. Inscriptions of the same evidence on any other sat MUST NOT confer ownership. A verifier MUST report ownership separately from authorship, and MUST state the confirmation depth it relied on and whether more than one independent Bitcoin index agreed on the sat’s location.

P9.12 After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT be reported as current unless it is signed by the author under P5.3, or by an editor under P5.16, and inscribed on the anchoring sat, extending the accepted head. This applies to every operation, including deactivation. An entry the author or an editor signs but that is not inscribed on the sat, and an inscription on the sat that neither signed, MUST NOT change the Original’s state. Holding the sat MUST NOT confer author or VH update authority, and transferring an Original MUST mean transferring the sat.

P9.13 A resolver MUST derive an anchored Original’s state by following, in chain order on the anchoring sat, the publications that extend the accepted head, not by taking the newest inscription. Each accepted publication MUST be at a strictly greater block height than the one before it; among publications that extend the same head, the earliest in chain order wins. Because at most one publication per block is accepted, chain order within a block only decides between candidates in that block. Inscriptions that do not extend the head MUST be ignored and SHOULD be reported. For an anchored Original, Bitcoin is the log’s authoritative source under VH-Log’s rule for one [S20], and chain order on the sat orders its entries; the author key checks of P9.15 still apply. The extension defines a Bitcoin `src` kind under the did:vh extension mechanism [S19], whose value is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias, and how the log is read from it under VH-Log’s rule for locations not reached over HTTPS [S23].

P9.14 An inscription MAY carry an entry in a compact encoding, such as a delta against the previous entry, defined by the extension under VH-Log’s rule for alternative encodings [S24]. It MUST decode deterministically to the full JSON entry, and entry hashes and proofs MUST be computed over the decoded full entry. An inscription that does not decode, or decodes differently for different readers, MUST be rejected.

P9.15 The entries before the anchoring entry, carried in the anchoring inscription, have claimed ordering (P7.6): each is checked against the signer DID version it names (P5.3), and a key’s removal after it was signed does not invalidate it, though the anchoring entry itself must be signed by a key valid at its inscription time. A verifier MUST check each entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, from the anchoring entry on, against its signer’s DID state at its inscription time, not its latest state. The rules below are stated for the author DID, which is the signer of an author entry. For an editor entry, they apply to the editor DID instead: its named version, its state at the inscription time and its signing key. Separately, an editor entry MUST be rejected if the author DID’s state at its inscription time is deactivated (P5.18). An inscription’s inscription time is the greatest block timestamp among the block containing it and that block’s ancestors, so it never decreases along the chain. The state at an inscription is the later of the author DID version the entry names and the latest author DID version whose versionTime is no later than the inscription time. The signing key MUST meet P5.3 both at the named version and in that state, and an entry inscribed when that state is deactivated MUST be rejected. A key removal or author DID deactivation therefore refuses entries whose inscription time is at or after its versionTime, unless, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, the entry names a later version that lists the key again (see below), and MUST NOT make non-current an accepted entry whose inscription time precedes it. Miners set block timestamps within consensus bounds, so inscription time can lag real time and an entry inscribed shortly after a removal can still be accepted. A verifier that cannot establish the author DID’s history through the inscription time MUST report incomplete identity evidence (P5.6), not success. For a did:btco author DID, the state is selected by chain order instead, and an entry naming a version that does not precede its inscription is rejected (P5.13). For a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, versionTime is claimed by the DID’s controller, so the report MUST state whether the author DID’s own witnesses or watchers support it. That controller can publish a version whose versionTime precedes inscriptions already accepted, though not its own previous version; a verifier that retained an earlier acceptance and then finds such a version MUST report a retroactive revocation conflict rather than silently change its result. Because the later version is used, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID a version that the entry names can also re-authorize a key that the version in effect at the inscription time had removed; only the author DID’s controller can arrange this. Block timestamps can lag real time, which is why a named version with a later versionTime is accepted for these methods; for did:btco, chain order leaves no such lag and P5.13 applies.

P9.16 An Original has claimed ordering before its anchor (P7.6); the anchoring inscription’s block is the first ordering evidence for that history, showing that every earlier entry existed no later than that block. A holder of an authorized author key can anchor a different branch of the same Original on another sat. The anchor is the first valid anchoring entry, including under P9.15, that is inscribed on the sat it names, in chain order (P5.13) under the adapter’s confirmation policy (P9.5). Anchoring settles any fork of the Original (P6.6): the anchor’s branch is the Original’s history. A verifier MUST report every anchoring entry it knows of for the Original. Any other than the anchor MUST be reported as a conflicting anchor; it and its descendants MUST NOT be reported as current and MUST NOT confer ownership. No index lists every sat, so a verifier MUST report the anchor’s uniqueness as not established and the ownership it reports as provisional (P9.11). If a verifier later learns of a valid anchoring entry earlier in chain order than the one it reported as the anchor, it MUST report the earlier one as the anchor and the previous one as a conflicting anchor, together with the change in owner, and MUST NOT change its result silently. This is an accepted risk: a holder of a stolen author key who also holds a sat can anchor first, making the author’s later anchor a conflicting one, so an author who needs protection SHOULD anchor early.

P9.17 Each inscription on the anchoring sat that carries Originals entries MUST carry complete entries as JSON Lines, with media type `application/vnd.originals.vh+jsonl` (provisional, D6), unless a compact encoding is defined under P9.14; none is defined yet. The anchoring inscription MUST carry the Original’s whole log, in order, from its genesis entry through the anchoring entry, which MUST be its last entry; every later inscription MUST carry exactly one entry. An inscription of this media type MUST NOT carry did:btco DID-document metadata or any other metadata, and one that does MUST be rejected. Resources are not inscribed: the entry’s resource descriptors are the content commitment of P9.4. The anchoring sat therefore carries the Original’s complete log [S20], and a verifier needs no copy of it: a copy that differs from the entries on the sat is ignored. An anchoring inscription whose entries do not verify as one log from its genesis, or whose last entry is not an anchoring entry naming that sat, is not an anchor. If a verifier cannot read the anchoring sat’s inscriptions in full, resolution MUST fail and the report MUST state missing evidence (P3.3). An inscription’s body, the encoded JSON Lines, MUST NOT exceed 350,000 bytes (provisional, D6), which keeps a full anchoring inscription within Bitcoin’s standard transaction size. The SDK MUST refuse to anchor an Original whose log through the anchoring entry exceeds it, and a verifier MUST reject a larger inscription.

P9.18 Bitcoin alone verifies an anchored Original’s log (P9.17) and, for a signer whose DID is a did:btco, that signer’s identity (P5.13): no host or copy is needed. Two things are not on Bitcoin. Resource bytes are not needed for the log to verify: a reader without them MUST report the log as verified and the missing content as unavailable (P7.4), never as a verification failure. The DID history of a did:vh or did:webvh signer is needed: without it identity evidence is incomplete (P5.6), and its freshness cannot come from Bitcoin (P9.15). An author who wants Bitcoin to be all a reader needs SHOULD use a did:btco author DID. Before inscribing the anchoring entry, the SDK MUST verify the log through the signed anchoring entry and the identity evidence of every signer of it, and MUST NOT inscribe otherwise. It MUST label the result pending Bitcoin acceptance until the inscription is confirmed, because the checks of P5.13, P9.15 and P9.16 depend on its position, and then verify the Original as anchored. It MUST tell the author what Bitcoin will not hold: the resource bytes and any did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories. This profile does not guarantee their replication or availability. When the anchoring sat is transferred, the sender’s SDK SHOULD give the recipient the resource bytes and off-chain signer DID histories it holds, and the recipient’s SDK SHOULD verify them.
