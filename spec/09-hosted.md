## Hosted publication and Bitcoin boundaries

### Hosted publication

The SDK hosted path publishes two logs: a did:webvh method log (`did.jsonl`) signed separately with Ed25519, and the asset’s own log (`cel.json`). This profile keeps two logs with different roles: the Original’s history is only in its `did:og` asset log, and the did:webvh log is a small web alias that points to the `did:og` and locates its files through `#files` (section 3). A host serves the selected VH profile and exact resource evidence; the public reader verifies the same checkpoint semantics as the local reader.

### Proposed hosted rules

P9.1 A published snapshot MUST bind its asset state, history checkpoint and resource descriptor. The public reader MUST verify that exact snapshot and MUST NOT combine history from one checkpoint with metadata or bytes presented as another.

P9.2 A publish retry MUST resume or repeat the same intended snapshot. Uploading bytes, publishing a locator or changing an index MUST NOT create a new asset transition unless an explicit authorized transition is part of the operation.

P9.3 Host relocation and locator updates MUST preserve authenticated continuity under the chosen profile. Public delivery MUST use the new profile discriminator and MUST NOT relabel evidence in another format as VH.

### Bitcoin adapter

The current inscription encodings are replaced, not retained. P9.17 defines the representation: each [[ref: publication]] on the anchoring sat is one inscription, or several consecutive parts (P9.32); its body is what its last entry signs, by default a preview of the resource version that entry names (P9.30), or else that version itself or no body; and its metadata carries the log, from genesis through the anchoring entry in the anchoring publication and one entry in each later one, with the DID history of each did:vh or did:webvh signer those entries rely on (P9.33). Bitcoin is read as the pinned ord release line reads it, on the network the genesis entry names, at a tip each report states (P9.31, P9.34). An inscription counts at one confirmation on every network (P9.5), and its metadata and body together are at most 350,000 bytes (P9.17); any compact encoding (P9.14) is open (D6).

P9.4 A Bitcoin publication MUST identify the exact accepted checkpoint and content commitment it represents. Only a verified change beyond the prior durable published boundary may form the next delta. A pending or failed transaction MUST NOT advance that durable boundary.

P9.5 Forks, reorganizations and competing publications MUST be exposed explicitly. Confirmation policy and reorganization handling MUST be defined by the adapter; transaction observation alone MUST NOT be described as permanent finality. In the baseline profile an inscription counts once it has one confirmation, on every network (D6); an unconfirmed inscription is reported as observed, never as part of the accepted log, and every report MUST state the confirmation depth of what it relied on. A report reflects only the inscriptions confirmed at its evidence set’s tip (P7.17): an inscription that a reorganization removed is not in a sat read at a later tip, and the report is derived without it. When the evidence set reads the anchoring sat in full at its tip with no anchoring publication of the Original on it, as after a reorganization that removed it, the Original is reported as not anchored: `bitcoin` `not-anchored`, a copy's anchoring entry and every entry after it `ignored` (P7.19, P9.12), and the state `valid-as-of` the entry before the anchoring entry, with `claimed` ordering (P7.6, P7.10). A sat that is not read in full at the tip is `missing-evidence`, as P7.14 states. A buyer or recipient meets reorganizations by depth: the transfer check requires the publications it relies on to have 6 confirmations (P7.22, P14.13). That depth applies to the publications, not to the transaction that last moved the sat: the holder follows the adapter’s confirmation policy (P9.11), so a sat transfer with one confirmation is reported, and the transfer check is met, once the publications are 6 deep. Counting at one confirmation does not permit discarding the prepared transactions a retry needs (P9.6).

P9.6 Transaction retries MUST distinguish rebroadcasting the same intended transaction from preparing a replacement. Asset edits MUST NOT be silently re-signed or rebased because a transaction is retried. A wallet, the holder of an anchoring sat, or whoever pays for or broadcasts an inscription MUST NOT acquire author or update authority by that role; for a did:btco author DID, possession of the DID’s sat is control of the author DID (P5.13).

P9.7 A host MUST serve resource bytes with their signed mediaType and MUST NOT let a valid signature override content-safety policy. Active content such as HTML or SVG MUST be served from an origin without user credentials, with content-type sniffing disabled and a restrictive content security policy, or rendered only in a sandbox. Any SDK consumer that renders resources or asset state strings, including desktop and mobile clients, MUST apply the same isolation and MUST escape names and metadata.

P9.8 An Original’s asset log MUST NOT use VH witnesses, whether or not it is hosted, and hosted publication requires none. Off Bitcoin an Original is author-signed with claimed ordering only (P7.6); ordering on the anchoring sat and a provisional holder come from anchoring (P9.10–P9.16); anchoring does not by itself establish that no other sat carries a competing anchor (P9.16). An author DID MAY use its own DID method’s witnesses or watchers, and this profile does not change them; the author DID of an Original SHOULD use them where its method has them, because P7.8 depends on its history for entries that are not inscribed. Entries inscribed on the anchoring sat depend only on signer history that Bitcoin evidences, with the witness proofs its method requires (P9.33).

P9.9 An asset log entry that sets the VH witness parameter to anything other than an empty object MUST be rejected (P9.8).

### Anchoring and holding

An Original moves up through layers one way only: from local to web, and from either to Bitcoin. Bitcoin is the last layer. The SDK requires web publication before anchoring; this profile does not (P9.10). Once an Original is anchored to a sat, its did:btco alias names that sat, and every later change is a reinscription on it.

An Original has one author for its whole life. Only the author anchors it, and after anchoring the author or a granted editor signs each change (P5.16). Paying for or broadcasting an inscription is not signing it, and gives no authority. Holding is separate: the Original’s holder is whoever holds the anchoring sat. On Bitcoin a change needs both: an authorized signature, and an inscription on the sat, which only its holder can make. After a sale the Original is frozen unless an authorized signer and the new holder cooperate. Selling or giving an Original means moving the sat, and needs nothing from the author.

P9.10 An Original MAY be anchored from the local layer or from the web layer. The author anchors it with an ordinary update entry (section 6) whose asset state adds the did:btco alias of the anchoring sat (section 3), inscribed on that sat as the last entry of the anchoring publication (P9.32). The first entry whose asset state lists a did:btco alias is the [[ref: anchoring entry]], and an entry inscribed on a sat whose alias its state does not list is not one. P9.16 decides which anchoring entry is the anchor. A did:btco DID in this profile has the form `did:btco:` followed by an optional network prefix, `sig:` for signet, `test:` for testnet or `reg:` for regtest, with no prefix meaning mainnet, and then the sat number in decimal with no sign and no leading zeros, below the total supply of sats (D6). The did:btco method does not yet define the network prefixes; this profile uses them pending that [S30]. A verifier MUST reject any other form, and an anchoring entry whose alias names a network other than the one its genesis entry names (P9.34). It MUST treat an inscription as on the sat a did:btco DID names only if the Bitcoin index it uses, on that network, reports the inscription on the sat with that number; the label under which inscriptions were supplied to it does not count. An inscription the index places on another sat, or a read from a different network, is not on that sat. Placement rests on the index: a verifier SHOULD use an index it runs itself or two independent indexes, MUST report which it used (P9.11), and MUST NOT accept an anchoring entry, a later inscription or a did:btco version whose placement two indexes it uses disagree on; it reports missing evidence until they agree. Anchoring MUST happen at most once and is terminal: every later entry MUST keep that did:btco alias, and a later entry that removes or changes it, or adds another did:btco alias, MUST be rejected. After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT add a did:webvh alias that the anchoring entry’s state does not list, including one that replaces an existing alias, and a verifier MUST reject it: the web is a layer before Bitcoin, and an Original does not move back to an earlier layer (D7). A did:webvh alias the anchoring entry’s state lists MAY stay or be removed, but once removed it MUST NOT be added again. An anchored Original is found through its did:btco alias, whose sat carries its complete log (P9.17).

P9.11 The [[ref: holder]] of an anchored Original is the current holder of its anchoring sat, as the Bitcoin adapter determines it under its confirmation policy (P9.5) at the tip the report is bound to (P9.31). An Original that is not anchored has no holder. Inscriptions of the same evidence on any other sat MUST NOT make anyone its holder. Holding an Original is not authority over it (P9.12), and until the anchor’s uniqueness is established the holder is provisional (P9.16). A verifier MUST report the holder separately from authorship, and MUST state the confirmation depth it relied on, the network and tip, and whether more than one independent Bitcoin index agreed on the sat’s location.

P9.12 After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT be reported as current unless it is signed by the author under P5.3, or by an editor under P5.16, and inscribed on the anchoring sat, extending the accepted head. This applies to every operation, including deactivation. An entry the author or an editor signs but that is not inscribed on the sat, and an inscription on the sat that neither signed, MUST NOT change the Original’s state. Holding the sat, or paying for or broadcasting an inscription on it, MUST NOT confer author or update authority, and transferring an Original MUST mean transferring the sat.

P9.13 A resolver MUST derive an anchored Original’s state by following, in chain order on the anchoring sat, the publications that extend the accepted head, not by taking the newest inscription. Each accepted publication MUST be later in chain order than the one before it, comparing the final inscription of each (P9.32); among publications that extend the same head, the earliest in chain order wins. Chain order includes the transaction and inscription index within a block, so a publication and the one that extends it can be in the same block, as when chained transactions are mined together or re-mined into one block after a reorganization (P9.5). Inscriptions that do not extend the head MUST NOT be accepted and SHOULD be reported; those waiting on an unverifiable inscription are labelled as P9.36 describes. For an anchored Original, Bitcoin is the log’s authoritative source, and chain order on the sat orders its entries; the author key checks of P9.15 still apply. `did:og` defines a Bitcoin `src` kind, whose value is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias, and how the log is read from it: from the Originals inscriptions on that sat, under P9.31 and P9.32, not over HTTPS.

P9.14 An inscription MAY carry an entry in a compact encoding, such as a delta against the previous entry, defined by a later version of this method; `og:1.0-draft.1` defines none (D6, D16). It MUST decode deterministically to the full JSON entry, and entry hashes and proofs MUST be computed over the decoded full entry. An inscription that does not decode, or decodes differently for different readers, MUST be rejected.

P9.15 Every entry inscribed on the anchoring sat is decided by the first row of this table, read in order, whose condition holds, using the terms P9.35 defines. The rules are stated for the author DID, the signer of an author entry; for an editor entry they apply to the editor DID instead: its named version, its state at the inscription and its signing key, except that rows 6 and 8 also need the author DID’s state at the inscription, because an editor entry is subject to the author DID’s deactivation (P5.18). The author DID and an editor DID may use different methods, and each is judged by the evidence its own method needs. Nothing else decides an inscribed entry: never versionTime, block timestamps, or a signer version that Bitcoin does not evidence.

A key removal or signer DID deactivation therefore refuses entries inscribed after Bitcoin evidences it and MUST NOT make non-current an accepted entry inscribed before it. A version of a did:vh or did:webvh signer that is not inscribed on the anchoring sat does not affect acceptance, whatever its versionTime, and inscribing it later does not change entries already accepted. Because every version a did:vh or did:webvh signer's entry depends on is on the anchoring sat, only an unread did:btco sat can make an inscription `unverifiable`: the sat of its signer, or, for an editor entry, the sat of a did:btco author DID, whose state an editor entry also depends on (row 6).

P9.35 These terms apply to P9.15 and P9.36. The *state of a signer DID at an inscription* is its latest version that Bitcoin evidences before that inscription: for a did:btco signer, the latest version preceding it in chain order on the DID's sat (P5.13); for a did:vh or did:webvh signer, the latest version in its [[ref: inscribed signer history]] through the publication that carries the entry (P9.33). A *pre-anchor entry* is an entry before the anchoring entry in the anchoring publication; it has claimed ordering (P7.6). A *post-anchor entry* is the anchoring entry or any entry inscribed after it. A did:btco signer's sat is *read in full* for an inscription when the evidence set (P7.17) holds that sat's complete inscription list at the report's tip (P9.31), which then includes every inscription before the candidate, any of which could remove the key (P5.13). Whether a sat is read in full depends only on the evidence set, never on what a verifier fetched at some other time.

P9.36 The accepted head advances only by publications whose entries are all `accepted` under P9.15 and that extend it, in chain order (P9.13). An unverifiable inscription does not make the identity evidence of the accepted state incomplete and does not stop that state being current, because the sat's holder chooses what is inscribed and has no authority over the Original (P9.12). It does not extend the head, and a later publication that extends the same head MUST NOT be accepted while an earlier one extending that head is `unverifiable`: chain order, not the evidence set, decides between them (P9.13). Such a later publication, and every publication extending an `unverifiable` inscription directly or through other waiting publications, is labelled `waiting` on it. The report MUST list each `unverifiable` inscription with the signer DID and version it names, and each `waiting` publication with the inscription it waits on, in its `bitcoin` details and as `inscription-unverifiable` and `publication-waiting` diagnostics (P14.25). In a larger evidence set that reads the missing sat in full, the inscription is decided under P9.15: if `accepted`, the head follows it and the publications waiting on it under P9.13; if `rejected`, the publications waiting on it are ignored and the next publication in chain order that extends that head is considered. A verifier therefore never accepts a later publication because evidence about an earlier one is missing.

P9.16 An Original has claimed ordering before its anchor (P7.6); the anchoring publication’s block is the first ordering evidence for that history, showing that every earlier entry existed no later than that block. A holder of an authorized author key can anchor a different branch of the same Original on another sat. The anchor is the first valid anchoring publication, including under P9.15 and P9.33, that is inscribed on the sat its anchoring entry names, in chain order (P5.13) on the network the genesis entry names (P9.34), under the adapter’s confirmation policy (P9.5). An anchoring publication that does not verify from Bitcoin evidence, including one whose entries name a signer version that Bitcoin does not evidence before them (P9.33), is not an anchor, whatever its position, and the first valid one after it is. A verifier that cannot yet decide an earlier anchoring publication, because a did:btco signer’s sat is not read in full (P9.35), MUST report `bitcoin` as `undetermined` (P7.18), and MUST NOT report a later anchoring publication as the anchor, until it can decide it. Anchoring settles any fork of the Original (P6.6): the anchor’s branch is the Original’s history. A verifier MUST report every anchoring publication it knows of for the Original. Any other than the anchor MUST be reported as a conflicting anchor; it and its descendants MUST NOT be reported as current and MUST NOT make anyone the Original’s holder. Bitcoin does not list inscriptions by `did:og`, so a verifier MUST report the anchor’s uniqueness as not established and the holder as `provisional` (P9.11, P7.18), unless it uses an index of Originals inscriptions, which finds every one by its metaprotocol (P9.31). A verifier that uses such an index, and trusts it to list every Originals inscription through a block, MAY report that no competing anchoring publication for the Original’s `did:og` exists through that block, and the anchor’s uniqueness as established through it, naming the index and the block; it MUST NOT report uniqueness beyond that block. An evidence set that holds a valid anchoring publication earlier in chain order than another gives the earlier one as the anchor and the other as a conflicting anchor, whatever a report over a smaller evidence set gave (P7.17). This is an accepted risk: a holder of a stolen author key who also holds a sat can anchor first, making the author’s later anchor a conflicting one, so an author who needs protection SHOULD anchor early.

P9.17 Each publication on the anchoring sat MUST carry complete entries in its metadata, as P14.12 encodes them, unless a compact encoding is defined under P9.14; none is defined yet (D6). The anchoring publication MUST carry the Original’s whole log, in order, from its genesis entry through the anchoring entry, which MUST be its last entry; every later publication MUST carry exactly one entry. Every publication’s body is set by the body mode its last entry signs, in the `body` member of its asset state (P14.5): `preview`, `full` or `none`. The entries before it in an anchoring publication carry no `body` and no preview (P9.30) and do not affect it. Whoever inscribes chooses nothing: the signer of the last entry has already chosen whether the full version becomes public and permanent. With `preview`, which an entry signs exactly when it names a preview (P9.30), the body MUST be the exact bytes of that preview, and its content type the preview’s `mediaType`; this is the default, so the sat’s newest accepted publication shows a preview of the Original’s current version. With `full`, the body MUST be the exact bytes of the resource version that its descriptor names, with that version’s `mediaType` as its content type. With `none`, the inscription MUST have no body and no content type. In a publication in parts, only the final part has a body (P9.32). A body is absent only when the inscription has no body at all, as P14.12 defines; a body of zero bytes is present, and is the full version only if that version’s `size` is 0. An Originals inscription MUST NOT name a delegate (P14.12), because a reader would show the delegate’s content in place of its body; this rule is provisional (P14.12). A verifier MUST check a body against the preview or descriptor it has to match (P4.3). It MUST reject a publication whose final inscription’s body or content type is not what its last entry’s `body` requires, by digest, size and content type, including a body for `none`, no body for `preview` or `full`, a content type with no body, the full version for `preview` and the preview for `full`; and a publication with an inscription that has a content encoding or names a delegate. Such a publication is rejected as a whole: its entries are not accepted from it and it is not an anchor, and the mismatch is not reported as a warning on an accepted state. The bytes of the version are not needed for the log to verify, and a verifier MUST NOT require them; a body that matches a preview verifies the preview only, never the version, whose availability is reported separately (P7.4, section 7). Versions not inscribed in full, including those before the anchoring entry’s and, by default, the current one, stay off Bitcoin (P9.18). The descriptors remain the content commitment of P9.4. The anchoring sat therefore carries the Original’s complete log, and a verifier needs no copy of it: a copy that differs from the entries on the sat is ignored. An anchoring publication whose entries do not verify as one log from its genesis, or whose last entry is not an anchoring entry naming that sat, is not an anchor. If a verifier cannot read the anchoring sat’s inscriptions in full, resolution MUST fail and the report MUST state missing evidence (P3.3). An inscription’s metadata and body together MUST NOT exceed 350,000 bytes (D6), which keeps each inscription within Bitcoin’s standard transaction size; a log or signer history too large for one inscription is published in parts (P9.32). The SDK MUST refuse to anchor an Original, or to publish an entry, whose publication would need more parts than P14.13 allows, and a verifier MUST reject a larger inscription or publication.

P9.18 Bitcoin alone verifies an anchored Original: its log (P9.17) and the identity of every signer of an entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, from the signer history inscribed on that sat for a did:vh or did:webvh signer (P9.33) and from the DID’s own sat for a did:btco signer (P5.13). No host, copy, signer DID host, witness or watcher is needed. The evidence this claim and the mainnet release gate depend on is exactly: the Originals inscriptions on the anchoring sat, and every inscription on the sat of each did:btco signer, read under the pinned ord rules (P9.31), on the network the genesis entry names (P9.34), at a tip the report states, with the confirmation depth it relied on (P9.5); and, only to report the anchor’s uniqueness as established, an index of Originals inscriptions that the verifier trusts (P9.16). The previews inscribed on the sat and the resource versions inscribed in full (P9.17, P9.30) are on Bitcoin too. Versions that were not inscribed in full, such as those before the anchoring entry’s and, by default, the current one, are content, not evidence, and are not needed for the log to verify: a reader without them MUST report the log as verified and the missing content as unavailable (P7.4), never as a verification failure. Before inscribing the anchoring publication, the SDK MUST verify the log through the signed anchoring entry, and the identity evidence of every signer of an entry in that log against the signer histories the publication will inscribe (P9.33), and MUST NOT inscribe otherwise. That log, like any log a writer checks before publishing an entry, is its own committed log, not yet on Bitcoin, and for an anchored Original only what is on Bitcoin is trusted (P9.13), so it cannot verify as published. A writer verifies it as a local copy: it verifies the copy whole, from genesis through the entry it is about to publish, and reports it as unverified on Bitcoin and never as current. A local-copy result is valid only for the writer’s own check before it publishes, and MUST NOT appear in a report given to anyone else. After submitting it, the SDK MUST label the result pending Bitcoin acceptance only while its Bitcoin evidence shows the publication’s transactions held in the mempool, or confirmed but not yet verified, and submitted, not observed while it does not, including after rejection or eviction or when its Bitcoin provider has no mempool view; it MUST NOT present a submitted inscription the network is not observed to hold as on its way to acceptance. The checks of P5.13, P9.15 and P9.16 depend on the publication’s position, so once it is confirmed the SDK verifies the Original and reports it as anchored, not anchored, or anchored with incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). Before inscribing any entry, it MUST tell the author the body mode that entry signs: the preview, the full version or no body (P9.17, P9.30); that this body, the log with its name and metadata, and the signer DID histories inscribed with it, become public and permanent on Bitcoin and cannot be removed by a later entry (P8.3); how many inscriptions the publication needs and what they will cost at the fee rate the SDK will use (P9.32); and, for active content such as HTML or SVG in the body, that Bitcoin explorers may render it outside this profile’s isolation rules (P9.7). It MUST tell the author what Bitcoin will not hold: the versions not inscribed in full, including the current one when the body is a preview or absent. This profile does not guarantee their replication or availability. When the anchoring sat is transferred, the sender’s SDK SHOULD give the recipient the versions not inscribed in full that it holds, and the recipient’s SDK SHOULD verify them.

### Unused rule numbers

Rule numbers P9.19–P9.29 are not used. They held rules for anchoring on an EVM chain, withdrawn when Bitcoin became the only anchor (D6), and are not reused.

### Previews

An inscription costs in proportion to its size, and creators pay for it. A preview lets an Original be anchored on Bitcoin for a small, predictable cost whatever the size of its resource: the inscription carries a small image or other file that stands for the version, signed in the entry, and the version itself stays off Bitcoin unless the author chooses to inscribe it.

P9.30 A preview stands for a resource version in an inscription (P9.17). An entry names it in its asset state’s `preview` member, beside the resource descriptor, by its media type, size and SHA-256 digest (P14.5), so that the preview is signed with the entry. A preview MUST be at least 1 and at most 8,192 bytes. A version’s `mediaType` is an `image/` type when its type, before the `/`, is `image`, compared ASCII case-insensitively (P14.14); this includes `image/svg+xml`, which the writer rasterises, so that a preview of an image is never active content (P9.7). For a version of an `image/` type, a writer MUST generate the preview from the version’s bytes as WebP, `image/webp`; how it scales and encodes the image, and whether a preview of an animated image is animated or one frame, is its own choice, and if it cannot decode the image, or cannot make a WebP preview within the limit, it MUST tell the author and name no preview. The author does not supply the preview of an image. For a version of any other media type, the author MAY supply a preview of any media type within that limit; with none, the entry names no preview. A writer MAY instead name no preview and sign `body` `full`, inscribing the full version within the 350,000-byte limit (P9.17); with no preview and the full version not chosen, the entry signs `body` `none`, and the inscription has no body, its entries in its metadata only. An entry that names a preview MUST sign `body` `preview`, and an entry that signs `preview` MUST name one (P14.5). Previews are made only when an entry is prepared for Bitcoin: an entry whose `aliases` list no did:btco DID, which includes every entry before an anchoring entry and so every entry of an Original for which no anchoring entry has been signed, MUST NOT carry `preview` or `body`, and a writer MUST NOT write either there; every entry whose `aliases` list a did:btco DID MUST carry `body`. The anchoring entry MAY name a preview. Every later entry that makes a new resource version, by the predicate of P4.4: one that changes the descriptor’s digest, or its `mediaType` alone, names a preview made for the new version, or none: a writer MUST make it from the new version’s bytes, as above, or take it from the author for a version that is not an `image/` type, and MUST NOT reuse its parent’s preview without doing so. A preview made that way MAY be byte-identical to its parent’s, as when two different images scale to the same WebP; it is valid, the entry names it with the new version’s descriptor, and its inscription shows the same preview bytes again (decided 21 November 2026, replacing the rule that a writer make another). That a writer made the preview from the new version binds writers only: a verifier cannot tell a regenerated preview from a copied one, and MUST NOT reject an entry because its preview equals its parent’s. Every later entry that makes no new version under P4.4, leaving both digest and `mediaType` unchanged, including a deactivation (P6.5), MUST carry its parent’s `preview` unchanged or carry none: its inscription shows the same preview again, or, with none, the full version or no body, as its `body` signs. An entry that carries none when its parent named one removes the preview for that version: no later entry names a preview until one makes a new version (P4.4). A writer MUST refuse to sign an entry that breaks these rules, and a verifier MUST reject an entry that breaks them, whose `preview` is empty or larger than 8,192 bytes, or that names a preview of a media type other than `image/webp` for a version whose `mediaType` is an `image/` type. A verifier cannot check that a preview depicts the version: a preview is its signer’s claim, and MUST NOT be reported as the version, or as evidence of the version’s bytes. A preview is not a resource version, does not advance the version (P4.4) and is not in the derived DID document (P14.9). For an editor entry, naming the preview of a new version is part of the change to the resource that the grant allows (P5.16); an editor entry that does not change the resource carries its parent’s `preview` and `body` unchanged, or no `preview` if its parent named none. Choosing `body` for a new version is part of the change to the resource too, so an editor whose grant allows the resource chooses whether that version is inscribed in full.

::: note Fees
Not normative. A writer may wait for a lower fee rate before inscribing, for example up to a ceiling the author sets. By default it inscribes at the current fee rate. Either way it tells the author the cost first (P9.18).

### Reading Bitcoin, publications and signer histories

P9.31 This profile reads Bitcoin as the ord release line 0.29.x indexes it [S31], with 0.29.1 as the reference release: which envelopes are inscriptions, which sat each inscription is on, and the order of a sat’s inscriptions are as that release line determines, and a later ord release applies only from a profile version that pins it. An [[ref: Originals inscription]] is an inscription whose metaprotocol field (ord tag 7) is exactly `og` (provisional, P14.12). Every other inscription, on any sat, is not an Originals inscription and is ignored for every Original, except as a did:btco version under P5.13. An Originals inscription MUST have only the fields that P14.12 lists; a verifier MUST reject one with any other field, including a pointer, a parent, a content encoding, a delegate or a field that ord does not recognise, and the publication it belongs to (P9.32). Every resolution and verification report is bound to one network, the one the Original’s genesis entry names (P9.34), and to one tip, the hash and height of the latest block the index had processed. A verifier MUST state that network and tip in its report, MUST read every inscription it relies on at that one tip, and MUST NOT combine inscriptions, sat locations or holders read at different tips. A verifier that uses more than one index (P9.10) MUST read them at the same tip, and they MUST report the same complete list of inscriptions on the anchoring sat, and on each did:btco signer’s sat, in the same order, and the same holder; otherwise it reports missing evidence until they agree.

P9.32 A [[ref: publication]] is one or more Originals inscriptions on the anchoring sat that together carry one step of the Original’s log: the anchoring publication carries the log from genesis through the anchoring entry, and each later publication exactly one entry (P9.17). A publication in one inscription has no `part` member in its metadata. A publication in parts has from 2 inscriptions to the limit of P14.13, and each part’s metadata names its position and the number of parts, as `part` (P14.12). Its parts MUST be consecutive Originals inscriptions on the anchoring sat in chain order, part 1 first; inscriptions on the sat that are not Originals inscriptions (P9.31) do not interrupt them. An Originals inscription on the sat that is not the next part, including the first inscription of another publication, breaks the publication: its parts so far are rejected, and the inscription that broke it is considered on its own. The publication’s entries are the entries of its parts in order, and its inscribed signer histories those of its parts in order (P9.33). Its last entry MUST be in its final part, and only its final part has a body (P9.17): every other part MUST have no body and no content type. A publication takes effect only when its final part is confirmed (P9.5), and its position in chain order is its final part’s: until then none of its parts changes the Original’s state or its holder, and a publication whose final part never appears changes nothing. Its entries and signer histories are checked as one publication, and if any check fails the whole publication is rejected. A writer splits a publication only when one inscription would exceed 350,000 bytes (P9.17), and tells the author before inscribing how many inscriptions it needs (P9.18).

P9.33 Every signer DID version that an entry inscribed on the anchoring sat names, or on which its check depends (P9.15), MUST be evidenced on Bitcoin before that entry’s inscription: for a did:btco signer by the DID’s own sat (P5.13), and for a did:vh or did:webvh signer by that DID’s log entries inscribed on the anchoring sat, in the `signers` member of Originals inscriptions (P14.12), with the witness proofs its method requires for them. This [[ref: inscribed signer history]] is built in chain order from accepted publications: the first accepted publication that carries entries of a DID MUST carry them from that DID’s genesis entry, and each later publication that carries more MUST continue exactly from the last entry already inscribed for it, with no gap and no repeated entry. A publication’s own signer history counts for its entries and is placed before them, so an entry may name a version inscribed in the same publication. Signer history in a publication that is not accepted is ignored, and a later publication carries it again. A verifier MUST verify each inscribed signer history under its DID’s method from that DID’s genesis entry, including, for a did:webvh, the moves that P5.14 accepts, and MUST reject a publication whose signer history does not verify, does not continue the history already inscribed, is for a DID of another method, or would exceed the limit of P14.13. Inscribed signer history is evidence of versions only and gives no authority. A version that the DID’s controller publishes elsewhere, including a different version at a number already inscribed, does not change it (P9.15). A writer MUST include, in each publication or an earlier accepted one, each did:vh or did:webvh signer’s history through the version that every entry in it names. It SHOULD include that history through the newest version of the DID it has verified, and MUST NOT inscribe an entry whose key that newest version no longer lists, so that a key removal already published binds the publication. Only the holder inscribes, so an author or editor who needs a key removal to bind without the holder’s help uses a did:btco DID, whose versions are on its own sat (P5.13).

P9.34 The genesis entry’s asset state names the Bitcoin network on which the Original can be anchored, in its `network` member (P14.5), and every later entry MUST carry it unchanged. An anchoring entry’s did:btco alias MUST name a sat on that network by its prefix (P9.10), and a verifier MUST reject one that does not. Chain order is compared only between inscriptions on that network, and inscriptions on any other network are not evidence about the Original (P9.31). A sat holds at most one Original: the first anchoring publication on a sat that is valid as a publication, under P9.15, P9.17, P9.32 and P9.33, takes the sat, whether or not it is its Original’s anchor under P9.16. Every later Originals inscription on that sat whose entries are for another `did:og` MUST be rejected, including a later anchoring publication, and a writer MUST refuse to anchor on a sat it knows is taken. A did:btco alias therefore resolves to the Original that took its sat, or to none (P3.9).
