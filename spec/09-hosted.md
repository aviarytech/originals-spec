## Hosted publication and Bitcoin boundaries

### Hosted publication

The SDK hosted path publishes two logs: a did:webvh method log (`did.jsonl`) signed separately with Ed25519, and the asset’s own log (`cel.json`). This profile keeps two logs with different roles: the Original’s history is only in its `did:og` asset log, and the did:webvh log is a small web alias that points to the `did:og` and locates its files through `#files` (section 3). A host serves the selected VH profile and exact resource evidence; the public reader verifies the same checkpoint semantics as the local reader.

### Proposed hosted rules

P9.1 A published snapshot MUST bind its asset state, history checkpoint and resource descriptor. The public reader MUST verify that exact snapshot and MUST NOT combine history from one checkpoint with metadata or bytes presented as another.

P9.2 A publish retry MUST resume or repeat the same intended snapshot. Uploading bytes, publishing a locator or changing an index MUST NOT create a new asset transition unless an explicit authorized transition is part of the operation.

P9.3 Host relocation and locator updates MUST preserve authenticated continuity under the chosen profile. Public delivery MUST use the new profile discriminator and MUST NOT relabel evidence in another format as VH.

### Bitcoin adapter

The current inscription encodings are replaced, not retained. P9.17 defines the representation: each inscription’s body is, by default, a preview of the resource version its last entry names, signed in that entry (P9.30), or else that version itself or no body, and its metadata carries the log, from genesis through the anchoring entry in the anchoring inscription and one entry in each later one. An inscription counts at one confirmation on every network (P9.5), and its metadata and body together are at most 350,000 bytes (P9.17); any compact encoding (P9.14) is open (D6).

P9.4 A Bitcoin publication MUST identify the exact accepted checkpoint and content commitment it represents. Only a verified change beyond the prior durable published boundary may form the next delta. A pending or failed transaction MUST NOT advance that durable boundary.

P9.5 Forks, reorganizations and competing publications MUST be exposed explicitly. Confirmation policy and reorganization handling MUST be defined by the adapter; transaction observation alone MUST NOT be described as permanent finality. In the baseline profile an inscription counts once it has one confirmation, on every network (D6); an unconfirmed inscription is reported as observed, never as part of the accepted log, and every report MUST state the confirmation depth of what it relied on. A reorganization that removes an accepted inscription MUST be reported with VH-Log’s `authoritative-source-changed` warning [S20], not silently. Counting at one confirmation does not permit discarding the prepared transactions a retry needs (P9.6).

P9.6 Transaction retries MUST distinguish rebroadcasting the same intended transaction from preparing a replacement. Asset edits MUST NOT be silently re-signed or rebased because a transaction is retried. A wallet, the holder of an anchoring sat, or whoever pays for or broadcasts an inscription MUST NOT acquire author or VH update authority by that role; for a did:btco author DID, possession of the DID’s sat is control of the author DID (P5.13).

P9.7 A host MUST serve resource bytes with their signed mediaType and MUST NOT let a valid signature override content-safety policy. Active content such as HTML or SVG MUST be served from an origin without user credentials, with content-type sniffing disabled and a restrictive content security policy, or rendered only in a sandbox. Any SDK consumer that renders resources or asset state strings, including desktop and mobile clients, MUST apply the same isolation and MUST escape names and metadata.

P9.8 An Original’s asset log MUST NOT use VH witnesses, whether or not it is hosted, and hosted publication requires none. Off Bitcoin an Original is author-signed with claimed ordering only (P7.6); ordering on the anchoring sat and a provisional owner come from anchoring (P9.10–P9.16); anchoring does not establish that no other sat carries a competing anchor (P9.16). An author DID MAY use its own DID method’s witnesses or watchers, and this profile does not change them; the author DID of an Original SHOULD use them where its method has them, because P7.8 and P9.15 depend on its history.

P9.9 An asset log entry that sets the VH witness parameter to anything other than an empty object MUST be rejected (P9.8).

### Anchoring and ownership

An Original moves up through layers one way only: from local to web, and from either to Bitcoin. Bitcoin is the last layer. The SDK requires web publication before anchoring; this profile does not (P9.10). Once an Original is anchored to a sat, its did:btco alias names that sat, and every later change is a reinscription on it.

An Original has one author for its whole life. Only the author anchors it, and after anchoring the author or a granted editor signs each change (P5.16). Paying for or broadcasting an inscription is not signing it, and gives no authority. Ownership is separate: the owner is whoever holds the anchoring sat. On Bitcoin a change needs both: an authorized signature, and an inscription on the sat, which only its holder can make. After a sale the Original is frozen unless an authorized signer and the new owner cooperate. Selling or giving an Original means moving the sat, and needs nothing from the author.

P9.10 An Original MAY be anchored from the local layer or from the web layer. The author anchors it with an ordinary update entry (section 6) whose asset state adds the did:btco alias of the anchoring sat (section 3), inscribed on that sat. The first entry whose asset state lists a did:btco alias is the [[ref: anchoring entry]], and an entry inscribed on a sat whose alias its state does not list is not one. P9.16 decides which anchoring entry is the anchor. A did:btco DID in this profile has the form `did:btco:` followed by an optional network prefix, `sig:` for signet, `test:` for testnet or `reg:` for regtest, with no prefix meaning mainnet, and then the sat number in decimal with no sign and no leading zeros, below the total supply of sats (D6). The did:btco method does not yet define the network prefixes; this profile uses them pending that [S30]. A verifier MUST reject any other form. It MUST treat an inscription as on the sat a did:btco DID names only if the Bitcoin index it uses, on that network, reports the inscription on the sat with that number; the label under which inscriptions were supplied to it does not count. An inscription the index places on another sat, or a read from a different network, is not on that sat. Placement rests on the index: a verifier SHOULD use an index it runs itself or two independent indexes, MUST report which it used (P9.11), and MUST NOT accept an anchoring entry, a later inscription or a did:btco version whose placement two indexes it uses disagree on; it reports missing evidence until they agree. Anchoring MUST happen at most once and is terminal: every later entry MUST keep that did:btco alias, and a later entry that removes or changes it, or adds another did:btco alias, MUST be rejected. After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT add a did:webvh alias that the anchoring entry’s state does not list, including one that replaces an existing alias, and a verifier MUST reject it: the web is a layer before Bitcoin, and an Original does not move back to an earlier layer (D7). A did:webvh alias the anchoring entry’s state lists MAY stay or be removed, but once removed it MUST NOT be added again. An anchored Original is found through its did:btco alias, whose sat carries its complete log (P9.17).

P9.11 The owner of an anchored Original is the current holder of its anchoring sat, as determined by the Bitcoin adapter under its confirmation policy (P9.5). An Original that is not anchored has no owner. Inscriptions of the same evidence on any other sat MUST NOT confer ownership. A verifier MUST report ownership separately from authorship, and MUST state the confirmation depth it relied on and whether more than one independent Bitcoin index agreed on the sat’s location.

P9.12 After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT be reported as current unless it is signed by the author under P5.3, or by an editor under P5.16, and inscribed on the anchoring sat, extending the accepted head. This applies to every operation, including deactivation. An entry the author or an editor signs but that is not inscribed on the sat, and an inscription on the sat that neither signed, MUST NOT change the Original’s state. Holding the sat, or paying for or broadcasting an inscription on it, MUST NOT confer author or VH update authority, and transferring an Original MUST mean transferring the sat.

P9.13 A resolver MUST derive an anchored Original’s state by following, in chain order on the anchoring sat, the publications that extend the accepted head, not by taking the newest inscription. Each accepted publication MUST be at a strictly greater block height than the one before it; among publications that extend the same head, the earliest in chain order wins. Because at most one publication per block is accepted, chain order within a block only decides between candidates in that block. Inscriptions that do not extend the head MUST NOT be accepted and SHOULD be reported; those waiting on an unverifiable inscription are listed as P9.15 describes. For an anchored Original, Bitcoin is the log’s authoritative source under VH-Log’s rule for one [S20], and chain order on the sat orders its entries; the author key checks of P9.15 still apply. `did:og` defines a Bitcoin `src` kind, whose value is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias, and how the log is read from it under VH-Log’s rule for locations not reached over HTTPS [S23].

P9.14 An inscription MAY carry an entry in a compact encoding, such as a delta against the previous entry, defined by the extension under VH-Log’s rule for alternative encodings [S24]. It MUST decode deterministically to the full JSON entry, and entry hashes and proofs MUST be computed over the decoded full entry. An inscription that does not decode, or decodes differently for different readers, MUST be rejected.

P9.15 The entries before the anchoring entry, carried in the anchoring inscription, have claimed ordering (P7.6): each is checked against the signer DID version it names (P5.3), and a key’s removal after it was signed does not invalidate it, though the anchoring entry itself must be signed by a key valid at its inscription time. A verifier MUST check each entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, from the anchoring entry on, against its signer’s DID state at its inscription time, not its latest state. The rules below are stated for the author DID, which is the signer of an author entry. For an editor entry, they apply to the editor DID instead: its named version, its state at the inscription time and its signing key. Separately, an editor entry MUST be rejected if the author DID’s state at its inscription time is deactivated (P5.18). An inscription’s inscription time is the greatest block timestamp among the block containing it and that block’s ancestors, so it never decreases along the chain. The state at an inscription is the later of the author DID version the entry names and the latest author DID version whose versionTime is no later than the inscription time. The signing key MUST meet P5.3 both at the named version and in that state, and an entry inscribed when that state is deactivated MUST be rejected. A key removal or author DID deactivation therefore refuses entries whose inscription time is at or after its versionTime, unless, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, the entry names a later version that lists the key again (see below), and MUST NOT make non-current an accepted entry whose inscription time precedes it. Miners set block timestamps within consensus bounds, so inscription time can lag real time and an entry inscribed shortly after a removal can still be accepted. For a did:vh or did:webvh signer, a verifier uses the newest version of its DID that it knows of (P7.8); if that version is older than the inscription time, it checks the entry against that version and reports which version it relied on, rather than reporting incomplete identity evidence. For an entry in the anchoring inscription, a verifier that has no verified version at or after the one the entry names reports incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). For any entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, in the anchoring inscription or after it, a verifier that holds the signer’s DID history through a version numbered at or above the one the entry names, but not under the versionId it names, MUST reject the entry; for a did:btco signer whose sat it has read in full, it rejects one naming a version that is not in that history (P5.13). For an inscription after the anchoring inscription, a verifier that cannot otherwise verify the signer’s named version MUST report the inscription as unverifiable; an inscription that fails any check not depending on the unverified version is rejected, not unverifiable. An unverifiable inscription does not make the identity evidence of the accepted state incomplete and does not stop that state being current, because the sat’s holder chooses what is inscribed and has no authority over the Original (P9.12). For a did:btco signer, an inscription after the anchoring inscription is also unverifiable when the named version is known but the verifier has not read every inscription on that DID’s sat before the candidate, because one of them could remove the key: the candidate is unverifiable until it has, and MUST NOT be accepted against an older known state. If such a gap is found after an inscription was accepted, that inscription becomes unverifiable and the verifier recomputes the head under the rule below, reporting the change; an entry in the anchoring inscription with such a gap has incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). The report MUST list each unverifiable inscription with the signer DID and version it names. An unverifiable inscription does not extend the accepted head until it is verified, and a later publication that extends the same head MUST NOT be accepted while an earlier inscription extending that head is unverifiable: chain order, not the verifier’s evidence, decides between them (P9.13). The verifier reports such a publication as waiting on the unverifiable inscription. When it can verify that inscription, it accepts it if valid, or otherwise rejects it and considers the next publication in chain order that extends that head; either way it MUST report the resulting change of head and MUST NOT change its result silently. Publications that extend an unverifiable inscription, directly or through other waiting publications, are listed as waiting on it; once it is accepted the verifier follows them under P9.13, and if it is rejected they are ignored. A verifier therefore never accepts a later publication because evidence about an earlier one is missing; its result can still change when an unverifiable inscription is resolved, or through a reorganization (P9.5), a retroactive revocation conflict, a did:btco gap found later, or an earlier anchor (P9.16), and each change is reported. An unverifiable inscription can leave an Original waiting: whoever holds the anchoring sat can inscribe an entry naming a did:vh or did:webvh signer version that does not exist, and no later publication from that head is accepted, even after the sat is transferred, until the named signer’s DID reaches that version number under another versionId, after which the entry is rejected. A verifier MUST reject an entry naming a did:vh or did:webvh version numbered above the limit of P14.13, which this profile never verifies (P7.7), or later than a deactivated version of that DID it holds, which cannot exist. The wait is permanent if the named signer never updates its DID that far. This profile accepts that risk; a did:btco signer cannot be named this way, because a verifier that reads its sat in full rejects a version not in it. For a did:btco author DID, the state is selected by chain order instead, and an entry naming a version that does not precede its inscription is rejected (P5.13). For a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, versionTime is claimed by the DID’s controller, so the report MUST state whether the author DID’s own witnesses or watchers support it. That controller can publish a version whose versionTime precedes inscriptions already accepted, though not its own previous version; a verifier that retained an earlier acceptance and then finds such a version MUST report a retroactive revocation conflict rather than silently change its result. Because the later version is used, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID a version that the entry names can also re-authorize a key that the version in effect at the inscription time had removed; only the author DID’s controller can arrange this. Block timestamps can lag real time, which is why a named version with a later versionTime is accepted for these methods; for did:btco, chain order leaves no such lag and P5.13 applies.

P9.16 An Original has claimed ordering before its anchor (P7.6); the anchoring inscription’s block is the first ordering evidence for that history, showing that every earlier entry existed no later than that block. A holder of an authorized author key can anchor a different branch of the same Original on another sat. The anchor is the first valid anchoring entry, including under P9.15, that is inscribed on the sat it names, in chain order (P5.13) under the adapter’s confirmation policy (P9.5). Anchoring settles any fork of the Original (P6.6): the anchor’s branch is the Original’s history. A verifier MUST report every anchoring entry it knows of for the Original. Any other than the anchor MUST be reported as a conflicting anchor; it and its descendants MUST NOT be reported as current and MUST NOT confer ownership. No index lists every sat, so a verifier MUST report the anchor’s uniqueness as not established and the ownership it reports as provisional (P9.11). If a verifier later learns of a valid anchoring entry earlier in chain order than the one it reported as the anchor, it MUST report the earlier one as the anchor and the previous one as a conflicting anchor, together with the change in owner, and MUST NOT change its result silently. This is an accepted risk: a holder of a stolen author key who also holds a sat can anchor first, making the author’s later anchor a conflicting one, so an author who needs protection SHOULD anchor early.

P9.17 Each inscription on the anchoring sat that carries Originals entries MUST carry complete entries in its metadata, as P14.12 encodes them, unless a compact encoding is defined under P9.14; none is defined yet (D6). The anchoring inscription MUST carry the Original’s whole log, in order, from its genesis entry through the anchoring entry, which MUST be its last entry; every later inscription MUST carry exactly one entry. Every inscription’s body is set by its last entry; the entries before it in an anchoring inscription name no preview (P9.30) and do not affect it. If that entry names a preview (P9.30), the body MUST be the exact bytes of that preview, and its content type the preview’s `mediaType`; this is the default, so the sat’s newest accepted inscription shows a preview of the Original’s current version. If the entry names no preview, the body MUST be either the exact bytes of the resource version that its descriptor names, with that version’s `mediaType` as its content type, or absent, with no content type. A body is absent only when the inscription has no body at all, as P14.12 defines; a body of zero bytes is present, and is the full version only if that version’s `size` is 0. An Originals inscription MUST NOT name a delegate (P14.12), because a reader would show the delegate’s content in place of its body; this rule is provisional (P14.12). A verifier MUST check a body against the preview or descriptor it has to match (P4.3). It MUST reject an inscription whose last entry names a preview and whose body is anything other than that preview, by digest, size and content type, including the full version or no body; an inscription whose last entry names no preview and whose body’s digest, size or content type differs from the version’s descriptor, or that has a content type and no body; an inscription that has a content encoding; and an inscription that names a delegate. Such an inscription is rejected as a whole, as an inscription whose body did not match the version was before previews: its entries are not accepted from it and it is not an anchor, and the mismatch is not reported as a warning on an accepted state. The bytes of the version are not needed for the log to verify, and a verifier MUST NOT require them; a body that matches a preview verifies the preview only, never the version, whose availability is reported separately (P7.4, section 7). Versions not inscribed in full, including those before the anchoring entry’s and, by default, the current one, stay off Bitcoin (P9.18). The descriptors remain the content commitment of P9.4. The anchoring sat therefore carries the Original’s complete log [S20], and a verifier needs no copy of it: a copy that differs from the entries on the sat is ignored. An anchoring inscription whose entries do not verify as one log from its genesis, or whose last entry is not an anchoring entry naming that sat, is not an anchor. If a verifier cannot read the anchoring sat’s inscriptions in full, resolution MUST fail and the report MUST state missing evidence (P3.3). An inscription’s metadata and body together MUST NOT exceed 350,000 bytes (D6), which keeps a full anchoring inscription within Bitcoin’s standard transaction size. The SDK MUST refuse to anchor an Original whose log through the anchoring entry and the body it would inscribe together exceed it, or to publish an entry whose inscription would, and a verifier MUST reject a larger inscription.

P9.18 Bitcoin alone verifies an anchored Original’s log (P9.17) and, for a signer whose DID is a did:btco, that signer’s identity (P5.13): no host or copy is needed. The previews inscribed on the sat and the resource versions inscribed in full (P9.17, P9.30) are on Bitcoin too. Two things are not. Versions that were not inscribed in full, such as those before the anchoring entry’s and, by default, the current one, are not needed for the log to verify: a reader without them MUST report the log as verified and the missing content as unavailable (P7.4), never as a verification failure. The DID history of a did:vh or did:webvh signer is needed: without it identity evidence is incomplete (P5.6) and its freshness cannot come from Bitcoin; an inscription after the anchor that the signer signs is unverifiable and leaves later publications from that head waiting (P9.15). An author who wants Bitcoin to be all a reader needs SHOULD use a did:btco author DID and grant only editors whose DIDs are did:btco; anchoring does not require it (P5.15). Before inscribing the anchoring entry, the SDK MUST verify the log through the signed anchoring entry and the identity evidence of every signer of an entry in that log and MUST NOT inscribe otherwise. That log, like any log a writer checks before publishing an entry, is its own committed log, not yet on Bitcoin, and for an anchored Original only what is on Bitcoin is trusted (P9.13), so it cannot verify as published. A writer verifies it as a local copy: it verifies the copy whole, from genesis through the entry it is about to publish, and reports it as unverified on Bitcoin and never as current. A local-copy result is valid only for the writer’s own check before it publishes, and MUST NOT appear in a report given to anyone else. After submitting it, the SDK MUST label the result pending Bitcoin acceptance only while its Bitcoin evidence shows the inscription’s transactions held in the mempool, or confirmed but not yet verified, and submitted, not observed while it does not, including after rejection or eviction or when its Bitcoin provider has no mempool view; it MUST NOT present a submitted inscription the network is not observed to hold as on its way to acceptance. The checks of P5.13, P9.15 and P9.16 depend on the inscription’s position, so once it is confirmed the SDK verifies the Original and reports it as anchored, not anchored, or anchored with incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). Before inscribing any entry, it MUST tell the author whether the inscription carries the preview, the full version or no body (P9.30); that this body, and the log with its name and metadata, become public and permanent on Bitcoin and cannot be removed by a later entry (P8.3); what the inscription will cost at the fee rate the SDK will use; and, for active content such as HTML or SVG in the body, that Bitcoin explorers may render it outside this profile’s isolation rules (P9.7). It MUST tell the author what Bitcoin will not hold: the versions not inscribed in full, including the current one when the body is a preview or absent, and any did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories. This profile does not guarantee their replication or availability. When the anchoring sat is transferred, the sender’s SDK SHOULD give the recipient the versions not inscribed in full and off-chain signer DID histories it holds, and the recipient’s SDK SHOULD verify them.

### Unused rule numbers

Rule numbers P9.19–P9.29 are not used. They held rules for anchoring on an EVM chain, withdrawn when Bitcoin became the only anchor (D6), and are not reused.

### Previews

An inscription costs in proportion to its size, and creators pay for it. A preview lets an Original be anchored on Bitcoin for a small, predictable cost whatever the size of its resource: the inscription carries a small image or other file that stands for the version, signed in the entry, and the version itself stays off Bitcoin unless the author chooses to inscribe it.

P9.30 A preview stands for a resource version in an inscription (P9.17). An entry names it in its asset state’s `preview` member, beside the resource descriptor, by its media type, size and SHA-256 digest (P14.5), so that the preview is signed with the entry. A preview MUST be at least 1 and at most 8,192 bytes. A version’s `mediaType` is an `image/` type when its type, before the `/`, is `image`, compared ASCII case-insensitively (P14.14); this includes `image/svg+xml`, which the writer rasterises, so that a preview of an image is never active content (P9.7). For a version of an `image/` type, a writer MUST generate the preview from the version’s bytes as WebP, `image/webp`; how it scales and encodes the image, and whether a preview of an animated image is animated or one frame, is its own choice, and if it cannot decode the image, or cannot make a WebP preview within the limit, it MUST tell the author and name no preview. The author does not supply the preview of an image. For a version of any other media type, the author MAY supply a preview of any media type within that limit; with none, the entry names no preview. A writer MAY instead name no preview and inscribe the full version, within the 350,000-byte limit (P9.17); with no preview and the full version not chosen, the inscription has no body, and its entries are in its metadata only. Previews are made only when an entry is prepared for Bitcoin: an entry whose `aliases` list no did:btco DID, which includes every entry before an anchoring entry and so every entry of an Original for which no anchoring entry has been signed, MUST NOT carry `preview`, and a writer MUST NOT write one there. The anchoring entry MAY name a preview. Every later entry that changes the resource’s digest MUST NOT carry its parent’s `preview` unchanged: it names a preview made for the new version, or none. If the preview a writer makes for the new version is byte-identical to its parent’s, it makes another, for example at another size or quality, or names none. Every later entry that does not change the digest, including a deactivation (P6.5), MUST carry its parent’s `preview` unchanged or carry none: its inscription shows the same preview again, or, with none, the full version or no body. An entry that carries none when its parent named one removes the preview for that version: no later entry names a preview until one changes the digest. A writer MUST refuse to sign an entry that breaks these rules, and a verifier MUST reject an entry that breaks them, whose `preview` is empty or larger than 8,192 bytes, or that names a preview of a media type other than `image/webp` for a version whose `mediaType` is an `image/` type. A verifier cannot check that a preview depicts the version: a preview is its signer’s claim, and MUST NOT be reported as the version, or as evidence of the version’s bytes. A preview is not a resource version, does not advance the version (P4.4) and is not in the derived DID document (P14.9). For an editor entry, naming the preview of a new version is part of the change to the resource that the grant allows (P5.16); an editor entry that does not change the resource carries its parent’s `preview` unchanged, or none if its parent named none.

Not normative. A writer may wait for a lower fee rate before inscribing, for example up to a ceiling the author sets. By default it inscribes at the current fee rate. Either way it tells the author the cost first (P9.18).
