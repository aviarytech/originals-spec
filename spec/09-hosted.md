## Hosted publication and Bitcoin boundaries

### Hosted publication

The SDK hosted path publishes two logs: a did:webvh method log (`did.jsonl`) signed separately with Ed25519, and the asset’s own log (`cel.json`). This draft keeps two logs with different roles: the Original’s history is only in its did:vh asset log, and the did:webvh log is a small web alias that points to the did:vh and locates its files through `#files` (section 3). A host serves the selected VH profile and exact resource evidence; the public reader verifies the same checkpoint semantics as the local reader.

The Boop adapter needs its actual publishing path replaced: ordinary publication uses local per-asset Ed25519 signing rather than SDK publishToWeb. Replacing only publishToWeb leaves that signing path in place. The exact-snapshot public-reader requirement from Boop issue 232 [S10] remains a regression requirement.

### Proposed hosted rules

P9.1 A published snapshot MUST bind its asset state, history checkpoint and resource descriptor. The public reader MUST verify that exact snapshot and MUST NOT combine history from one checkpoint with metadata or bytes presented as another.

P9.2 A publish retry MUST resume or repeat the same intended snapshot. Uploading bytes, publishing a locator or changing an index MUST NOT create a new asset transition unless an explicit authorized transition is part of the operation.

P9.3 Host relocation and locator updates MUST preserve authenticated continuity under the chosen profile. Public delivery MUST use the new profile discriminator and MUST NOT relabel evidence in another format as VH.

### Bitcoin adapter

The current inscription encodings are replaced, not retained. P9.17 defines the representation: each inscription’s body is the Original’s resource at the version its last entry names, under that version’s media type, and its metadata carries the log, from genesis through the anchoring entry in the anchoring inscription and one entry in each later one. An inscription counts at one confirmation on every network (P9.5), and its metadata and body together are at most 350,000 bytes (P9.17); any compact encoding (P9.14) is open (D6).

P9.4 A Bitcoin publication MUST identify the exact accepted checkpoint and content commitment it represents. Only a verified change beyond the prior durable published boundary may form the next delta. A pending or failed transaction MUST NOT advance that durable boundary.

P9.5 Forks, reorganizations and competing publications MUST be exposed explicitly. Confirmation policy and reorganization handling MUST be defined by the adapter; transaction observation alone MUST NOT be described as permanent finality. In the baseline profile an inscription counts once it has one confirmation, on every network (D6); an unconfirmed inscription is reported as observed, never as part of the accepted log, and every report MUST state the confirmation depth of what it relied on. A reorganization that removes an accepted inscription MUST be reported with VH-Log’s `authoritative-source-changed` warning [S20], not silently. Counting at one confirmation does not permit discarding the prepared transactions a retry needs (P9.6).

P9.6 Transaction retries MUST distinguish rebroadcasting the same intended transaction from preparing a replacement. Asset edits MUST NOT be silently re-signed or rebased because a transaction is retried. A wallet or the holder of an anchoring sat MUST NOT acquire author or VH update authority by possession alone; for a did:btco author DID, possession of the DID’s sat is control of the author DID (P5.13).

P9.7 A host MUST serve resource bytes with their signed mediaType and MUST NOT let a valid signature override content-safety policy. Active content such as HTML or SVG MUST be served from an origin without user credentials, with content-type sniffing disabled and a restrictive content security policy, or rendered only in a sandbox. Any SDK consumer that renders resources or asset state strings, including desktop and mobile clients, MUST apply the same isolation and MUST escape names and metadata.

P9.8 An Original’s asset log MUST NOT use VH witnesses, whether or not it is hosted, and hosted publication requires none. Off Bitcoin an Original is author-signed with claimed ordering only (P7.6); ordering on the anchoring sat and a provisional owner come from anchoring (P9.10–P9.16); anchoring does not establish that no other sat carries a competing anchor (P9.16). An author DID MAY use its own DID method’s witnesses or watchers, and this profile does not change them; the author DID of an Original SHOULD use them where its method has them, because P7.8 and P9.15 depend on its history.

P9.9 An asset log entry that sets the VH witness parameter to anything other than an empty object MUST be rejected (P9.8).

### Anchoring and ownership

An Original moves up through layers one way only: from local to web, and from either to Bitcoin. Bitcoin is the last layer. An Original whose genesis entry names a listed EVM chain as its anchor chain is anchored on that chain first, on a token rather than a sat, and can later move up to a chain in a higher layer or to Bitcoin (P9.19–P9.29, proposed). The SDK requires web publication before anchoring; this draft does not (P9.10). Once an Original is anchored to a sat, its did:btco alias names that sat, and every later change is a reinscription on it.

An Original has one author for its whole life. Only the author anchors it, and after anchoring the author or an editor the author has granted signs each change (P5.16). Ownership is separate: the owner is whoever holds the anchoring sat. On Bitcoin a change needs both: an authorized signature, and an inscription on the sat, which only its holder can make. After a sale the Original is frozen unless an authorized signer and the new owner cooperate. Selling or giving an Original means moving the sat, and needs nothing from the author.

P9.10 An Original MAY be anchored from the local layer or from the web layer. The author anchors it with an ordinary update entry (section 6) whose asset state adds the did:btco alias of the anchoring sat (section 3), inscribed on that sat. The first entry whose asset state lists a did:btco alias is the anchoring entry, and an entry inscribed on a sat whose alias its state does not list is not one. P9.16 decides which anchoring entry is the anchor. A did:btco DID in this profile has the form `did:btco:` followed by an optional network prefix, `sig:` for signet, `test:` for testnet or `reg:` for regtest, with no prefix meaning mainnet, and then the sat number in decimal with no sign and no leading zeros, below the total supply of sats (D6). The did:btco method does not yet define the network prefixes; this profile uses them pending that [S30]. A verifier MUST reject any other form. It MUST treat an inscription as on the sat a did:btco DID names only if the Bitcoin index it uses, on that network, reports the inscription on the sat with that number; the label under which inscriptions were supplied to it does not count. An inscription the index places on another sat, or a read from a different network, is not on that sat. Placement rests on the index: a verifier SHOULD use an index it runs itself or two independent indexes, MUST report which it used (P9.11), and MUST NOT accept an anchoring entry, a later inscription or a did:btco version whose placement two indexes it uses disagree on; it reports missing evidence until they agree. Anchoring MUST happen at most once and is terminal: every later entry MUST keep that did:btco alias, and a later entry that removes or changes it, or adds another did:btco alias, MUST be rejected. After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT add a did:webvh alias that the anchoring entry’s state does not list, including one that replaces an existing alias, and a verifier MUST reject it: the web is a layer before Bitcoin, and an Original does not move back to an earlier layer (D7). A did:webvh alias the anchoring entry’s state lists MAY stay or be removed, but once removed it MUST NOT be added again. An anchored Original is found through its did:btco alias, whose sat carries its complete log (P9.17).

P9.11 The owner of an anchored Original is the current holder of its anchoring sat, as determined by the Bitcoin adapter under its confirmation policy (P9.5). An Original that is not anchored has no owner. Inscriptions of the same evidence on any other sat MUST NOT confer ownership. A verifier MUST report ownership separately from authorship, and MUST state the confirmation depth it relied on and whether more than one independent Bitcoin index agreed on the sat’s location.

P9.12 After anchoring, an entry MUST NOT be reported as current unless it is signed by the author under P5.3, or by an editor under P5.16, and inscribed on the anchoring sat, extending the accepted head. This applies to every operation, including deactivation. An entry the author or an editor signs but that is not inscribed on the sat, and an inscription on the sat that neither signed, MUST NOT change the Original’s state. Holding the sat MUST NOT confer author or VH update authority, and transferring an Original MUST mean transferring the sat.

P9.13 A resolver MUST derive an anchored Original’s state by following, in chain order on the anchoring sat, the publications that extend the accepted head, not by taking the newest inscription. Each accepted publication MUST be at a strictly greater block height than the one before it; among publications that extend the same head, the earliest in chain order wins. Because at most one publication per block is accepted, chain order within a block only decides between candidates in that block. Inscriptions that do not extend the head MUST NOT be accepted and SHOULD be reported; those waiting on an unverifiable inscription are listed as P9.15 describes. For an anchored Original, Bitcoin is the log’s authoritative source under VH-Log’s rule for one [S20], and chain order on the sat orders its entries; the author key checks of P9.15 still apply. The extension defines a Bitcoin `src` kind under the did:vh extension mechanism [S19], whose value is the anchoring sat’s did:btco alias, and how the log is read from it under VH-Log’s rule for locations not reached over HTTPS [S23].

P9.14 An inscription MAY carry an entry in a compact encoding, such as a delta against the previous entry, defined by the extension under VH-Log’s rule for alternative encodings [S24]. It MUST decode deterministically to the full JSON entry, and entry hashes and proofs MUST be computed over the decoded full entry. An inscription that does not decode, or decodes differently for different readers, MUST be rejected.

P9.15 The entries before the anchoring entry, carried in the anchoring inscription, have claimed ordering (P7.6): each is checked against the signer DID version it names (P5.3), and a key’s removal after it was signed does not invalidate it, though the anchoring entry itself must be signed by a key valid at its inscription time. A verifier MUST check each entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, from the anchoring entry on, against its signer’s DID state at its inscription time, not its latest state. The rules below are stated for the author DID, which is the signer of an author entry. For an editor entry, they apply to the editor DID instead: its named version, its state at the inscription time and its signing key. Separately, an editor entry MUST be rejected if the author DID’s state at its inscription time is deactivated (P5.18). An inscription’s inscription time is the greatest block timestamp among the block containing it and that block’s ancestors, so it never decreases along the chain. The state at an inscription is the later of the author DID version the entry names and the latest author DID version whose versionTime is no later than the inscription time. The signing key MUST meet P5.3 both at the named version and in that state, and an entry inscribed when that state is deactivated MUST be rejected. A key removal or author DID deactivation therefore refuses entries whose inscription time is at or after its versionTime, unless, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, the entry names a later version that lists the key again (see below), and MUST NOT make non-current an accepted entry whose inscription time precedes it. Miners set block timestamps within consensus bounds, so inscription time can lag real time and an entry inscribed shortly after a removal can still be accepted. For a did:vh or did:webvh signer, a verifier uses the newest version of its DID that it knows of (P7.8); if that version is older than the inscription time, it checks the entry against that version and reports which version it relied on, rather than reporting incomplete identity evidence. For an entry in the anchoring inscription, a verifier that has no verified version at or after the one the entry names reports incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). For any entry inscribed on the anchoring sat, in the anchoring inscription or after it, a verifier that holds the signer’s DID history through a version numbered at or above the one the entry names, but not under the versionId it names, MUST reject the entry; for a did:btco signer whose sat it has read in full, it rejects one naming a version that is not in that history (P5.13). For an inscription after the anchoring inscription, a verifier that cannot otherwise verify the signer’s named version MUST report the inscription as unverifiable; an inscription that fails any check not depending on the unverified version is rejected, not unverifiable. An unverifiable inscription does not make the identity evidence of the accepted state incomplete and does not stop that state being current, because the sat’s holder chooses what is inscribed and has no authority over the Original (P9.12) (owner decision, 23 October 2026). For a did:btco signer, an inscription after the anchoring inscription is also unverifiable when the named version is known but the verifier has not read every inscription on that DID’s sat before the candidate, because one of them could remove the key: the candidate is unverifiable until it has, and MUST NOT be accepted against an older known state. If such a gap is found after an inscription was accepted, that inscription becomes unverifiable and the verifier recomputes the head under the rule below, reporting the change; an entry in the anchoring inscription with such a gap has incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). The report MUST list each unverifiable inscription with the signer DID and version it names. An unverifiable inscription does not extend the accepted head until it is verified, and a later publication that extends the same head MUST NOT be accepted while an earlier inscription extending that head is unverifiable: chain order, not the verifier’s evidence, decides between them (P9.13) (owner decision, 23 October 2026). The verifier reports such a publication as waiting on the unverifiable inscription. When it can verify that inscription, it accepts it if valid, or otherwise rejects it and considers the next publication in chain order that extends that head; either way it MUST report the resulting change of head and MUST NOT change its result silently. Publications that extend an unverifiable inscription, directly or through other waiting publications, are listed as waiting on it; once it is accepted the verifier follows them under P9.13, and if it is rejected they are ignored. A verifier therefore never accepts a later publication because evidence about an earlier one is missing; its result can still change when an unverifiable inscription is resolved, or through a reorganization (P9.5), a retroactive revocation conflict, a did:btco gap found later, or an earlier anchor (P9.16), and each change is reported. An unverifiable inscription can leave an Original waiting: whoever holds the anchoring sat can inscribe an entry naming a did:vh or did:webvh signer version that does not exist, and no later publication from that head is accepted, even after the sat is transferred, until the named signer’s DID reaches that version number under another versionId, after which the entry is rejected. A verifier MUST reject an entry naming a did:vh or did:webvh version numbered above the limit of P14.13, which this profile never verifies (P7.7), or later than a deactivated version of that DID it holds, which cannot exist. The wait is permanent if the named signer never updates its DID that far. This profile accepts that risk (owner decision, 23 October 2026); a did:btco signer cannot be named this way, because a verifier that reads its sat in full rejects a version not in it. For a did:btco author DID, the state is selected by chain order instead, and an entry naming a version that does not precede its inscription is rejected (P5.13). For a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, versionTime is claimed by the DID’s controller, so the report MUST state whether the author DID’s own witnesses or watchers support it. That controller can publish a version whose versionTime precedes inscriptions already accepted, though not its own previous version; a verifier that retained an earlier acceptance and then finds such a version MUST report a retroactive revocation conflict rather than silently change its result. Because the later version is used, for a did:vh or did:webvh author DID a version that the entry names can also re-authorize a key that the version in effect at the inscription time had removed; only the author DID’s controller can arrange this. Block timestamps can lag real time, which is why a named version with a later versionTime is accepted for these methods; for did:btco, chain order leaves no such lag and P5.13 applies.

P9.16 An Original has claimed ordering before its anchor (P7.6); the anchoring inscription’s block is the first ordering evidence for that history, showing that every earlier entry existed no later than that block. A holder of an authorized author key can anchor a different branch of the same Original on another sat. The anchor is the first valid anchoring entry, including under P9.15, that is inscribed on the sat it names, in chain order (P5.13) under the adapter’s confirmation policy (P9.5). Anchoring settles any fork of the Original (P6.6): the anchor’s branch is the Original’s history. A verifier MUST report every anchoring entry it knows of for the Original. Any other than the anchor MUST be reported as a conflicting anchor; it and its descendants MUST NOT be reported as current and MUST NOT confer ownership. No index lists every sat, so a verifier MUST report the anchor’s uniqueness as not established and the ownership it reports as provisional (P9.11). If a verifier later learns of a valid anchoring entry earlier in chain order than the one it reported as the anchor, it MUST report the earlier one as the anchor and the previous one as a conflicting anchor, together with the change in owner, and MUST NOT change its result silently. This is an accepted risk: a holder of a stolen author key who also holds a sat can anchor first, making the author’s later anchor a conflicting one, so an author who needs protection SHOULD anchor early.

P9.17 Each inscription on the anchoring sat that carries Originals entries MUST carry complete entries in its metadata, as P14.12 encodes them, unless a compact encoding is defined under P9.14; none is defined yet (D6; owner decision, 1 November 2026). The anchoring inscription MUST carry the Original’s whole log, in order, from its genesis entry through the anchoring entry, which MUST be its last entry; every later inscription MUST carry exactly one entry. Its body MUST be the exact bytes of the resource version that its last entry’s descriptor names, and its content type that version’s `mediaType`, so the sat’s newest accepted inscription shows the Original’s current version; an inscription whose last entry does not change the resource carries that version again. A verifier MUST check the body against that descriptor (P4.3), and MUST reject an inscription whose body digest, size or content type differs from it, or that has a content encoding. Versions not inscribed, including those before the anchoring entry’s, stay off Bitcoin (P9.18). The descriptors remain the content commitment of P9.4. The anchoring sat therefore carries the Original’s complete log [S20], and a verifier needs no copy of it: a copy that differs from the entries on the sat is ignored. An anchoring inscription whose entries do not verify as one log from its genesis, or whose last entry is not an anchoring entry naming that sat, is not an anchor. If a verifier cannot read the anchoring sat’s inscriptions in full, resolution MUST fail and the report MUST state missing evidence (P3.3). An inscription’s metadata and body together MUST NOT exceed 350,000 bytes (D6), which keeps a full anchoring inscription within Bitcoin’s standard transaction size. The SDK MUST refuse to anchor an Original whose log through the anchoring entry and current version together exceed it, or to publish an entry whose inscription would, and a verifier MUST reject a larger inscription.

P9.18 Bitcoin alone verifies an anchored Original’s log (P9.17) and, for a signer whose DID is a did:btco, that signer’s identity (P5.13): no host or copy is needed. The resource versions inscribed on the sat (P9.17), including the current one, are on Bitcoin too. Two things are not. Versions that were never inscribed, such as those before the anchoring entry’s, are not needed for the log to verify: a reader without them MUST report the log as verified and the missing content as unavailable (P7.4), never as a verification failure. The DID history of a did:vh or did:webvh signer is needed: without it identity evidence is incomplete (P5.6) and its freshness cannot come from Bitcoin; an inscription after the anchor that the signer signs is unverifiable and leaves later publications from that head waiting (P9.15). An author who wants Bitcoin to be all a reader needs SHOULD use a did:btco author DID and grant only editors whose DIDs are did:btco; anchoring does not require it (owner decision, 21 October 2026) (P5.15). Before inscribing the anchoring entry, the SDK MUST verify the log through the signed anchoring entry and the identity evidence of every signer of an entry in that log, and MUST NOT inscribe otherwise. That log, like any log a writer checks before publishing an entry, is its own committed log, not yet on the chain, and for an anchored Original only what is on the chain is trusted (P9.13, P9.20), so it cannot verify as published. A writer verifies it as a local copy: it verifies the copy whole, from genesis through the entry it is about to publish, and reports it as unverified on the chain and never as current. A local-copy result is valid only for the writer’s own check before it publishes, and MUST NOT appear in a report given to anyone else (owner decision, 13 November 2026). After submitting it, the SDK MUST label the result pending Bitcoin acceptance only while its Bitcoin evidence shows the inscription’s transactions held in the mempool, or confirmed but not yet verified, and submitted, not observed while it does not, including after rejection or eviction or when its Bitcoin provider has no mempool view; it MUST NOT present a submitted inscription the network is not observed to hold as on its way to acceptance. The checks of P5.13, P9.15 and P9.16 depend on the inscription’s position, so once it is confirmed the SDK verifies the Original and reports it as anchored, not anchored, or anchored with incomplete identity evidence (P5.6). Before inscribing any entry, it MUST tell the author that the resource version the inscription carries, and the log with its name and metadata, become public and permanent on Bitcoin and cannot be removed by a later entry (P8.3), and, for active content such as HTML or SVG, that Bitcoin explorers may render it outside this profile’s isolation rules (P9.7). It MUST tell the author what Bitcoin will not hold: the versions before the anchoring entry’s and any did:vh or did:webvh signer DID histories. This profile does not guarantee their replication or availability. When the anchoring sat is transferred, the sender’s SDK SHOULD give the recipient the uninscribed versions and off-chain signer DID histories it holds, and the recipient’s SDK SHOULD verify them.

### Anchoring on an EVM chain (proposed)

An author can choose at genesis to anchor an Original on one of the EVM chains this profile lists, before Bitcoin or instead of it (owner request, 9 November 2026; owner decisions, 10 November 2026: a named list of chains, a trust class for each, and publications held in contract state; owner decision, 11 November 2026: an Original anchored on an EVM chain can move up through the anchor layers, never sideways or down). The rules above still apply. A token takes the place of the sat: one token in a single contract with no administrator, which has the same code and, wherever the chain allows it, the same address on every listed chain (P14.21). Chains differ in who can reorder, censor or rewrite them, so each listed chain has a trust class, and a report states the chain and its class and never presents an EVM anchor as a Bitcoin anchor. The values these rules refer to are in section 14 and are provisional.

P9.19 An Original’s first anchor chain is fixed at genesis. The genesis entry’s asset state MAY carry an `anchor` member naming an EVM chain and a minter address (P14.18). Without it, the anchor chain is Bitcoin. Every later entry MUST carry the previous entry’s `anchor` member unchanged, or carry none if the previous entry had none, unless it is a move entry (P9.28), and a verifier MUST reject any other entry that adds, removes or changes it. No entry adds an `anchor` member to an Original whose genesis entry had none: an Original that starts on the path to Bitcoin stays on it. A writer MUST NOT write an `anchor` member naming a chain that P14.22 does not list. A verifier MUST report an Original whose `anchor` member names a chain its revision of P14.22 does not list as unsupported (P3.3), not invalid, because a later revision can list it. While an Original’s `anchor` member names an EVM chain, it can be anchored only on the one token P14.19 derives from that member, on the chain it names. Its [[ref: anchoring entry]] is the first entry whose asset state lists that token’s alias (P9.10, P9.20), and it is anchored only once that entry is validly published on the token and final (P9.22, P9.23). A verifier MUST reject an entry carrying an `anchor` member that lists a did:btco alias, or a token alias (P14.18) other than that token’s. It MUST also reject an entry of an Original without an `anchor` member that lists a token alias. Layers still move one way (P9.10): from local to web, from either to the Original’s first anchor chain, and from there only upward (P9.28).

P9.20 While an Original’s anchor chain is an EVM chain, P3.5, P5.10, P5.17, P5.18, P6.5, P6.6, P7.6, P7.8–P7.10, P7.14, P7.15, P9.4–P9.6 and P9.10–P9.18 apply with these readings, and P9.19–P9.29 add to them. Anchoring on an EVM chain is not terminal, as P9.10 makes anchoring on Bitcoin: the Original can move up (P9.28).

| Where the rules say | Read |
|---|---|
| the anchoring sat | the Original’s token (P14.19) |
| its did:btco alias | its token alias, a did:evm DID (P14.18) |
| an inscription on the sat | a publication on the token: a record the anchor contract holds for it (P14.20) |
| an inscription’s metadata, content type and body | a publication’s metadata, media type and body (P14.20) |
| chain order | the order of the token’s publication records, by index, which is the order in which the chain executed them |
| block height | the publication’s record index. A record holds no block number, so P9.13 accepts publications in index order, and more than one can be accepted from one block |
| block timestamp | the timestamp that the publication’s record holds, which is the chain’s own block timestamp |
| inscription time | the greatest timestamp among the publication’s record and the token’s earlier records |
| a Bitcoin index | a node or JSON-RPC provider for the chain the `anchor` member names, checked as P9.21 requires |
| the network | the chain the `anchor` member names |
| the confirmation policy and one confirmation (P9.5) | finality (P9.22) |
| the holder of the sat | the token’s holder (P9.23) |
| Bitcoin readers | readers of the chain the `anchor` member names |
| off Bitcoin; not settled on Bitcoin | while the Original is not anchored |

What these rules say about did:btco forms and network prefixes, sat numbers, ord tags and Bitcoin’s standard transaction size does not apply. P9.21–P9.27 cover the same ground for EVM chains. A verifier MUST NOT read an Original’s publications from any chain other than the one its `anchor` member names, even if the same contract is deployed there.

P9.21 P14.22 lists the supported chains, and for each its chain ID, the hash of its first block and its trust class (P9.27). A chain ID alone does not identify a chain, because anyone can run another chain with the same ID. Before relying on a node or provider, a verifier MUST check that it reports the listed chain ID and that the hash of its block 0 is the one P14.22 lists; a node or provider that reports another is not serving that chain, and its answers are not evidence. A verifier MUST read publications and ownership only from the anchor contract at the address P14.21 fixes for that chain. Before relying on any read, it MUST check that the code at that address, on the named chain and at the block it reads, has the runtime code hash P14.21 pins. Code at another address, or other code at that address, is not an Originals anchor, whatever interface it offers. The contract has no owner, administrator, upgrade, pause or self-destruct path, so whoever deployed it has no authority over any Original. Anyone MAY deploy it, by the deterministic deployment P14.21 defines, on a listed chain that lacks it. Deploying it on a chain that is not listed does not make that chain supported. A verifier MUST report an Original anchored on a listed chain where the pinned code is not at that address as unsupported (P3.3), never as verified or as not anchored.

P9.22 A publication counts once its record is in the contract’s state at the chain’s finalized block, as reported by the verifier’s nodes or providers through the JSON-RPC `finalized` block tag [S35]. For a rollup, finality is the chain’s own: typically, its block was derived from data in a finalized block of the chain it settles to. A verifier MUST read the contract’s state at the finalized block, not at a later one. A node or provider is usable only if it passes P9.21’s checks of the chain ID and the hash of block 0, and can serve the contract’s state at that block, however far finality trails the head: on some chains that is further back than an ordinary node keeps state. A verifier MUST drop a provider that is not usable, and its answers are not evidence; the reads of the providers that remain usable stand. One usable provider is enough, even when it is the only one the verifier is configured with (owner decision, 13 November 2026). Evidence is missing only when no usable provider remains (P9.25). A verifier that uses two usable providers MUST take the lower of the two finalized block numbers they report, and MUST read that block’s hash and the contract’s state at that block from both. A read at any other block, including the finalized block of the provider that is ahead, is not evidence for that comparison. Providers whose finalized blocks differ in number do not thereby disagree: one of them is behind. They disagree only if they report different hashes for that block, or different state at it, and the verifier then reports missing evidence until they agree (P9.10); it MUST NOT settle a disagreement by dropping one of them. A publication recorded in a later block is observed, never part of the accepted log. If a block the verifier accepted as finalized is later replaced, the verifier MUST report this as P9.5 requires, with VH-Log’s `authoritative-source-changed` warning [S20], and MUST also report that the chain’s finality failed. A verifier assumes that finality holds: it is not required to read again the hash of every finalized block it relied on before. When it has evidence, from a provider or elsewhere, that such a block was replaced, it MUST report this as a warning as above and MUST discard every retained result and checkpoint (P9.25) that rested on that block or a later one. Every report MUST state the number and hash of the finalized block it relied on and name the provider or providers its read came from, and MUST mark a read that only one usable provider answered as uncorroborated.

P9.23 The owner of an Original anchored on an EVM chain is the address the anchor contract’s `ownerOf` returns for its token at the finalized block (P9.11). That address may belong to an account or to a contract. Only the holder, the address approved for that token through `approve`, or an operator the holder approved through `setApprovalForAll` [S33], can publish on the token, and the contract checks this when a publication is recorded (P14.20). Being able to publish confers no authority to sign changes (P9.12). Minting the token does not anchor the Original: until a valid anchoring entry is published on the token, the Original is not anchored and has no owner, whoever holds the token. Minting is open to anyone, so the contract holds tokens that anchor nothing: a token’s existence, or what its `tokenURI` returns, does not show that it anchors a valid Original. Only the verified history does.

P9.24 The token is derived from the Original and the minter its genesis entry fixes (P14.19), so an Original has exactly one possible token on each chain it is anchored on, and cannot be anchored on any other. Anchoring entries published on that token are subject to P9.16: the first valid one in chain order (P9.20) is the anchor, any other is a conflicting anchor, and the anchor settles any fork (P6.6), under the chain’s finality and trust class (P9.27). P9.16 requires uniqueness to be reported as not established; for an Original anchored on an EVM chain, a verifier MAY instead report the anchor’s uniqueness in the terms of the chain’s class (P9.27): as established on a `consensus` or `exit-protected` chain, as attested by the operator on an `operator` chain, and as attested by the validators on a `consortium` chain. In each case it states that this holds only under the chain’s finality and the providers it used. Only the minter can mint the token. If the minter’s key is lost before the token is minted, the Original can never be anchored there, and a move that names that minter can never arrive (P9.29). If the key is stolen first, the thief can mint and hold the token, and the author can then anchor only with the thief’s cooperation.

P9.25 Each publication MUST carry complete entries and the resource version, as P9.17 requires of an inscription, and the anchor contract MUST hold both in its state, as P14.20 defines (owner decision, 10 November 2026). State is what every node of the chain keeps and what the contract itself can read, so the bytes last as long as the chain does and need no archive of event logs. A publication whose last entry names a resource version that an earlier publication on the token already stored MAY refer to those bytes instead of storing them again (P14.20); it still names its body, and a verifier checks that body against the last entry’s descriptor as for any other. The 350,000-byte limit applies to the metadata and body a publication stores, and the contract enforces it on every publication and every upload, so no record can exceed it (P14.20). A verifier MUST read the token’s publication records, and the metadata and body each names, from the contract’s state at the finalized block (P9.22). Event logs are an aid to finding publications and are never evidence: a verifier MUST NOT accept a publication from an event log, or decide resolution on the absence of one. A verifier MUST decide from a record’s own fields whether to read its bytes. It MUST reject a record whose metadata or body length exceeds the limit, or whose media type is longer than P14.20 allows, without reading its metadata or body, and such a record never makes evidence missing. The bytes of every other record can be read in ranges of any size (P14.20), so none is too large for a provider to serve, and a record that is no valid publication is rejected once read. A holder can therefore not stop an Original’s earlier accepted history from resolving by publishing bytes on its token. A holder can make it costly: the number of records on a token is not limited, and a verifier reading a token for the first time reads every record. A verifier that retained a verified checkpoint MAY resume from it and read only the later records. A checkpoint is identified by the versionId of the accepted head entry, `<number>-<entry hash>`. Each entry hash covers the previous entry’s versionId, so that versionId pins every earlier entry: “line N is checkpoint `<hash>`” fixes the whole log through line N. The checkpoint also records the chain read that verified it: the chain, as its CAIP-2 ID [S31]; the token, as the contract address and the token ID; the index of the record that accepted the head entry; the number and hash of the finalized block it was read at (P9.22); and the chain’s trust class at that block (P9.27). A retained result that lacks any of these is not a checkpoint. A checkpoint holds no entries: whoever resumes from it supplies the log, which MUST reach exactly that versionId, its entry numbered N having that versionId. A verifier MUST NOT resume from a checkpoint with a log that does not, and reads the token from its first record instead. Entries after line N in a supplied log are not evidence; the later records are. A verifier MUST NOT make a checkpoint while a publication waits on an unverifiable entry (P9.15) (owner decision, 13 November 2026). This profile sets no cap on records and no reader budget, so a first verification can be expensive, and it does not guarantee that a token with many records stays practical to read. If a verifier cannot read the token’s records, or the bytes of a record it has not rejected, from state, resolution MUST fail with missing evidence (P3.3, P9.17), and MUST NOT be decided on the part it did read. A verifier that holds the finalized block’s header from a source it trusts MAY check its reads against that block’s state root.

P9.26 An entry that carries an `anchor` member MUST NOT name a did:btco DID as its author or in an editor grant, and a verifier MUST reject one that does. An Original that has moved to Bitcoin MAY grant editors whose DIDs are did:btco, from the move entry on; its author DID, fixed at genesis, is not one. P5.13 orders did:btco versions on Bitcoin, and nothing orders them against an EVM chain. For a did:vh or did:webvh signer, P9.15 applies with the inscription time that P9.20 defines.

P9.27 Each listed chain has one trust class, which says who can undo or block what an anchor on it establishes:

| Class | The chain | A report describes ordering and ownership as | Layer (P9.28) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `consensus` | has a validator or miner set that anyone can join, and no party holds a key that can change its rules or its state: a change takes effect only when its validators adopt it | proven on that chain | proven chain |
| `exit-protected` | is a rollup that publishes its transaction data to a `consensus` chain; a transaction can be forced in without its operator; and its operator alone cannot upgrade it: an upgrade either waits long enough for users to leave, or needs a security council that the operator does not control | proven on that chain; its operator can delay publications | proven chain |
| `consortium` | has its own validator set, and a named party or group decides who joins it; its validators acting together can reorder, censor or rewrite it | attested by that chain’s validators | attested chain |
| `operator` | has one party, or parties it controls, that can without delay upgrade it, or filter or reorder its transactions; or does not publish its transaction data to a `consensus` chain | attested by that chain’s operator | attested chain |

A verification report for an Original anchored on an EVM chain MUST name the chain and its trust class, as the revision of P14.22 the verifier implements lists it, and MUST name the token, the finalized block and the providers its read came from, marking the read uncorroborated when only one provider answered (P9.22). It MUST describe ordering and ownership as the table states. For a `consortium` or `operator` chain it MUST NOT describe ordering, ownership, the anchor’s uniqueness or the settlement of a fork as proven: a fork settled by an anchor on such a chain is settled as attested by that chain, not by independently proven ordering (P9.24). It MUST NOT describe any EVM anchor as a Bitcoin anchor. A class states who can rewrite a chain, not how costly that is, so two chains of one class are not equally strong, and a report MUST NOT present them as such. A later revision of P14.22 can change a chain’s class in either direction; a verifier then reports the class of the revision it implements.

Before writing a genesis entry with an `anchor` member, the SDK MUST tell the author:

- that the choice of first chain is permanent, and which layer it is in: the Original can later move only upward, to a chain in a higher layer or to Bitcoin, never sideways, down or back (P9.28);
- that a move does not free the Original from a chain: it stays dependent on every chain it was ever anchored on (P9.29);
- the chain’s trust class and what it means: once the Original is anchored, its ordering, its owner and whether changes can be published rest on that chain, and on whoever the class says can upgrade, pause, censor or reorder it;
- that its publications last only as long as the chain’s nodes keep its state, and that this profile can do nothing for an Original whose chain stops;
- that a large publication can need several staged transactions (P14.20), and that every entry carries the full asset state, so large metadata makes each later edit cost more to publish;
- which minter address it fixes.

The labels of P9.18 apply. A publication is pending while a provider’s transaction pool holds its transactions, or once it is recorded in a block that is not yet finalized. It is submitted, not observed, otherwise.

P9.28 Anchor layers are ordered. The lowest is an attested chain: a listed chain of class `operator` or `consortium`. Above it is a proven chain: a listed chain of class `exit-protected` or `consensus`. Bitcoin is above both, and last (P9.27). An Original anchored on an EVM chain MAY move up: to a chain in a higher layer, or to Bitcoin. It MUST NOT move to a chain in the same layer or in a lower one, and an Original anchored on Bitcoin does not move (P9.10) (owner decision, 11 November 2026). A move is made by a move entry: an ordinary update entry (section 6), signed by the author and not by an editor, whose asset state:

- replaces the `anchor` member with one naming the destination chain and a minter there (P14.18), or removes it when the destination is Bitcoin; and
- replaces the token alias with the destination’s alias: that of the one token P14.19 derives from the new `anchor` member, or the did:btco alias of the destination sat (P9.10).

A verifier MUST reject a move entry whose destination is not in a higher layer than the chain the previous entry’s `anchor` member names, a move entry that an editor signed, an entry that changes `anchor` without listing the destination’s alias, and a move entry that comes before the Original’s anchoring entry: an Original moves only from a chain it is anchored on. It reports a move entry whose destination chain its revision of P14.22 does not list as unsupported (P9.19). Layers are judged by the classes of P14.22 as they applied at the timestamp of the departure record (P9.29, P14.22), so a later change of a chain’s class does not undo a move. Until the departure record is read, as when a writer prepares a move entry or a reader holds a copy of the log, layers are judged provisionally by the classes in the verifier’s revision of P14.22; once the record is read, the classes at its timestamp decide. How the history of classes is kept and resolved is open (D6). A move to Bitcoin puts the move entry in the place of the anchoring entry for the rules of Bitcoin; its publication there is an arrival, not a first anchor (P9.29). From it P9.10–P9.18 apply without the readings of P9.20, except as P9.29 states for the arrival, and that anchor is terminal.

P9.29 A move has two publications, and both carry the move entry.

- **Departure.** The move entry is published on the token the Original is leaving, as any other entry is (P9.12, P9.13). Only that token’s holder, or an address the holder approved (P9.23), can publish there, so a move needs the author’s signature and the owner’s act, like every other change. The move entry’s signer is checked here, once, against its DID state at the departure’s inscription time (P9.15). Once a move entry is accepted on a token, the Original has departed: a verifier MUST NOT accept any later publication on that token, and reports those it finds.
- **Arrival.** The first valid publication on the destination that carries the Original’s whole log, in order, from its genesis entry through the move entry as its last entry, with the body P9.17 requires. On an EVM chain it is a publication on the destination token (P9.25). On Bitcoin it is the anchoring inscription on the destination sat (P9.17). Its entries MUST be the entries accepted through the departure, and a publication carrying any other log is not an arrival. An arrival is checked only for carrying exactly those entries and for its body. P9.15’s check of an anchoring entry against its signer’s DID state at its inscription time does not apply to an arrival, on an EVM chain or on Bitcoin: a key removed, or a signer DID deactivated, after the departure MUST NOT make an arrival invalid (owner decision, 11 November 2026). The entries an arrival carries that were accepted on a chain keep the ordering of that stretch, as described below, not the claimed ordering that P9.15 and P9.16 give entries before a first anchor; those from before the first anchoring entry stay carried, with claimed ordering. Later publications on the destination carry one entry each, and P9.15 applies to them.

A verifier follows the path from the Original’s first anchor chain: it reads each chain the log names, in order, through its departure, and the last one through its finalized block. It MUST NOT accept an [[ref: arrival]] unless the departure is accepted and final on the chain the Original left (P9.22), read as P9.21 requires. Between a departure and its arrival it MUST report the Original as departed and not arrived, with the state of the move entry, the destination, and no owner. If the minter the move entry names never completes the arrival, the Original stays so. If the departure is final and the verifier cannot read the destination, it MUST report the departure as finalized and the destination as unreachable, with missing evidence (P3.3), and MUST NOT report an arrival or the lack of one. Of the log it then reports only what P7.14 allows a reader that cannot read the chain the Original is anchored on now: at most the entries before the first anchoring entry, as valid as of the checkpoint before it, with claimed ordering. The anchoring entry and every later entry, through the move entry, MUST be reported as not checked against the Original’s current anchor, though the chain left accepted them, and the verifier reports no owner and nothing as current (owner decision, 13 November 2026). A verified move is reported as a move, with its source and its destination. It is not a conflicting anchor or a change of anchor under P9.16, and a verifier that retained the anchor on the token left MUST NOT report the arrival as one; conflict and finality warnings are for actual conflicts and finality failures. From the arrival, the owner is the holder of the destination token or sat (P9.11, P9.23), and whoever holds the token left behind owns nothing.

The chains an Original has left remain evidence. An author who has sold an Original could otherwise sign a move entry and arrive elsewhere alone. A verifier that cannot read a chain the Original left, as P9.21, P9.22 and P9.25 require, cannot confirm that the owner there agreed to the move. It MUST report the move as unconfirmed, and MUST NOT report an owner or report the Original as current, though it reports the log that the arrival carries as verified, its proofs and hash links checked from genesis (P3.3). It MUST NOT describe the ordering of the entries accepted on the unread chain as proven or attested: without that chain’s evidence their ordering is claimed. Publications on the destination after the arrival are read and checked as usual and reported with it, as following an unconfirmed move: their entries are verified as part of the log, and none is reported as current until the move is confirmed. If a chain the Original left later replaces the block that held the departure, the verifier reports a finality failure there (P9.22) and the move as unconfirmed.

A report for an Original that has moved MUST list every chain it was anchored on, in order, each with its trust class, the entries accepted while it was there, and, for each chain it read, the finalized block and providers used. It MUST describe ordering and ownership for each stretch as that chain’s class requires (P9.27), except a stretch on a chain it could not read, whose ordering is claimed as stated above. Moving up does not raise what is said of an earlier stretch: ordering and ownership attested on one chain are not proven because the Original later moved to another. Entries from before the Original’s first anchoring entry are described as carried, with claimed ordering, after any number of moves: their proofs and hash links are checked, and carrying them to a later chain does not establish their order. The arrival shows only that the log through the move entry existed no later than its block on the destination (P9.16).

Before writing a move entry, the SDK MUST tell the author:

- that the move cannot be reversed, and which layers remain above the destination;
- that the Original stays dependent on the chain it leaves, which a verifier still reads to confirm the move;
- what becomes public and permanent on the destination, as P9.18 requires before any publication.

The SDK MUST refuse a move whose arrival would exceed the 350,000-byte limit (P9.17, P9.25). Before inscribing an arrival on Bitcoin after a move off an EVM chain, the SDK MUST require the departure to be accepted and final on the chain the Original leaves (P9.22), and MUST then verify the log through the move entry as a local copy (P9.18); it MUST NOT inscribe otherwise (owner decision, 13 November 2026).

::: example Harbor at dusk, anchored on a rollup
Alice creates her Original with `anchor` naming the rollup `eip155:4663` and her address A as minter. The SDK first tells her that this chain is of class `operator`, so its operator can delay, filter or reorder her publications and can upgrade the chain, and that the Original can later move only upward. A month later she mints the token from A and publishes entries 1 to 4 on it, with version 3 of the photograph as the body. Entry 4 lists the token alias. Once that block is finalized, Bob’s verifier checks that each of its two providers serves the chain whose first block P14.22 lists, and checks the contract’s code hash. It reads the token’s one record, its metadata and its body from state at the finalized block, verifies entries 1 to 4 and the body against entry 4’s descriptor, and reads `ownerOf`. It reports the Original as anchored on `eip155:4663`, a chain of class `operator`, current as of that block and owned by A, with ordering, ownership and uniqueness attested by the chain’s operator. Alice’s next entry changes only the name, so its publication stores that entry and refers to the photograph already stored. When Alice sells the token to Carol, Carol becomes the owner. Alice’s next edit takes effect only when Carol publishes it.
:::

::: example Harbor at dusk moves up
Two years on, Alice and Carol agree to move the Original from `eip155:4663`, an attested chain, to Ethereum, a proven chain. Alice signs a move entry whose `anchor` names `eip155:1` and Carol’s address C as minter, and whose alias is the token that derives from them. Carol publishes it on the old token: the departure. She mints the new token on Ethereum from C and publishes the whole log on it, ending with the move entry, with the current photograph: the arrival. Bob’s verifier reads both chains. It reports the Original as anchored on `eip155:1`, a chain of class `consensus`, owned by C. It reports the stretch on `eip155:4663` as attested by that chain’s operator, and what is published from now on as proven on Ethereum. The Original can now move only to Bitcoin. Had Alice signed a move entry naming her own address and published the log on Ethereum herself, the verifier would have found no departure on the old token, and accepted nothing.
:::
