## Security considerations

This section collects the threats discussed throughout the draft.

| Adversary | Can | Cannot, under the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Malicious host or mirror | Withhold bytes or evidence, serve an old valid prefix of the asset log or of a signer’s DID log, show different branches of an unanchored Original to different readers; a first-time reader cannot tell, and retained versionIds protect only repeat readers (P7.6, P7.8); withhold a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s log, or serve a truncated one, leaving an anchored Original’s identity evidence incomplete and later inscriptions by that signer `unverifiable` (P9.33), and, when that signer signed an entry in the anchoring publication, the anchor itself `undetermined` for every reader without a retained copy of the log (P9.16, P9.18) | Alter bytes or entries without failing digest or proof checks (P2.3, P4.3); hide a fork of an anchored Original from a reader that reads the anchoring sat (P9.13); forge a signer’s DID log, or have an inscribed entry accepted by withholding or truncating one, which leaves evidence missing and is never accepting (P9.15, P9.33) |
| Every holder of an anchored Original’s uninscribed resource versions, if all withhold them | Make that content unavailable (P9.18) | Stop the log or its did:btco signers’ identities verifying from Bitcoin, or substitute other history (P9.17, P5.13) |
| Network attacker | Observe fetches; block or delay them | Forge entries or bytes; steer resolvers at private addresses (P8.5) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, before the author removes it | Edit or deactivate any Original the key controls that is not anchored, or whose sat the thief also holds; a deactivation is terminal on its branch, and permanent once inscribed (P6.5); create a branch of an unanchored Original, which leaves it an unresolved conflict with no current branch until anchoring (P6.6); anchor an unanchored Original on a sat the thief holds, which, if inscribed first, is the anchor with the thief as its holder, and the author’s later anchor is a conflicting one (P9.16) | Move control away from the author DID (P5.9) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, after the author removes it | Sign entries on unanchored Originals naming an older author DID version, reported as ordering unproven and never current (P7.9); for a did:vh or did:webvh author, if the thief also holds the anchoring sat, keep inscribing entries signed with the key and naming a version that lists it, because a removal in a did:vh or did:webvh log never binds inscribed entries and a verifier only warns of it; this is an accepted risk, and an author who needs removals to bind uses a did:btco DID (P9.33) | Have such an entry reported as current off Bitcoin (P7.10), or, for a did:btco author, accepted as an anchoring entry or a later inscription after the author DID’s own sat evidences the removal (P5.13, P9.15) |
| Holder of a stolen editor key, before the editor removes it | Change the content the grant covers; fork an unanchored Original | Change grants, aliases, lifecycle or parameters, anchor or deactivate (P5.16) |
| Thief of the wallet holding a did:btco editor DID’s sat | Take permanent control of that editor identity and change the content its grants cover until the author revokes them (P5.17) | Act as the author or change any grant (P5.16) |
| Author, before anchoring | Change anything in the Original, grant and revoke editors, and deactivate it (P5.16); fork it from any entry, which off Bitcoin leaves an unresolved conflict unless the fork’s other branches hold only editor entries, which the author can supersede (P6.6, P6.9); remove a key, which leaves the entries it signed `key-removed-since` until a later author entry endorses them (P5.9, P7.20); anchor any branch first (P9.16) | Transfer authorship, which never transfers (P5.8, D7); make an editor entry valid outside its grant (P5.15); make an unanchored Original current (P7.10); undo, after an inscription, a change that Bitcoin orders (P9.13) |
| Editor | Change the content its grant allows without the author; publish a competing entry from an earlier head, forking an unanchored Original until the author supersedes that branch with an author entry or anchors it (P6.6, P6.9) | Widen its grant, add or change aliases, anchor, change lifecycle or parameters, deactivate, or change the author DID or its keys (P5.16, P5.18) |
| Editor after revocation, or holder of a revoked editor’s key | Fork an unanchored Original from an entry before the revocation, which leaves it a `conflict` until the author signs an entry superseding that branch, off-chain and without disclosure (P6.9), or anchors it; each new fork from an earlier point needs superseding again | Have an entry accepted whose parent’s state no longer grants it (P5.17) |
| Shared host | Refuse or delay submissions; publish a refused candidate it was sent, which forks an unanchored Original (P6.8) | Change, rebase or re-sign a signed entry without failing verification; accept two entries with the same parent without a reader that sees both reporting a fork (P6.6, P6.7) |
| Someone claiming a moved author or editor DID | Publish a DID document whose `alsoKnownAs` lists the identity DID, or redirect its old location | Make their keys sign for that identity without a move in the identity DID’s own log (P5.14) |
| Malicious archive or bundle | Exhaust verifier resources; attempt path traversal; roll back to an older prefix | Have an over-limit history reported as complete or current, or replace newer local history (P7.5, P7.7) |
| Malicious host application, or other software holding the key | Ask the signer to sign an unintended change; if the author’s assertionMethod key is also used outside Originals, get a signer for that other use to sign an Originals entry, which verifiers accept because they cannot tell the key’s uses apart (P5.3, P5.11) | Obtain a signature from a conforming Originals signer for a change it did not show the author (P5.11) |
| Malicious content author | Publish signed active content | Run script on the platform origin or in a client (P9.7) |
| Signer who names a preview | Inscribe a preview that does not depict the version, or, for a version that is not an image, active content as its preview, which Bitcoin explorers may render (P9.7, P9.18) | Have the preview verified as the version, or as evidence of the version’s bytes, which are checked only against the descriptor (P4.3, P9.30); name a preview in an entry that is not prepared for Bitcoin (P9.30) |
| Holder of the anchoring sat | Sell or give away the Original by moving the sat; refuse to inscribe the author’s or editors’ changes, freezing the Original; inscribe changes the author or a granted editor signed; inscribe a did:btco DID document on the sat, which a generic did:btco resolver returns but Originals verifiers ignore (P3.5, P3.8, P9.31); with a granted editor, keep changing the content the grant covers while refusing to inscribe the author’s revocation (P5.17, P9.12); inscribe an entry, which it can forge, naming a did:vh or did:webvh version that never exists, leaving that inscription `unverifiable` and every later publication `waiting` on it permanently (P9.36), which harms only the holder, since only the holder can inscribe on the sat, and a buyer’s transfer check fails on it (P7.22) | Author, rotate or deactivate on their own (P9.12); get an entry accepted that names a did:btco signer version not on that signer’s own sat before it (P5.13), or a did:vh or did:webvh version that the signer’s resolved log does not hold (P9.33) |
| Holder of a signed but uninscribed entry, such as the holder of the anchoring sat or anyone the signer gave it to | Inscribe it on the anchoring sat at any time, if it still extends the accepted head and its signer’s key meets P5.3 at the version it names and, for a did:btco signer, in the state its sat evidences at that inscription: a signed entry never expires, so a change the signer later decided against can still be published. Authors and editors SHOULD NOT sign an entry they do not intend to see inscribed; they can void one only by getting another entry from the same head inscribed first, or, for a did:btco signer, by a key removal its sat evidences before it (P9.13, P9.15) | Inscribe it once another publication has extended that head (P9.13) |
| Index of Originals inscriptions | Omit a competing anchor, so a verifier that trusts it reports uniqueness as established through a block when it is not (P9.16) | Change which publication is the anchor, or what is accepted, for a verifier that reads the inscriptions itself (P9.16, P9.31) |
| Thief of the holder’s wallet | Become the holder by moving the sat; freeze the Original by refusing to inscribe | Sign any change (P9.12) |
| Thief of the wallet holding a did:btco author DID’s sat | Take permanent control of the author DID, which the author cannot recover; add their own assertionMethod keys and sign as the author; deactivate the author DID, making every unanchored Original non-current (P6.5) | Change an anchored Original without its sat’s holder (P9.12); change the author DID named by any Original (P5.8) |
| Host of a did:webvh author or editor DID, or a watcher or other holder of a did:vh author or editor DID’s log, which has no location [S3] | Withhold the DID log, or serve a truncated one, leaving Originals with incomplete identity evidence for readers with no copy, and, for an anchored Original, later inscriptions by that signer `unverifiable` and the publications after them `waiting` (P9.33, P9.36), and, for a signer of an entry in the anchoring publication, the anchor itself `undetermined` (P9.16, T9-140), so that a lapsed did:webvh domain leaves the Original un-anchored for any reader without a retained copy of the log (P9.18); withhold newer versions, so a reader that does not know of a key removal does not see it or warn of it (P7.8) | Forge the DID log, whose SCID commits to its genesis and each of whose versions is signed by the updateKeys of the version before it; have an inscribed entry accepted by withholding or truncating the log, which leaves evidence missing (P9.15, P9.33); affect the author binding of Originals whose author DID is a did:btco (P5.13) |
| Copier who inscribes the same evidence on another sat | Hold an inscription of it | Become the Original’s holder, or change the Original (P9.11) |
| Author after selling an anchored Original | Sign changes, which take effect only if the holder inscribes them; rotate keys or deactivate their DID, which, for a did:btco DID, refuses later inscriptions once its sat evidences it, and, for a did:vh or did:webvh DID, refuses none, a verifier only warning of it (P9.15, P9.33); anchor a competing branch of the Original on another sat and sell it to a buyer whose verifier uses no index of Originals inscriptions, which reports its uniqueness as not established and its holder as provisional (P9.16) | Change or deactivate the Original alone; change the holder or move it to another sat (P9.10, P9.12); void accepted inscriptions or the anchor with a DID version published later, whatever its versionTime, since each inscribed entry is checked at the version it names (P9.15, P9.33); anchor a second Original on the same sat (P9.34); hide the competing anchor from a verifier that uses an index of Originals inscriptions through its block (P9.16) |
| Distributor of a carried file | Strip the carrier; attach an older valid prefix, or the log of another Original that has a version with the same bytes; append a carrier to bytes it does not match | Have mismatched bytes verified or the file presented as a version of that Original, have a forged log verified once the signers’ DIDs are resolved, have an older prefix reported as current, or have a carried log preferred over the Original’s anchor (P7.12–P7.14) |
| Mail or malware scanner | Flag or strip a file whose trailer follows the format’s own end, a pattern also used to smuggle data | Change what a reader verifies; a stripped carrier leaves the version’s bytes (P14.15) |
| Author of a carried file’s log, or of an anchored Original | Name DIDs on a host they control and learn the address and time of each reader that resolves them, including each reader that verifies an anchored Original by resolving its signers (P9.33) | Make a reader resolve without the caller’s opt-in (P7.13, P8.5, P9.33) |
| did:vh or did:webvh signer that forks its own log | Publish two branches of its DID log and withhold the branch whose versionId an inscribed entry names, which leaves that version missing and the entry `unverifiable`, or the anchor `undetermined`, freezing the Original on that sat for readers without that branch (P9.33, P9.36) | Void an inscribed entry or the anchor: a versionId that no verifying log holds is missing evidence, never a rejection, and the result does not depend on which branch a reader sees first (P9.15, P9.33) |
| Holder of a sat other than the anchoring sat | Inscribe an earlier anchoring publication of the same `did:og` whose signer evidence is unavailable, which is ignored: not listed, no diagnostic, the default and transfer checks unaffected, and only the anchor’s uniqueness left unestablished, with a warning shown beside the report (P9.16, P7.22) | Displace the anchor without evidence that its own publication verifies; affect the Original with an anchoring publication of another `did:og` (P9.16, P9.34) |
| did:vh or did:webvh author of an anchored Original | Withhold the log versions that an earlier anchoring publication on another sat names, and anchor the same `did:og` again, so that a reader with no copy of those versions takes the later one as the anchor (P9.16, P9.33) | Hide that there was an earlier claim from a reader shown the warning of P9.16, or from one that retained the author’s log (P7.8): with the log, the earlier publication verifies and is the anchor, and the later one a conflicting anchor |
| Bitcoin reorganization | Undo a publication accepted at one confirmation, or reorder competing anchors, changing the head, anchor or holder in a report at a later tip (P9.5, P9.16); a buyer relying on the transfer check needs 6 confirmations, so only a reorganization of 6 or more blocks can undo what it accepted (P7.22) | Advance the durable published boundary (P9.4, P9.5); invalidate a chain of publications re-mined into one block, which chain order still orders (P9.13) |

Asset logs do not use witnesses (P9.8). An Original that is not anchored has claimed ordering only and is never reported as current (P7.10): a valid prefix is not proof of current state, a host can show a first-time reader an old prefix or a different branch, and recovery from key compromise is limited (section 5). Anchored Originals get their ordering from chain order on the sat (P9.13); history before the anchor stays claimed, bounded by the anchor’s block, and the anchor’s uniqueness and holder are reported as not established and provisional unless an index of Originals inscriptions shows no competing anchor through a given block (P9.16). The anchoring sat carries the complete log and the preview, or the full bytes, of each version inscribed on it (P9.17, P9.30), and no signer DID history: a did:vh or did:webvh signer’s log is resolved from its DID, so its host can leave an anchored Original’s identity evidence incomplete but cannot forge it, and a key removal in that log never binds inscribed entries, an accepted risk for which an author or editor who needs removals to bind uses a did:btco DID (P9.18, P9.33); versions not inscribed in full, by default including the current one, survive only where someone keeps them, and missing bytes leave the log verified (P9.18). That host is also an availability dependency of the anchor itself: if the log of a signer of an entry in the anchoring publication cannot be obtained, the anchor is `undetermined` (P9.16), so a did:webvh author whose domain lapses un-anchors the Original for every reader without a retained copy of its log. Resolving a signer DID needs the caller’s opt-in (P7.13, P9.33), because the author chooses the DID’s host and learns who resolves it; a reader that wants to avoid that keeps or is given the log instead. A signer that forks its own DID log can withhold the branch an entry names, which freezes that entry as missing evidence, `unverifiable` or the anchor `undetermined`, but never voids it (P9.33). The holder of the anchoring sat can inscribe an entry naming a did:vh or did:webvh version that never exists, leaving it `unverifiable` and every later publication `waiting` for ever (P9.36); only the holder can inscribe on the sat, so this harms only the holder, and a buyer sees the failing transfer check (P7.22). An anchoring publication of the Original on another sat that cannot be decided does not stop the anchoring sat’s later valid publication from being the anchor (P9.16). A verifier that reads only that later sat, or that lacks the earlier one’s signer evidence, may therefore report the later one as the anchor while the true earlier anchor’s evidence is unavailable; once that evidence is in an evidence set, the earlier one is the anchor and the later one a conflicting anchor. This is accepted because uniqueness is provisional anyway: the report never establishes it through the undecided publication’s block (P9.16). The first valid anchor wins, so authors who need protection against a stolen key should anchor early. Off Bitcoin no freshness policy makes an Original current; it is reported only as valid as of a checkpoint (P7.10).

Permanent history cannot be redacted (P8.3). A key or DID used across Originals links them publicly.
