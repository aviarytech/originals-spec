## Security considerations

This section collects the threats discussed throughout the draft.

| Adversary | Can | Cannot, under the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Malicious host or mirror | Withhold bytes or evidence, serve an old valid prefix of the asset log or the author’s DID log, show different branches of an unanchored Original to different readers; a first-time reader cannot tell, and retained versionIds protect only repeat readers (P7.6, P7.8) | Alter bytes or entries without failing digest or proof checks (P2.3, P4.3); hide a fork of an anchored Original from a reader that reads the anchoring sat (P9.13) |
| Network attacker | Observe fetches; block or delay them | Forge entries or bytes; steer resolvers at private addresses (P8.5) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, before the author removes it | Edit or deactivate any Original the key controls that is not anchored, or whose sat the thief also holds; deactivation is permanent; create a branch of an unanchored Original, which stays a reported fork until anchoring; anchor an unanchored Original on a sat the thief holds, which, if inscribed first, is the anchor with the thief as its permanent owner, and the author’s later anchor is a conflicting one (P9.16) | Move control away from the author DID (P5.9) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, after the author removes it | Sign entries on unanchored Originals naming an older author DID version, reported as ordering unproven and never current (P7.9) | Have such an entry reported as current, or accepted on an anchoring sat after the removal (P9.15) |
| Malicious archive or package | Exhaust verifier resources; attempt path traversal; roll back to an older prefix | Have an over-limit history reported as complete or current, or replace newer local history (P7.5, P7.7) |
| Malicious host application, or other software holding the key | Ask the signer to sign an unintended change | Obtain a signature from a conforming signer for a change it did not show the author, because the key is used only for Originals (P5.11) |
| Malicious content author | Publish signed active content | Run script on the platform origin or in a client (P9.7) |
| Owner, the holder of the anchoring sat | Sell or give away the Original by moving the sat; refuse to inscribe the author’s changes, freezing the Original; inscribe changes the author signed | Author, rotate or deactivate on their own (P9.12) |
| Thief of the owner’s wallet | Take ownership by moving the sat; freeze the Original | Author any change (P9.12) |
| Thief of the wallet holding a did:btco author DID’s sat | Take permanent control of the author DID, which the author cannot recover; add their own Originals keys and sign as the author; deactivate the author DID, making every unanchored Original non-current (P6.5) | Change an anchored Original without its sat’s holder (P9.12); change the author DID named by any Original (P5.8) |
| Host of a did:vh or did:webvh author DID, after anchoring | Withhold the author DID log, leaving anchored Originals with incomplete identity evidence | Affect the author binding of Originals whose author DID is a did:btco (P5.13) |
| Copier who inscribes the same evidence on another sat | Hold an inscription of it | Become the owner, or change the Original (P9.11) |
| Author after selling an anchored Original | Sign changes, which take effect only if the owner inscribes them; rotate keys or deactivate their DID, which refuses later inscriptions only; publish a did:vh or did:webvh author DID update with a backdated versionTime, no earlier than their previous DID update, that removes the key and so rejects entries inscribed since, unless the author DID’s witnesses or watchers refute that time; a verifier that accepted those entries earlier reports a retroactive revocation conflict; anchor a competing branch of an Original on another sat and sell it to a buyer whose verifier does not know the first anchor, which that verifier reports as having unestablished uniqueness and provisional ownership (P9.16) | Change or deactivate the Original alone; change the owner or move it to another sat (P9.10, P9.12); make the Original non-current by a timely rotation or deactivation (P9.15) |
| Bitcoin reorganization | Undo an observed publication | Advance the durable published boundary (P9.4, P9.5) |

Asset logs do not use witnesses (P9.8). An Original that is not anchored has claimed ordering only: a valid prefix is not proof of current state, a host can show a first-time reader an old prefix or a different branch, and recovery from key compromise is limited (section 5). Anchored Originals get their ordering from inscription order on the sat (P9.13); history before the anchor stays claimed, bounded by the anchor’s block, and the anchor’s uniqueness and owner are reported as not established and provisional (P9.16). The first valid anchor wins, so authors who need protection against a stolen key should anchor early. The freshness policy verifiers apply to author DID state remains open (D3).

Permanent history cannot be redacted (P8.3). A key or DID used across Originals links them publicly.
