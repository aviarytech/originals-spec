## Security considerations

This section collects the threats discussed throughout the draft.

| Adversary | Can | Cannot, under the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Malicious host or mirror | Withhold bytes or evidence, serve an old valid prefix of the asset log or of a signer’s DID log, show different branches of an unanchored Original to different readers; a first-time reader cannot tell, and retained versionIds protect only repeat readers (P7.6, P7.8) | Alter bytes or entries without failing digest or proof checks (P2.3, P4.3); hide a fork of an anchored Original from a reader that reads the anchoring sat (P9.13); change which entries of an anchored Original are accepted by withholding or forking a signer’s DID log, because inscribed entries are checked only against signer history that Bitcoin evidences (P9.15, P9.33) |
| Every holder of an anchored Original’s uninscribed resource versions, if all withhold them | Make that content unavailable (P9.18) | Stop the log or its signers’ identities verifying from Bitcoin, or substitute other history (P9.17, P9.33) |
| Network attacker | Observe fetches; block or delay them | Forge entries or bytes; steer resolvers at private addresses (P8.5) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, before the author removes it | Edit or deactivate any Original the key controls that is not anchored, or whose sat the thief also holds; a deactivation is terminal on its branch, and permanent once inscribed (P6.5); create a branch of an unanchored Original, which leaves it an unresolved conflict with no current branch until anchoring (P6.6); anchor an unanchored Original on a sat the thief holds, which, if inscribed first, is the anchor with the thief as its holder, and the author’s later anchor is a conflicting one (P9.16) | Move control away from the author DID (P5.9) |
| Holder of a stolen author key, after the author removes it | Sign entries on unanchored Originals naming an older author DID version, reported as ordering unproven and never current (P7.9); for a did:vh or did:webvh author, if the thief also holds the anchoring sat, keep inscribing entries signed with the key, because the removal binds an anchored Original only once inscribed on its sat (P9.33) | Have such an entry reported as current, or accepted as an anchoring entry or a later inscription after Bitcoin evidences the removal: on the author DID’s own sat for did:btco (P5.13), or inscribed on the anchoring sat for did:vh or did:webvh (P9.15, P9.33) |
| Holder of a stolen editor key, before the editor removes it | Change the content the grant covers; fork an unanchored Original | Change grants, aliases, lifecycle or parameters, anchor or deactivate (P5.16) |
| Thief of the wallet holding a did:btco editor DID’s sat | Take permanent control of that editor identity and change the content its grants cover until the author revokes them (P5.17) | Act as the author or change any grant (P5.16) |
| Editor | Change the content its grant allows without the author; publish a competing entry from an earlier head, forking an unanchored Original until the author anchors it (P6.6) | Widen its grant, add or change aliases, anchor, change lifecycle or parameters, deactivate, or change the author DID or its keys (P5.16, P5.18) |
| Editor after revocation, or holder of a revoked editor’s key | Fork an unanchored Original from an entry before the revocation, which leaves it unresolved until the author anchors it | Have an entry accepted whose parent’s state no longer grants it (P5.17) |
| Shared host | Refuse or delay submissions; publish a refused candidate it was sent, which forks an unanchored Original (P6.8) | Change, rebase or re-sign a signed entry without failing verification; accept two entries with the same parent without a reader that sees both reporting a fork (P6.6, P6.7) |
| Someone claiming a moved author or editor DID | Publish a DID document whose `alsoKnownAs` lists the identity DID, or redirect its old location | Make their keys sign for that identity without a move in the identity DID’s own log (P5.14) |
| Malicious archive or package | Exhaust verifier resources; attempt path traversal; roll back to an older prefix | Have an over-limit history reported as complete or current, or replace newer local history (P7.5, P7.7) |
| Malicious host application, or other software holding the key | Ask the signer to sign an unintended change; if the author’s assertionMethod key is also used outside Originals, get a signer for that other use to sign an Originals entry, which verifiers accept because they cannot tell the key’s uses apart (P5.3, P5.11) | Obtain a signature from a conforming Originals signer for a change it did not show the author (P5.11) |
| Malicious content author | Publish signed active content | Run script on the platform origin or in a client (P9.7) |
| Signer who names a preview | Inscribe a preview that does not depict the version, or, for a version that is not an image, active content as its preview, which Bitcoin explorers may render (P9.7, P9.18) | Have the preview verified as the version, or as evidence of the version’s bytes, which are checked only against the descriptor (P4.3, P9.30); name a preview in an entry that is not prepared for Bitcoin (P9.30) |
| Holder of the anchoring sat | Sell or give away the Original by moving the sat; refuse to inscribe the author’s or editors’ changes, or a signer’s DID history that removes a key, freezing the Original or keeping that key in force for it; inscribe changes the author or a granted editor signed; inscribe a did:btco DID document on the sat, which a generic did:btco resolver returns but Originals verifiers ignore (P3.5, P3.8, P9.31); with a granted editor, keep changing the content the grant covers while refusing to inscribe the author’s revocation (P5.17, P9.12) | Author, rotate or deactivate on their own (P9.12); get an entry accepted that names a signer version not inscribed on the sat, or not on a did:btco signer’s own sat, before it (P9.33) |
| Holder of a signed but uninscribed entry, such as the holder of the anchoring sat or anyone the signer gave it to | Inscribe it on the anchoring sat at any time, if it still extends the accepted head and its signer’s key still meets P5.3 in the state Bitcoin evidences at that inscription: a signed entry never expires, so a change the signer later decided against can still be published. Authors and editors SHOULD NOT sign an entry they do not intend to see inscribed; they can void one only by getting another entry from the same head inscribed first, or by a key removal that Bitcoin evidences before it (P9.13, P9.15) | Inscribe it once another publication has extended that head (P9.13) |
| Index of Originals inscriptions | Omit a competing anchor, so a verifier that trusts it reports uniqueness as established through a block when it is not (P9.16) | Change which publication is the anchor, or what is accepted, for a verifier that reads the inscriptions itself (P9.16, P9.31) |
| Thief of the holder’s wallet | Become the holder by moving the sat; freeze the Original by refusing to inscribe | Sign any change (P9.12) |
| Thief of the wallet holding a did:btco author DID’s sat | Take permanent control of the author DID, which the author cannot recover; add their own assertionMethod keys and sign as the author; deactivate the author DID, making every unanchored Original non-current (P6.5) | Change an anchored Original without its sat’s holder (P9.12); change the author DID named by any Original (P5.8) |
| Host of a did:vh or did:webvh author or editor DID | Withhold the DID log, leaving Originals that are not anchored with incomplete identity evidence for readers with no copy; withhold newer versions, so a reader of an unanchored Original that does not know of a key removal does not see it (P7.8) | Affect which entries of an anchored Original are accepted, which depends only on the signer history inscribed on its sat (P9.15, P9.33); affect the author binding of Originals whose author DID is a did:btco (P5.13) |
| Copier who inscribes the same evidence on another sat | Hold an inscription of it | Become the Original’s holder, or change the Original (P9.11) |
| Author after selling an anchored Original | Sign changes, which take effect only if the holder inscribes them; rotate keys or deactivate their DID, which refuses later inscriptions once Bitcoin evidences it: on the DID’s own sat for a did:btco, or, for a did:vh or did:webvh, once the holder inscribes that version on the anchoring sat (P9.15, P9.33); anchor a competing branch of the Original on another sat and sell it to a buyer whose verifier uses no index of Originals inscriptions, which reports its uniqueness as not established and its holder as provisional (P9.16) | Change or deactivate the Original alone; change the holder or move it to another sat (P9.10, P9.12); void accepted inscriptions or the anchor with a DID version published off Bitcoin, whatever its versionTime (P9.15); inscribe now an entry that only a signer version published after the sale would make valid (P9.33); anchor a second Original on the same sat (P9.34); hide the competing anchor from a verifier that uses an index of Originals inscriptions through its block (P9.16) |
| Distributor of a carried file | Strip the carrier; attach an older valid prefix, or the log of another Original that has a version with the same bytes; append a carrier to bytes it does not match | Have mismatched bytes verified or the file presented as a version of that Original, have a forged log verified once the signers’ DIDs are resolved, have an older prefix reported as current, or have a carried log preferred over the Original’s anchor (P7.12–P7.14) |
| Mail or malware scanner | Flag or strip a file whose trailer follows the format’s own end, a pattern also used to smuggle data | Change what a reader verifies; a stripped carrier leaves the version’s bytes (P14.15) |
| Author of a carried file’s log | Name DIDs on a host they control and learn the address and time of each reader that resolves them | Make a reader resolve without the caller’s opt-in (P7.13, P8.5) |
| Bitcoin reorganization | Undo a publication accepted at one confirmation, or reorder competing anchors, changing the head, anchor or holder, which is reported (P9.5, P9.16) | Advance the durable published boundary (P9.4, P9.5); invalidate a chain of publications re-mined into one block, which chain order still orders (P9.13) |

Asset logs do not use witnesses (P9.8). An Original that is not anchored has claimed ordering only and is never reported as current (P7.10): a valid prefix is not proof of current state, a host can show a first-time reader an old prefix or a different branch, and recovery from key compromise is limited (section 5). Anchored Originals get their ordering from chain order on the sat (P9.13); history before the anchor stays claimed, bounded by the anchor’s block, and the anchor’s uniqueness and holder are reported as not established and provisional unless an index of Originals inscriptions shows no competing anchor through a given block (P9.16). The anchoring sat carries the complete log, the DID histories of its did:vh and did:webvh signers, and the preview, or the full bytes, of each version inscribed on it (P9.17, P9.30, P9.33); versions not inscribed in full, by default including the current one, survive only where someone keeps them, and missing bytes leave the log verified (P9.18). The first valid anchor wins, so authors who need protection against a stolen key should anchor early. Off Bitcoin no freshness policy makes an Original current; it is reported only as valid as of a checkpoint (P7.10).

Permanent history cannot be redacted (P8.3). A key or DID used across Originals links them publicly.
